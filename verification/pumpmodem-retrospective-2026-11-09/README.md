# pumpModem CPU estimation retrospective and Luna exercises

Additional parallel Luna review found a useful architectural issue, but it also repeated known observations, followed an incorrect call path and missed real defects. The strongest lesson is to give independent reviewers different, verified parts of the computational path and make their claims executable. More review of the same narrow excerpt was much less useful.

The newer work in **Speed up Robust CPU throughput estimates** exposes a potentially more serious oversight: repeated waveform preparation and unnecessary synchronization may dominate the remaining workload. This report includes that continuation separately from the earlier blind exercises. Findings from an ongoing implementation are distinguished from measured accepted gains.

## Scope and method

The original request concerned complete receiver CPU capacity, productive use of all 12 logical threads within one received stream, and rapid GPU-assisted forecasting. It did not ask merely for a faster isolated kernel or several simultaneous simulations. The reviewed earlier work ran from approximately 01:28 to 07:29 on November 9, 2026, America/Chicago. That is elapsed conversation time, including waits and user follow-ups, not six hours of proven wasted work. The preceding 5.5 hours was described by the original user and is not counted again.

The first historical capture ended at transcript physical line 6850. During this study the target chat resumed at 12:26; the user then explicitly asked this review to include its latest findings. The first exercise cohort remains frozen and is not credited with knowledge of that continuation. Source at the original start was immutable commit `28f08825670b76472628b50d98cd648a87aa62e4`; the pre-tensor packet used `15b17f7d3886f1baadf3c22b8990083eda6f85cd`. Later source probes use pre-restructuring commit `247b7202520d32e4b239fdaa770fbbb8a42d210e`, not the concurrently edited checkout. [Scope and original-prefix hash](results/history-scope.json), [packet hashes](packets/freeze.json), [later source capture](results/latest-source-freeze.json).

The study used eighteen fresh Luna-high packet reviews: six decision points, each with a general reviewer, an architecture reviewer and an executable-check reviewer. Two additional source searches examined a much broader frozen baseline. One of those received an explicit caller correction in a separately retained follow-up. Three further independent probes were added for the newer bank, cost and dependency findings. Small local checks exercised actual code; no receiver campaign, GPU allocation or production change was performed by this study.

Participants were instructed to read only their assigned pre-outcome materials, with fresh conversation context. This was instruction-based blinding in a shared filesystem, not OS isolation. Packet selection and excerpts were retrospective. The comparison changes total review effort, prompts and source coverage as well as concurrency; it is not a controlled estimate of the effect of parallel scheduling. [Protocol](protocol.md), [source packet generator](build_packets.py), [independent screening](results/screening.md).

The small remainder is the explicitly named fixed-control component. It would be wrong to infer that all controller overhead is small: Python rendering, projection and joins are also charged inside fine processing and other stages. The cost review made that overgeneralization; Astra rejected it. The correct conclusion is regime-specific prioritization: fine work matters greatly for rows 1–3, while bank work is at least as important for rows 5 and 7.

The new source-only bank reviewer independently identified serial native requests, narrow per-lane FFT batching, redundant casts and temporary arrays. It **did not** establish the reported sixfold waveform revisit. It correctly noted that a native `PatternSource` cache already exists. The historical/source check resolves the distinction: rows 5 and 7 use 13 epochs × 2 bits × 3 clocks × 2 rays. Each epoch-bit lane therefore participates in six native sample/projection passes, while the source object and some keyed chip tiles are reused. This structure was already present at the original `28f08825` baseline. There was no direct provider-call counter proving six fresh generator constructions. Four saved bank measurements show about 0.989–1.004 busy-core equivalents including native-child CPU, despite a 12-worker allocation. The one-core bottleneck and large service share are measured; the six-way work expansion is source-derived. [Latest evidence map](results/latest-findings.md), [bank review and its 16 passing mock tests](results/new-bank-blind.md).

The new dependency reviewer identified limited task width and repeated barriers, but was too restrictive about the necessary boundary. A state trajectory must process its own blocks in order; that does not require every independent candidate to rendezvous after every block. Within a fixed-state segment, candidate tiles can potentially own rendering, projection, statistics and their ordered updates, then meet at genuine global timing/pruning decisions. Astra identified that distinction by inspecting the actual state and segment code. Thread-safe source/cache ownership, deterministic merges and bounded memory remain necessary. [Fresh dependency review](results/new-dependencies-blind.md).

The latest chat's **2.4% fine-processing figure needs a narrower label**. It is 1.48248 seconds divided by 61.76420 seconds for one scaled-FP16 row-3 paired driver, including 32.32984 seconds of validation-only CPU reference/checking. It is not the full eight-case GPU trace. Fine work is 5.248% of that pair's 28.24786-second common-component sum, which itself is not a separately measured unpaired trace. Eliminating fine work entirely would cap those unchanged denominators at approximately 1.0246× and 1.0554× respectively. Neither bound applies to the local CPU rows above or a redesigned testing workflow. Nor does it invalidate the earlier **coarse** tensor trials: the historical chat explicitly selected larger coarse Fourier work because fine matrices were too small. [Original timing fields and derived fractions](results/tensor-denominator-evidence.json).

These supplementary probes strengthen the case for parallel **cost, caller and dependency** analysis. They also expose its limits: the cost lens independently found the large bank share, while the bank lens did not establish the sixfold mechanism and the dependency lens retained an unnecessarily strong barrier assumption. Expert arbitration and small executable discriminators remain necessary.

## Astra arbitration and a better approach

Astra independently checked the original user wording, decisive transcript evidence, relevant callers and existing transform alternatives. Its initial arbitration judged the host-heavy GPU plan a strategic mismatch; constructor and serialization problems were cheap integration misses; mixed precision and tensor trials were reasonable experiments; and the comparator was a sound negative control. [Full arbitration](results/astra-arbitration.md).

After incorporating the newer findings and correcting their denominators and dependency claims, Astra's mandatory [completion review](results/completion-review.md) judged this retrospective ready to deliver. That verdict covers this report; receiver restructuring and performance qualification remain separate work. [Artifact checks](results/artifact-validation.json) confirm packet hashes, the original transcript prefix, local links and parseable research files.

The practical workflow is:

1. Map the actual executable to complete receiver phases and their recurring frequency. Rank total CPU service and full cloud turnaround separately before choosing an optimization target.
2. Check which computed results are actually used: repeated native lane generation, shared input projections, requested Fourier bins, known-zero hypotheses and unnecessary reconstruction. Preserve required work and state semantics.
3. Give three Luna branches distinct questions: actual caller/interface contracts; computational complexity and device placement; cheapest executable falsifier. Require source references and a checkable prediction. Screen unsupported findings before expensive expert review.
4. Keep one owner for mutable caches and ordered global state. Hand workers immutable prepared inputs and sufficiently large independent candidate tasks. Synchronize where pruning/merging really requires it, not automatically after every small operation.
5. Run inexpensive import, constructor, representation-roundtrip, geometry/memory and numerical checks before full measurements. Use an actual-shape integrated comparison before retaining a tensor implementation.
6. Freeze measurement-relevant source; invalidate and refresh only affected phase evidence. Seal a prediction before a fresh holdout, retain failures and keep timing accuracy separate from real-time feasibility.

This combines source review with executable evidence. It does not prescribe many agents on every change or make low utilization alone proof of a defect. Numerical constraints, dependency boundaries and practical throughput govern what can usefully run in parallel.

The primary technical references support this approach but do not benchmark pumpModem: [NVIDIA CUDA Best Practices](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-c-best-practices-guide/index.html) explains transfer and synchronization costs; [NVIDIA matrix multiplication guidance](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/performance/dl-performance-matrix-multiplication/index.html) explains shape-dependent efficiency; [SciPy ZoomFFT](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.ZoomFFT.html) and [FFTW pruned transforms](https://www.fftw.org/pruned.html) show alternatives and their limitations. Source-specific applicability is recorded in the [research note](results/primary-research.md) and Astra's arbitration.

## Limits and preservation

There is no measured counterfactual showing how many historical hours, tokens or dollars would have been saved. Historical work overlapped; short repair intervals are not total wasted-effort estimates. The study does not establish a tenfold useful-Luna increase or half of weekly usage cost. The user-provided weekly planning index is unitless—Luna 0.1, Terra 1, Sol approximately 10, Astra approximately 20—and is neither official pricing nor a conversion from these token counts into subscription allowance.

[Numeric session telemetry](results/telemetry.json) records local runtime model/effort metadata and the latest cumulative usage per session, with cached input retained as a subset. It includes coordination and screening rather than just the successful reviews. The main chat and any active continuations are partial at capture. These records are not provider billing or measured subscription allowance. Two initial Luna dispatches needed omitted authorization context supplied before proceeding; results written to two unintended study-relative locations were moved without changing their contents. Such administrative friction is included in the study, not evidence of receiver performance.

Only this local study's files were added to the delegation-kit checkout. The active pumpModem checkout, installed kit, model pins and global configuration were not changed by this study. Other work in the target chat continues independently. No commit, push, publication or new cloud expenditure was performed.
