## C1 general review

### First work to optimize

Do not choose a kernel from these excerpts alone: no profile identifies the dominant cost. First capture a matched, end-to-end single-stream baseline for the complete receiver workload, including acquisition, fine tracking, unsuccessful candidates, recurring setup, and quiet-slot completion. Record wall time and CPU time by stage, rows/candidates/taps and valid rows, plus executor queue delay and per-job run time. Keep the input, precision, work coverage, and output checks fixed while varying worker counts (1, 2, 4, 8, 12). Then optimize the stage whose measured share limits total throughput; verify results and coverage after each change. This separates algorithmic work from scheduling: large serial stage time points to algorithmic or controller work; queue delay with idle workers points to dispatch/serialization; workers busy with little throughput gain points to insufficient task parallelism or contention.

### Concerns to check

1. **Potentially duplicated per-block preprocessing across the two tracking lanes (hypothesis, not established waste).** `PracticalTrackingReceiver.push` calls `nominal.push` and then `adaptive.push` serially ([triad_tracking_receiver.py:714–717](../..)). If the lanes independently repeat basis construction, projection, or Gram calculation for identical inputs/configuration, this is both a large serial cost and a candidate for safely shared immutable preprocessing. If configurations differ, sharing may be invalid. **Observable:** stage timings show comparable preprocessing twice per input block. **Cheapest check:** instrument the two lanes' preprocessing counts and times on one matched complete run; compare inputs/configuration before proposing reuse. **Action:** share only results proven identical; otherwise optimize each measured hot path.

2. **The executor may not expose enough parallel work, or its work may be too small.** `_score_block` builds jobs, submits them in a loop, then waits for every future ([triad_incremental_receiver.py:301–326]). Parallel work appears limited to independent runtime updates/jobs; basis projection and Gram construction happen before submission ([274–289]), and job creation/submission is controlled serially. **Observable:** either substantial pre-submit/controller time or idle workers/queue waits, or workers remain busy with little scaling. **Cheapest check:** timestamp job creation, submit, worker start/end and completion for the same block at 1/12 workers. **Action:** if pre-submit work dominates, optimize or parallelize that stage; if queueing dominates, reduce dispatch overhead; if workers are underfilled, add only demonstrated independent work; if saturated without gains, investigate task granularity/contention rather than increasing workers blindly.

3. **DFT projection rejects partially erased blocks.** `_score_block` raises when `projection_mode == 'dft'` and `valid` is neither all true nor all false ([triad_incremental_receiver.py:243–246]); the comment says a covariance adapter is required. This may be an intentional statistical constraint, but if partial erasures occur in the target workload it prevents complete-work estimates. **Observable:** representative input raises before scoring the block. **Cheapest check:** run the matched workload and count partial-valid DFT blocks. **Action:** if present, preserve correctness and implement/qualify the required covariance handling before throughput comparisons; otherwise retain the fast path.

### Choice to preserve

Keep the two lanes' independent channel states and mix their results only after updates. The code also shares immutable observation arrays and computes global timing once before lane updates ([triad_incremental_receiver.py:305–310]; [triad_tracking_receiver.py:726–745]). This preserves the distinct models and avoids conflating parallel simulations with capacity in one stream.
