1. **Low-precision transform error can change candidate selection.** In `tools/triad_gpu_bank.py:295-300` and `:332-338`, transformed correlations feed peak extraction; a small error could reorder close peaks or move a selected lag/frequency. This is a hypothesis, not an observed defect. **Check:** on identical receiver inputs and actual shapes, compare baseline and tensor variants' correlations, peak ordering, acquisition outcomes, and margins, including unsuccessful candidates. **Action:** retain only a mode that preserves required accuracy and work coverage with credible margins; otherwise use the existing precision/path.

2. **A faster matrix kernel may not improve complete same-stream throughput.** The bank path batches products/transforms and retains results before yielding (`:287-304`); tensor packing, launches, or memory use may erase a kernel-level gain. Independent simulations do not answer the stated 12-thread, one-stream requirement. **Check:** time the complete receiver workload on the local CPU with one stream and all 12 logical threads, including acquisition, tracking, unsuccessful candidates, recurring setup, and quiet-slot completion; compare matched baseline and candidate runs. **Action:** keep the change only if complete-workload throughput and margins meet the user's goal.

3. **Do not replace the fine-path covariance with reduced precision.** The source explicitly promotes the projected covariance input to complex128 and explains that complex64 can leave an imaginary diagonal or negative roundoff eigenvalues (`tools/triad_gpu_fine.py:170-179`). **Check:** if proposing any covariance change, compare the strict PSD guard on representative and near-singular inputs against the existing FP64 result. **Action:** preserve FP64 unless that guard and downstream accuracy are demonstrated; the first projection prototype can leave this calculation untouched.
