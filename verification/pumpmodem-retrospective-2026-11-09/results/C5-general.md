# C5 general review

## Concrete concerns

1. **FP32 structural validity is not an accuracy criterion for the receiver decision.** `triad_gaussian_state.py:96-110` checks Gram Hermitian/PSD behavior with a default complex64 tolerance of `2e-5`, then repairs small negative Gram eigenvalues. A Gram can pass those checks while its small eigenvalues or correlations have shifted enough to change an accumulated likelihood or the ranking/margin of close candidates. That is especially relevant when blocks accumulate and decisions depend on small likelihood differences. **Predicted outcome:** no validation error, but FP32 and FP64 produce different candidate ordering, likelihood margin, or selected result. **Cheapest decisive check:** on one identical representative complete-stream input, run the proposed FP32 front end and the current/reference FP64 path; compare per-block `r` and Gram plus final likelihoods, rankings, and margins, including the closest candidate decision. Include acquisition, fine tracking, unsuccessful candidates, setup, and quiet-slot completion. Set acceptance limits from the receiver’s decision margin/accuracy contract; the packet does not provide a numeric limit. If any decision or required margin changes, retain FP64 for the sensitive stage or revise the boundary and repeat the paired check.

2. **FP32 Gram formation can be sensitive to nearly dependent projected tap vectors.** The source forms Gram entries as inner products in `triad_incremental_receiver.py:281-282`, while the state uses the smallest Gram eigenvalue to validate/repair at `triad_gaussian_state.py:101-109`. Rounding can overwhelm a small eigenvalue even without high in-band dynamic range; the present excerpts do not establish a lower bound on Gram conditioning. **Predicted outcome:** an otherwise finite, valid block is rejected as non-PSD or receives a roundoff repair large enough to perturb the likelihood. **Cheapest decisive check:** among actual representative blocks, record the smallest eigenvalue relative to Gram scale and the FP32-vs-FP64 eigenvalue/likelihood deltas; also test one constructed near-dependent tap pair as a boundary probe. If observed conditioning approaches the FP32 error floor or causes material output drift, compute Gram in FP64 (or use a qualified higher-precision path) while keeping the rest of the proposed boundary.

## Choice worth preserving

Keeping cumulative likelihood accumulation in FP64 is a sound separation: many small block contributions can accumulate even when individual front-end arrays are well behaved. The source explicitly keeps the cumulative log likelihood FP64 (`triad_gaussian_state.py:65-66`). Preserve that property and judge the lower-precision front end against complete-stream outputs, not just per-block finiteness or Gram structure.
