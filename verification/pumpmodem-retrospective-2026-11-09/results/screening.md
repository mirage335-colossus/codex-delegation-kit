## Comparison and limits

Across five cases, the generalist alone gives source-supported mechanism-level leads for C1 serial pre-update preparation and C5 Gram precision, useful gates for C2 and C4, and correctly preserves C3 independent booleans and conjunctive acceptance. The union adds C2's specific host-heavy CPU/GPU boundary (architecture concern 2; falsifier concern 1), and adds distinct, conditional discriminators in C5 (falsifier concern 1). C1's architecture and falsifier do not establish a further historical mechanism beyond the generalist's preparation lead. C4 adds an algorithm-replacement/actual-shape check, not an additional established failure. C3 is a negative control: the later inconsistent test expectation was unavailable and need not be predicted; shared empty-case concern is constrained by the omitted guard, and proposed score-shape mismatch by normal record validation. Later single-bin FFT redundancy, integrated subclass constructor guard and workspace cap are not identified in these exercise answers; their absence does not prove detectability from the packets. The transcript reports the latter two at line 1564, outside the specific questions represented here.

All 15 responses are source-only reasoning from bounded excerpts, with no runtime checks or measured performance; reviewers used file tools to read packets and write responses. The additional reviews use both extra reviewer work and different prompts, so this comparison cannot isolate parallel scheduling, reliability, cost, or speed. No claim of historical causality follows from mere temporal ordering. No unconditional false defect assertion was found in these responses; several concerns explicitly say the hypothesis is unverified. Omitted upstream guards and later test changes limit claims about reachability and what could be discovered from the packets.
