# C4 architecture review

1. **Start with the lag ZoomFFT path (lines 321–334), but first confirm it dominates a complete same-stream run.** Each lag batch forms `mixed` and calls the plan transform across `kernel_rows`; this is the clearest matrix-shaped candidate for a tensor-compatible Fourier projection. A direct matrix formulation could reuse a frequency-by-kernel coefficient matrix over lag windows, but extra materialization, layout conversion, or rebuilding coefficients could erase its gain. Shapes and plan reuse costs are absent, so dominance and benefit are hypotheses. **Predicted outcome:** a microbenchmark may improve while acquisition-to-quiet-slot throughput does not, or peak memory may force narrower batches. **Cheapest decisive check:** instrument this path's time, allocations, and call counts in one complete representative run; then compare a small matched transform wrapper at observed shapes, including coefficient setup. Prototype only if the path is material and the wrapper wins with safe memory headroom.

2. **Treat reduced precision as a candidate-decision risk, not just a numeric-difference check.** Coarse search ranks peaks after batched correlations (lines 295–300); fine statistics also feed correlations and covariance into a strict FP64 PSD guard (lines 169–179). TF32/scaled FP16 error could change peak ordering, candidate acceptance, or covariance validity near boundaries even if average errors look small. No tensor speed or error evidence is provided. **Predicted outcome:** marginal candidates may reorder or the guard may reject a block; the effect on full work and margins is unknown. **Cheapest decisive check:** on identical recorded observations, compare full-precision and candidate outputs plus candidate identities, unsuccessful-candidate coverage, and guard outcomes, emphasizing near-threshold cases. Retain reduced precision only if decisions and coverage meet the existing accuracy contract and end-to-end margins remain credible.

3. **Optimize the required one-stream workload, not an artificial parallel-simulation throughput number.** The objective explicitly counts acquisition, fine tracking, unsuccessful candidates, recurring setup, and quiet-slot completion on the local CPU with 12 logical threads; independent simultaneous simulations cannot establish that capacity. A fast isolated tensor kernel omits scheduling/setup and CPU/GPU overlap costs. **Predicted outcome:** per-kernel speedup may fail to improve completed receiver work per quiet slot, or can reduce margins due to setup/transfer overhead. **Cheapest decisive check:** compare repeatable baseline and prototype runs on the same receiver stream, hardware, thread setting, and workload boundaries, reporting complete work and margins. Use that result, rather than aggregate independent streams, for the retain decision.
