For practical numerical software, begin with the lowest arithmetic precision that plausibly serves the application on the target hardware. Optimize useful end-to-end work within the available computational ceiling. Higher precision must earn its cost through an observed application-level benefit; use it selectively where that benefit occurs. Make the default automatic and hardware-aware so users do not have to tune the precision of individual processing stages. Preserve explicit correctness, resource and authorization requirements. Compare complete algorithms and costs, not datatype names or isolated arithmetic peaks.
