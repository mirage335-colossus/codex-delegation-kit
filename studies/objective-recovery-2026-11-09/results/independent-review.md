## Common decision mechanism and actual benefit

All six preserve the negative qualification rather than falsely passing it, distinguish resident/cold scopes and source checkpoints, and avoid unauthorized receiver/cloud execution. These are meaningful strengths. None explicitly resolves the central decision question: whether a user who prioritized practical all-thread utilization and cross-machine defaults should discard a parity-preserving, resource-admitted improvement merely because it misses the assistant's 15% threshold. Listing the user's goals elsewhere does not perform that comparison. The values helper also endorses retirement from the same gate, so supplied assistance did not independently correct this assumption.

The gate must remain failed. Separately, missing exact candidate code, native inputs, runner, final source identity, broad workloads and a characterized complexity/resource tradeoff justify withholding production adoption. They do not establish that the measured improvement has no practical value or must be discarded. A supported outcome could preserve the candidate evidence, recover source if available or define a new source-bound test, and make a prospective objective-based decision without rewriting the old result. More CPU time is a genuine cost worth evaluating; busy-core increase alone is not productive utilization proof.

Pipeline opportunity discovery is a different dimension. u763 adds a concrete serial two-lane scheduling hypothesis with appropriate ownership constraints. Other owners identify tracking as the dominant stage and sensible telemetry gaps. Neither discovery proves the wider-controller default should be adopted or rejected, and none demonstrates receiver throughput benefit. The packet is sufficient for these bounded investigations despite being insufficient for production reconstruction.
