# Historical continuation findings

## Decision

Keep the archived disposition: retire the wider-controller candidate and do not adopt it as the default. The independent audit confirms the decisive gate failure. The screened `p3w18` candidate won selection with a 10.05% one-look point gain, but its two ABBA resident ratios were 0.9455 and 0.9772 and the combined resident reduction was only 3.89%, below the predeclared 15% adoption threshold. Cold complete-lifecycle time improved 6.69%, so the cold-regression gate passed. Average busy-core equivalents rose 4.116 to 5.778 of 12; that indicates a utilization increase, not evidence of near-full saturation. The audited screen/qualification had exact within-phase exposed-work, score, and message parity, modeled and sampled memory compliance, no retries/fallbacks, and matching driver/input identities. These support rejecting performance adoption, not rejecting the measured descriptor optimization or changing the 12-thread default.

## Earlier checkpoint and scheduling opportunity

The separate c54 checkpoint is source `c54e24698c69c79805b5aad86205abdd36d2ca7c`, with unchanged source/input during its trace. Its default used 12 aggregate worker permits, two owners, and a `[12,6,6,6,6]` allocation across five windows. The trace shows useful rolling overlap: job 2 starts while earlier jobs are still draining, and ordered commits remain parent-owned. This is evidence that the extant two-owner scheduler can overlap public-window work; callable intervals and owner-thread `thread_time_ns` are not OS CPU-running intervals.

The selected c54 fine-cycle sample is small and explicitly early/fixed rather than a representative warm average. In its two cycles, basis-statistics took about 20–22 ms; dispatch took 6–9 ms; the six basis jobs individually took about 4–8 ms; Gaussian work took about 1 ms; merge took 1–4 ms. This sample does not support spending the next optimization on micro-parallelizing Gaussian updates, and it cannot establish that basis job batching is a net win. Across complete window scoring, fine tracking dominates observed wall intervals (about 3.4–5.8 s), but the trace's selected narrow cycle cannot attribute that full stage or prove CPU saturation. The first practical scheduling action is a source-bound, multi-case CPU profiling/forecast pass on the actual final receiver, instrumenting complete receiver service and per-stage OS CPU time while keeping the existing ordered-commit and public-window semantics. Compare the current two-owner overlapping schedule against a small, predeclared alternative only if profiling shows substantial runnable serial/idle gaps in the complete workload; retain 12 hardware-thread defaults and require complete-work and memory gates.
