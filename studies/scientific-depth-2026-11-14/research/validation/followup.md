# Validation follow-up: interpolation, matched controls, and one bounded pilot

This supplement preserves the original chapter/source ledger and adds a code-informed calculation, a cross-domain measurement/validation matrix, a single bounded pilot plan, and the HF public-reference comparator supplied by the independent HF branch. It is evidence and design only: no modem, receiver, hardware, or full simulation run was performed.

## A. Reproducible native-formula probe

### Source contract inspected

PumpModem HEAD `13db435fc85114be2e2a0298580d5c3e2bcba996`; worktree had unrelated dirty files under prohibited `_appraisal/`, which were not read. The authorized `src/triad_simulation_stream.cpp` defines `Complex = std::complex<double>` and `interpolate(history, first, end, position)` around lines 193–223. An integer `position >= 0` returns the indexed sample directly. A fractional position uses offsets −7 through +8, `sinc(d)` times a 16-sample Blackman window, then divides by the coefficient sum. Samples outside the retained history return zero. `process_one` generates the source through `floor(position)+8` before interpolation (around lines 811–842), so the centered filter uses up to eight source samples of lookahead. Construction uses a zero-initialized fixed ring of `ceil(maximum_delay × Fs × tx_scale)+64` complex samples (around lines 476–489 and 635–667), and `read()` advances a persistent cursor and calls the same stateful `process_one` for each output sample across reads (around lines 1014–1030). The configuration comments say signal gain is applied before the channel, noise variance is absolute complex-sample (E|n|^2), and no normalization uses future whole-stream power (`triad_modem_v3.hpp` around lines 276–281). These are source inspections; not product runtime observations.

### Experiment and observed output

`fractional_delay_probe.py` independently re-expresses only the interpolation equation in Python's standard library; the exact command was:

```text
python3 studies/scientific-depth-2026-11-14/research/validation/fractional_delay_probe.py > studies/scientific-depth-2026-11-14/research/validation/fractional_delay_probe.txt
```

Inputs are synthetic unit complex samples at normalized (F_s=1), fractional delay (	au=8.25) samples, analytic complex tones (x[k]=e^{j2\pi fk}) over six normalized frequencies, an impulse at (k=64), a tone-plus-impulse stream split at chunk sizes 16/31, and a gain-scaling check. No project waveform, package, external data, or helper binary is used. Output:

* At 0.05–0.20 cycles/sample the calculated response stays within −0.0015 to +0.0002 dB and about 0.005° of ideal phase. At 0.35 cycles/sample the response is −0.0159 dB / +0.1013°, and at 0.45 it is −1.6535 dB / +13.7940° (−0.0851 sample inferred-delay error). This locates a finite-kernel edge sensitivity; it is not evidence of nominal modem in-band error because the prepared profile's occupied DATA/Helper/guard band relative to its actual sample rate must be supplied and checked first.
* An integer delay of 8 samples takes the exact-sample branch: one unit impulse output, unit peak and unit output energy. Fractional delays produce a 16-sample finite response: at 3.25 samples an impulse at 64 first appears at 60 (four samples before the input impulse sample), at 8.25 it first appears at 65, and at 12.25 it first appears at 69. Peak is 0.89670 and output energy 0.93553 for each fractional case. The centered interpolator therefore has a finite pre/post response and is not a strictly causal physical impulse response when used as a zero-latency time warp; the first nonzero output depends on delay in samples. It also preserves coefficient sum/DC response rather than impulse energy.
* With a discontinuous complex tone turned on at sample 0 and zero prehistory, the fractional 8.25-sample output is nonzero in 8 nominal pre-arrival samples; maximum magnitude among those is 0.7971. This is the finite band-limited interpolation kernel responding to an abrupt/non-bandlimited boundary. It is not a physical radio precursor and not itself an implementation defect. A real waveform's ramp, occupied band, capture lead, propagation delay and explicit channel tail determine how to interpret the transient.
* A deliberately broken counterfactual that drops history/lookahead at every 16- or 31-sample chunk boundary differs from continuous interpolation on 144/160 or 85/160 samples, max absolute errors 1.5072 or 1.0909. It proves that such a reset would alter a fractional filter; it does **not** show that `StreamingChannel::read()` resets state. Source inspection shows it retains a stream cursor and ring history.
* Scaling the source by 0.5 before interpolation gives output RMS exactly 0.5× the unit-gain stream. Re-normalizing each output back to unit RMS would erase that actual level change. The source config comment explicitly states the simulated stream is not normalized by future whole power; `signal_gain` is upstream and noise variance is a separate absolute quantity.

Interpretation and stop condition: this check validates the mathematical consequences of the inspected 16-tap formula, not compilation, simulator behavior, receiver correctness, bandwidth qualification, or physical propagation. It does not cover the frontend filters, ADC, noise, oscillator, dynamically moving delay, chunk production scheduling, channel combination, or complete source/receiver pipeline. The owner's prepared receiver profile and exact occupied band are still required to decide whether the 0.35/0.45 normalized-frequency edge is relevant. If actual profile tones and the product's configured source ramp/tail lie well inside the checked flat region and far from reset/capture edges, the issue should not alter the channel-model decision. If not, run a narrow actual-helper comparison at that profile only after authorization.

### Equal-power noise counterexample

The accompanying `noise_power_counterexample.py` gives a second small analytical check of the independent ITU P.372 finding that mean power alone can fail to characterize non-Gaussian noise. With unit mean complex sample power, circular complex Gaussian white noise has (P(|n|\ge4)=e^{-16}=1.12535\times10^{-7}). A different i.i.d. zero-mean mixture with independent uniform phase, amplitude 10 with probability 0.01 and zero otherwise, has the same (E|n|^2=1), zero nonzero-lag covariance (and hence the same flat second-order PSD), but a 1% exceedance probability—88,861× higher. In 10,000 samples, probability of any such exceedance is 0.1125% versus effectively 100%. `noise_power_counterexample.txt` preserves the actual output. This is a mathematical counterexample, not a measured radio-noise distribution or receiver-run failure. It supports the calibration/control requirement to record both power/PSD and an amplitude exceedance/APD statistic or raw waveform when impulsiveness matters.

Use independent *path/session/event* holdouts. Do not randomly split time-adjacent windows. Cases with no traceable reference remain synthetic or relative-level evidence.

## C. One bounded pilot capture/comparison

Purpose: reject large calibration, normalization, false-quiet and convention artifacts before any expensive channel campaign. This is a **screening/calibration pilot**, not a reliability qualification and not an environmental distribution estimate. It is run only when the required domain-specific fixture/capture access and a prepared receiver profile are available.

### Four records per domain (20 records total)

For each of VLF, MF groundwave, 30 MHz groundwave, HF skywave and EME, capture four immutable streams at the actual prepared profile's permitted sample rate and full complex I/Q bandwidth:

1. **Null/control:** no DATA source plus measured background/validity; separately record a validity-mask counterfactual without changing samples. This is a negative control, not a claim of physically absent external RF.
2. **Calibration fit:** full known source waveform at the chosen reference plane with a calibrated frequency sweep across the actual occupied band at two source levels (to check linearity), sufficient to fit gain and phase without hiding frequency-selective behavior.
3. **Independent check:** known waveform at a withheld frequency/level/phase or polarization, with an independent instrument measurement; do not use it to refit.
4. **Target:** one measured target path/event at the same predeclared modem calendar, with truth sidecar, timed DATA and valid quiet/tail. Use the same recorded target samples for candidate and baseline.

Bound each *active source interval* to VLF 60 s, MF groundwave 10 s, 30 MHz groundwave 10 s, HF skywave 60 s, and EME 15 s per record. These are pilot screening windows, not scientifically sufficient event durations. Here `source_samples` is the complete prepared waveform length, including its actual raised/tapered ramps and public-calendar quiet/wait intervals; the active interval does not authorize dropping those samples. Compute the complete capture span from the actual prepared waveform/channel/profile before capture. The native `StreamingChannel` source computes `tail_samples = ceil(delay_tail_real) + perturb_tail + tx_interpolation_tail + frontend_tail`, where `delay_tail_real = (spread_delay_ms/1000 + delay_excursion_seconds) × Fs × rx_scale`, `tx_interpolation_tail = ceil(8 × rx_scale/tx_scale)+2`, `perturb_tail = max(actual selective response tail, ceil(selective_tail_floor_seconds × Fs × rx_scale))`, and `frontend_tail` is the configured lowpass decay to its 1e-6 criterion (zero when disabled). `dt_rx = 1/(Fs × rx_scale)`. Its natural total is `N_natural = ceil(capture_lead × Fs_rx + (max(0, source_samples/(Fs × tx_scale) + timing_offset) + capture_trail) × Fs_rx + tail_samples)`, where `Fs_rx=Fs × rx_scale`. The stream then sets `N_total = max(N_natural, minimum_capture_samples)`; the minimum is an endpoint, not additional tail. In the live source, `minimum_capture_samples` is set to `calendar.slot(end_slot).observation.stop`, where `end_slot` is chosen to include the two scored quiet slots. Therefore use `N_{d,j}=max(N_{natural,d,j},N_{public-stop,d,j})` and **do not add two quiet slots again** when the public stop endpoint already contains them. For a record without a public calendar, supply one explicit stop-sample endpoint that includes two scored quiet slots. For actual measured signals, retain the observed longer endpoint if it exceeds both. Do not use the `+64` ring-history slots as an output-tail estimate: those are bounded source memory. The 1/2/3 s tails in the first arithmetic draft below are therefore underaccounted, superseded values, not capture instructions.

For each record (j) in domain (d), calculate the exact natural stream length and take the maximum with its explicit public calendar stop sample, which already includes required quiet slots when available:

`N_d,j = max(N_natural,d,j, N_public-stop,d,j)`.

This uses the actual prepared sample rate, scales, maximum delay, selective tail/floor, frontend filter tail, lead/trail, timing offset, complete source length/ramp and exact public stop. Do not count quiet slots twice. For one complex-double receive lane plus a one-byte validity mask, exact raw bytes are `17 × N_d,j`. The corrected parametric five-regime sample budget is

\[
N_{all}=\sum_{d\in\{VLF,MF,30GW,HF,EME\}}\sum_{j=1}^{4}N_{d,j}
\]

and raw sample/mask bytes `17 × N_all`, plus bounded metadata. Four records per regime remain 20 records. Because rates and the tail-bearing profile scalars are not supplied, no corrected absolute `N_all`, bytes, or input-seconds total can be asserted yet. At a common illustrative 48,000 complex samples/s, the active intervals alone total 620 seconds, 29,760,000 samples, and 505,920,000 bytes (482.5 MiB) for one lane; this is a strict lower bound before capture lead/trail, source timing extension, channel/filter tails, two quiet slots and any extra observed tail. Two simultaneous lanes double that lower bound. The 48 ksample/s illustration is not the modem's selected or default radio profile. Cap raw samples + all per-record metadata at 1.25 GiB, with a 16-MiB metadata sub-cap; calibration certificates may be stored once by hash. Do not substitute the stand-alone `Format.sample_rate=960` for a prepared Robust profile: the native owner states actual DATA/helper bands and selected profile determine the valid rate, and Helper pilots occupy frequencies far beyond that standalone default. Reject before capture if the actual prepared rate/bandwidth, max delay, profile tail parameters, quiet-slot duration or available storage is unknown or exceeds the cap; do not shorten the tail to fit.

Each raw file is stored once and replayed to baseline and candidate, so there are 40 receiver runs (20 captures × 2 versions); exact per-version input-seconds are the sum of each record's (N_{d,j}/F_{s,rx}), not the 620-second active-only lower bound. Record complete per-version processing cost against each capture's actual input seconds. This pilot is not the demanding-case service benchmark and cannot establish the project's <1 complete processing second per input second criterion; that still needs the full demanding sample/workload and owner-defined condition. This report does not forecast runtime or duplicate the native branch's pipeline-wide `W=2H` workspace/bandwidth budget. For the source-side synthetic interpolation history only, the current channel uses `16 × (ceil(F_s × tau_max × tx_scale)+64)` bytes per complex-double history ring; that `+64` is a memory halo, not output-tail time. Keep source history separate from receiver leased workspace and raw-capture storage. Actual `tau_max`, scale and profile rate must be measured/declared.

Preserved first-draft arithmetic (underaccounted): `followup.md` before the tail review used 652 input-seconds / 507.4 MiB per lane at hypothetical 48 ksample/s by including arbitrary 1/2/3 s tails. That value omitted selective perturbation, interpolation, frontend, configured capture lead/trail, timing extension and two scored quiet slots; it is superseded and must not be used to allocate or truncate captures. The corrected active-only lower bound above and the parametric full-tail formula are the current design.

### Randomization, controls and stop/reject criteria

Randomize candidate/baseline processing order per record; reset receiver state and verify both runs used identical raw-file hash, masks, sample addresses, profile config and source revision. Keep the truth sidecar inaccessible until output commit. Fit only record 2; compare record 3 and record 4 without refitting. For record 4, report per-frame/bit correctness, false admission on record 1, completion after the valid tail, and full CPU/workspace service. For each condition, preserve raw output plus level/delay/noise/clip/clock observations and calibration covariance. Fail or stop the evidence claim if: any calibration certificate does not cover the band/level; reference-plane uncertainty is unknown; sample clock/time uncertainty exceeds the chosen Doppler/timing discriminator; passband misses DATA/Helper plus guard; validity mask and signal are conflated; receiver states/configs differ; raw hash differs between paired versions; target data are reused during tuning; path tail/quiet criteria are not met; any buffer cap is exceeded; or there is no independent target observable. Rejection means “not interpretable/needs input,” not “receiver failed.”

Five target records are one per regime, not five samples from a shared population. No rare-failure bound, field availability, EME link reliability, or transfer across route/day/event may be reported from this one pilot. Later independent-event sizing must start from a prespecified service failure probability; e.g. zero failures in 300 independent trials only bounds one-sided 95% failure probability below about 0.994% under IID assumptions, as shown in `desk_checks.txt`.

## D. Conducted HF comparator and its correct role

The independent HF reference branch used the official ITU-R Study Group 3 HF reference corpus revision `82017594a1c6cacfaa7e86954c4ae7b3a5825a3d`; its `reference_checks.py` / `reference_checks.txt` result is recorded at [research/hf-reference/reference_checks.txt](../hf-reference/reference_checks.txt). The data pairing is D1 Table 1 path/frequency/SSN/year/month metadata with Table 2 measured fields and D1Comp historical predictions; fields are normalized to 1 kW EIRP. Matched keys include ID/year/month, frequency and UTC hour. The exact prediction producer executable is unknown (upstream labels ITURHFProp/REC533), so this is an independent reference-data comparison, not a verified standalone current P.533 run.

For 181 route/frequency IDs and 1,613 unique ID/month keys spanning 1964–1985, 16,268 measured cells were present and 22,444 code-99 cells were missing; missing observations were not treated as zero. Thirteen absolute errors above 50 dB were retained. Across the observed monthly-median pairs, bias was −0.06 dB, SD/RMSE 10.23 dB, and 4,942/16,268 (30.4%) differed by at least 10 dB. Subgroups differed: 2–5 MHz bias −3.04/RMSE 9.78 dB; 15–30 MHz bias +2.62/RMSE 12.21 dB; long-way paths RMSE 12.27 dB. The Oslo–Norddeich matched example for July 1982 UTC 01, R12=115 reported measured/predicted 21/31 dBµV/m at 6.4 MHz, −4/2 at 12.7 MHz, and −21/−8 at 17.1 MHz. Overall near-zero bias therefore hides large spread and frequency/path dependence.

These are monthly median field comparisons, not time samples or service outcomes. Rows sharing route, month, frequency and station are dependent; do not apply an IID binomial/mean confidence interval to 16,268 as if independent environmental episodes. D1 helped calibrate/standardize P.533, so the fitted reference set is not an independent holdout for estimating new-model generalization. Use it to expose units, missing-data denominator rules, subgroup residuals and plausible scale discrepancies; reserve new independently measured routes/events for model-transfer claims. It does not test the modem receiver or characterize short-time high-latitude trajectories.
