## P.1147-4 at 1 MHz

The main result is an annual median of half-hourly median **skywave field strengths**, tied to sunset or sunrise, with a vertical transmitting antenna and a vertically oriented ground-level receiving loop aligned with the great-circle path. The published distance applicability is 50–12,000 km. Ground reflectivity and terminal polarization couple into the observed component. The median and exceedance figures summarize the field population for the specified time relative to sunset or sunrise. They cannot supply a sequence of complex tap phases or reception events. For paths under 2000 km, midpoint sunset and sunrise govern the tested hourly loss. The reference time is six hours after sunset. The long-route recipe selects the more illuminated control point 750 km inward from one terminal and applies segmentwise geomagnetic corrections above 3000 km. [P.1147-4 §§1, 2.1–2.8 and 3](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.1147-4-200708-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf).

The actual checks use synthetic inland endpoints (42°N, 94°W) to (39°N, 91°W) and to (35°N, 88°W), with geographic great-circle distances 419.01 and 936.69 km on the 6371 km sphere. Both ends are taken as average terminal ground and off the sea-gain branch. A centered dipole built from the WMM2025 degree-one coefficients places the midpoint at 49.09° and 47.22° geomagnetic latitude, respectively. Endpoint inclination estimates exceed 62° in each case, leaving a substantial separation from the P.1147 45° polarization-loss switch. This is a documented magnetic approximation for these conditional cases. An installed-antenna or equatorial path requires actual dip/declination, radiation pattern and polarization at both endpoints. The imposed 300 V in the ray direction makes the launch normalization explicit and avoids counting a pattern twice. It is equivalent to P.1147's 1 kW EMRP ray reference, with a separate idealized fixed-monopole sensitivity below. [NOAA WMM2025 technical report Table 2 and §3.5](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/71569/noaa_71569_DS1.pdf), [P.1147-4 §§2.2–2.4](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.1147-4-200708-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf), [BS.561-2](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bs/R-REC-BS.561-2-198607-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf).

For the two routes at a nominal 100 km E-region equivalent mirror, the calculation evaluates the continuous-distance slant formula, the region-2 MF intercept 107 dB, composite ionospheric loss of 5.98/11.56 dB, and the R12=12 solar loss of 0.08/0.09 dB. Appendix 1 gives sunset on 2025-10-15 at approximately 23:30/23:26 UTC and sunrise on 2025-10-16 near 12:22/12:13 UTC. The underlying diurnal correction remains annual. The date anchors clock times, and R12 values 12 and 100 are input scenarios independent of the observed 2025 sunspot record. At R12=12 the fields in dBµV/m are:

The separate daytime evidence in Annex 2 gives an annual MF midday median about 43 dB below the six-hour nighttime reference in its observations. An E-to-F mechanism check is important at the upper MF end. For the 419 km route the flat 100 km mirror gives `cos(i)=0.431`. Thus the condition in Annex 1 predicts penetration when foE is below 0.431 MHz at 1 MHz or below 0.711 MHz at 1.65 MHz, under that ideal geometry. The exercise does not choose an observed foE. At the 419 km night reference, 220 km F-layer sensitivity gives a 43.40 dBµV/m normalized-ray field versus 47.61 dBµV/m at 100 km. Holding the horizon c.m.f. of a short vertical over ideal ground fixed, the elevation factor is `20 log10(cos Δ)`, with `cos Δ=d/p` for this flat calculation. That gives 40.18 versus 46.72 dBµV/m, a 6.54 dB difference. The geometric excess paths correspond to 0.629 versus 0.151 ms relative to the geometric surface distance. The figures exclude dispersive group delay, unknown groundwave phase and actual modal coexistence. They support testing a coherent ground-plus-sky overlap with differential delays near those orders of magnitude, separately from the receiver noise and HF multi-mode tests. At the 937 km comparison, the same conditional geometric calculation produces only a 2.28 dB difference with the fixed monopole, and the penetration thresholds fall to 0.209 and 0.345 MHz. There is no reason to assign F dominance at either route from these sensitivity numbers alone. [P.1147-4 Annex 1 and §§2.2, 2.5](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.1147-4-200708-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf), [calculation output](reference_checks.txt).

To compare a desired modem signal at 1 MHz with a cochannel MF skywave interferer, use the same antenna observation operator, power convention and Rx filter bandwidth for both. For two arrivals of the same signal, aggregate their complex voltage after aligning actual timing, frequency and receive polarization. A deterministic phase sweep gives possible narrowband cancellation even when separate median powers are adequate, and clearing the sky ray provides a matched ground control. For an independent broadcaster retain it as interference with its occupied spectrum and duty. A 100 Hz noise-band level and identical total power across a broadband interfering waveform generally constrain different amounts of in-band interference. Quantitative night availability still needs a matched site noise/interference and gain calibration, field-strength time samples with valid exposure, antenna pattern and phase, and measured ground properties along the path. P.1147 explicitly includes a terminal conductivity effect and its loop result cannot stand in for a receiving connector input.

## Printed P.533 and implementation behavior

P.533 computes E/F2 basic MUF and E-layer screening from climatological control points, solar/ionospheric quantities and geometry. Its short-path model uses mode-specific E/F2 mirror heights, initiation/elevation geometry, slant path spreading, illuminated absorptive penetration points, loss above MUF, ground-reflection hop loss, an auroral/month/local-time correction and a D1-derived excess loss. For >9000 km it uses a composite empirical approach. The 7000–9000 km interval interpolates between those field models, with receiver power interpolation specified separately. The public code's ordinary ≤7000 km receiver power uses each mode's direction-specific receive gain and sums watts, as printed in §6. Its intermediate implementation instead uses the interpolated field with the maximum 0–8° pattern gain in that path direction. Those differ when short-path mode gains differ appreciably from the low-elevation gain. A calibrated single antenna response that already includes effects like polarization and installation loss should be supplied once at this interface. [P.533-14 Annex 1 §§2–6](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.533-14-201908-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf), [pinned `MedianAvailableReceiverPower.c`](https://github.com/ITU-R-Study-Group-3/ITU-R-HF/blob/82017594a1c6cacfaa7e86954c4ae7b3a5825a3d/P533/Src/P533/MedianAvailableReceiverPower.c).

For a short-path term check, hold route, gain and absorption fixed and change only `f/BMUF`. Printed Eq. (25) for E-layer excess loss is quadratic above the basic MUF, with an 81 dB cap. The pinned C E-layer equation has a square-root shape with a +5 dB term and a 58 dB cap. Printed F2 Eq. (26) uses a square root and 62 dB cap. C adds 5 dB (capped at 60 dB) at distances through 3000 km and uses a different linear formula (capped at 80 dB) beyond.
