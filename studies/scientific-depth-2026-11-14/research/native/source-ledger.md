## 1. Bello's time-varying channel representation and WSSUS restrictions

- **Identity/date:** Philip A. Bello, “Characterization of Randomly Time-Variant Linear Channels,” *IEEE Transactions on Communication Systems*, 11(4), 360–393, Dec. 1963. DOI [10.1109/TCOM.1963.1088793](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCOM.1963.1088793).
- **Access/locator:** attempted the official [IEEE full-text endpoint](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/stamp/stamp.jsp?arnumber=1088793&tp=) on 2026-11-14; the web tool reported that URL was inaccessible, so full primary text was not verified. The [COIT historical library record](https://forohistorico.coit.es/index.php/biblioteca/articulos-seminales/item/characterization-of-randomly-time-variant-linear-channels), a secondary hosted abstract, states the dual time/frequency system functions and distinguishes WSS, uncorrelated scattering (US), WSSUS, and quasi-WSSUS. Crossref-derived CiNii metadata at [CiNii](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1363388843473109760) confirms author, journal, date, pages and DOI.
- **Supported finding:** use the channel as a general random time-varying linear system; WSSUS is a specialization. The task's channels may require locally stationary regimes or nonstationary state changes, so choosing WSSUS should be an explicit approximation.
- **Transfer/limit:** secondary abstract only; no table/derivation from the 1963 primary paper was inspected. The equation in the chapter is a generic linear path model and is not attributed as a quotation/equation transcribed from Bello. The scope distinction is supported by the abstract alone.

## 2. Counterevidence for classical deterministic Jakes SOS

- **Identity/date:** M. F. Pop and N. C. Beaulieu, “Limitations of Sum-of-Sinusoids Fading Channel Simulators,” *IEEE Transactions on Communications*, 49(4), 699–708, Apr. 2001. DOI [10.1109/26.917776](https://doi.org/10.1109/26.917776).
- **Access/locator:** public full text uploaded by author Norman C. Beaulieu at [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/3160388_Limitations_of_sum-of-sinusoids_fading_channel_simulators), opened 2026-11-14. Metadata and author-upload notice: webpage metadata and paper pp. 699–700. Abstract is p. 699. Paper pp. 700–705 derive the Jakes ensemble statistics; §II and pp. 701–703 show the nonstationarity/time-varying ensemble PDF and time-difference plus time-sum dependence. Section III and p. 705 discuss the random-phase improvement; pp. 704–705 distinguish first/second-order matches from likely higher-order mismatch due to phase dependence.
- **Supported finding:** a repeated deterministic oscillator bank is cheap but its ensemble stationarity and other statistics cannot be assumed from a visually plausible time trace or periodogram. The paper also shows an improvement can repair WSS/first-second-order behavior but still fail to reproduce higher-order statistics. This is material when fade crossing/duration/outage matters.
- **Limit:** the analysis concerns Clarke/Jakes-style Rayleigh multipath under its stated assumptions; it does not rule out SOS in general, nor establish HF/ionospheric, groundwave, VLF, EME, or exact project performance. It is a methodological counterexample; use the exact reported target statistics for a chosen law.

## 3. Low-oscillator stochastic SOS alternative

- **Identity/date:** Yahong Rosa Zheng and Chengshan Xiao, “Simulation Models With Correct Statistical Properties for Rayleigh Fading Channels,” *IEEE Transactions on Communications*, 51(6), 920–928, June 2003. DOI [10.1109/TCOMM.2003.813259](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCOMM.2003.813259).
- **Access/locator:** author-hosted full PDF at [University of Missouri/Lehigh](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xiaoc/Pub/Zheng_Xiao_TCOM.pdf), opened 2026-11-14. Title/abstract and DOI/issue/date are on pp. 920–921; mathematical reference model and required correlations are §II, pp. 920–923; model/statistical analysis is §III, pp. 923–926; numerical evaluation is §IV, pp. 926–927; conclusion is p. 927. The authors' abstract says the construction randomizes path gains, initial phases and conditional Doppler frequencies; quadrature and complex-envelope autocorrelations/cross-correlations exactly match desired values even at single-digit oscillator count, while envelope/phase PDFs, LCR, AFD and squared-envelope correlation converge asymptotically; it reports good convergence for 8 sinusoids in their evaluations.
- **Supported finding:** if the selected process is isotropic-scattering Rayleigh fading, randomized SOS is a credible cheap alternative to naive deterministic Jakes. It directly addresses multiple uncorrelated fading waveforms and evaluates high-order behavior, unlike a naïve symmetry-preserving oscillator design.
- **Limit:** “8 terms” is the authors' tested convergence result for their model/statistics, not a universal bound or calibration. Higher-order behavior is asymptotic, and all assumptions must match. SOS is not a generic process generator for nonstationary or non-isotropic models.

## 4. Current standardized high-frequency TDL example

- **Identity/date:** ETSI, [3GPP TR 138 901 V19.5.0 (2026-09), Release 19](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_tr/138900_138999/138901/19.05.00_60/tr_138901v190500p.pdf), “Study on channel model for frequencies from 0.5 to 100 GHz.” Official PDF title/revision page 1; cover is dated 2026-09.
- **Primary locators:** Clause 7.7.2, printed pp. 91–92: TDLs are for 0.5–100 GHz, up to 2 GHz bandwidth; A/B/C are NLOS profiles and D/E LOS; each tap has classical Jakes PSD and maximum Doppler from velocity/wavelength; LOS cases use Rician first tap with a `0.7 f_D` component; delay profiles can be RMS-delay scaled. Tables 7.7.2-1 onward give tap data.
- **Supported finding:** use as a current implementation/parameterization precedent for per-tap state, Jakes-based spectral law, LOS/Rician mixture and independent delay scaling. It provides an explicit relation from physical velocity to maximum Doppler and illustrates why each path may require a different temporal component.
- **Limit:** release's model domain starts at 0.5 GHz. It is not empirical validation below 30 MHz or near 5.8 GHz Earth–Moon–Earth. Even where 5.8 GHz lies numerically inside the range, its terrestrial scenarios/measurements are not an EME link model. Do not copy the TDL tap tables as universal path priors.

## 5. ITU HF recommendation as performance appraisal precedent

- **Identity/date/status:** ITU-R P.533-14, “[Method for the prediction of the performance of HF circuits](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.533-14-201908-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf),” approved/published Aug. 2019. Official recommendation page ([status](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.533/en)) checked 2026-11-14 lists P.533-14 as in force (main), superseding earlier versions.
- **Primary locators:** official PDF, title/scope pp. 1–2, paragraphs 1 and 2: method predicts available frequencies, signal levels, reliability for analog/digital HF systems and includes expected time/frequency spreads; recommendation 1 gives skywave applicability 2–30 MHz; considering paragraph (b) requires transmitting/receiving antenna performance characteristics. Clause 10.2 covers digital reliability with time/frequency spreading; §10.2.2 treats time delay.
- **Supported finding:** appraisal should include signal level, time and frequency spread, SNR, and digital reliability, with antenna/front-end inputs. It is more than a received-level prediction.
- **Limit:** prediction procedure for sky-wave HF 2–30 MHz; not a sample-time stochastic channel model, not groundwave, not VLF, not EME, and not transferable without correct antenna and path inputs.

## 7. Fractional-delay structures and colored-noise detection

- **Fractional-delay source:** V. Välimäki and T. I. Laakso, “Fractional Delay Filters—Design and Applications,” in *Nonuniform Sampling*, 2001, Chapter 20, pp. 835–895, DOI [10.1007/978-1-4615-1229-5_20](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4615-1229-5_20). Full author-uploaded chapter available at [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/246232971_Fractional_Delay_Filters-Design_and_Applications), opened 2026-11-14. §§3.1–3.2 discuss polyphase/FIR designs and their rational-delay-grid restriction; §5 and pp. 53–55 describe variable delay and Farrow implementation. Farrow's fixed subfilter coefficients avoid expensive coefficient updates for every delay change, but can cost more than direct FIR if delay is constant/infrequent. The chapter also shows approximation error depends on chosen bandwidth/filter design; e.g. its Lagrange examples lose high-frequency accuracy. This directly supports retaining/test-calibrating current interpolation and choosing Farrow only for a variable-delay use case. The original Farrow article itself was not retrieved.
- **Colored-noise source:** Steven M. Kay, “Asymptotically Optimal Detection in Unknown Colored Noise via Autoregressive Modeling,” *IEEE Transactions on Acoustics, Speech, and Signal Processing*, 31(4), 927–940, 1983, DOI [10.1109/TASSP.1983.1164156](https://doi.org/10.1109/TASSP.1983.1164156). Institutional publication record and abstract at [University of Rhode Island Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/ele_facpubs/1403/), opened 2026-11-14. The primary abstract treats known-signal detection in colored Gaussian noise with unknown covariance, models noise as AR of known order/unknown coefficients, derives a GLRT, and reports asymptotic equivalence to optimal prewhitener+matched filter as records grow; simulations say the required length may be small. This supports evaluating a compact AR/whitening path when Gaussian colored noise is evidenced, not universal assumptions about non-Gaussian interference or short modem records.

## 8. Reproducible AR(1) versus Jakes covariance check

- **Artifacts:** [`ar1_vs_jakes.py`](./ar1_vs_jakes.py) and the retained actual stdout [`ar1_vs_jakes.out`](./ar1_vs_jakes.out), run with `python3 studies/scientific-depth-2026-11-14/research/native/ar1_vs_jakes.py` on the assignment checkout, 2026-11-14.
- **Method:** evaluate the classical isotropic correlation `J0(2π f_D τ)` with the convergent power series for J0 over `0 ≤ 2π f_D τ ≤ 2π`. Set an AR(1) coefficient equal to the Jakes lag-one correlation at `Δt`; compare `ρ^lag` with Jakes at lags 1, 5, 10, 20, for `f_D Δt = 0.01` and `0.05`. This does not implement or simulate either channel; it is a deterministic covariance comparison using the standard Bessel reference stated in the papers.
- **Observed output:** at `f_D Δt=0.01`, lag 1 matches 0.999013283 by construction; lag-20 target is 0.642511837 vs AR(1) 0.980449556 (error 0.337937720). At `0.05`, lag-5 target 0.472001216 vs 0.883256603 (error 0.411255388); lag-10 target -0.304242178 vs 0.780142227 (error 1.084384405); lag-20 target 0.220276909 vs 0.608621895 (error 0.388344986).
- **Conclusion/limit:** matching one lag does not match spectral/long-run behavior. This rejects AR(1) as a universal Jakes substitute, not as a useful exponential-covariance or short-timescale approximation. It is not an estimate of modem or channel performance.

## 9. Absolute RF-phase precision check

- **Artifacts:** [`phase_precision.py`](./phase_precision.py) and retained actual stdout [`phase_precision.out`](./phase_precision.out). Run with `python3 studies/scientific-depth-2026-11-14/research/native/phase_precision.py` on 2026-11-14.
- **Method/result:** compare exact decimal product of `1793404800.123456789 s × 5.8 GHz` against binary64. The exact fractional cycle is `0.2`; binary64 time rounds to `1793404800.1234567`, its time ULP is `2.38418579102e-7 s`, product ULP `2048` cycles, and computed fractional cycle is `0` (0.2-cycle error for that input).
- **Conclusion/limit:** do not multiply large absolute UTC by RF in binary64 to derive phase.
