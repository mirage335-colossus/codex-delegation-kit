# Native continuous simulation and receiver integration

## Decision handoff

**Reuse the canonical continuous C++ channel and complete receiver.** The current path already streams the composite source through one `StreamingChannel` to the same `CompleteStreamReceiver`, with physical-time state, bounded TX history, fractional delay/hop interpolation, phase integration, a validity mask, existing Gaussian/OU/bounded path laws, and front-end effects. The receiver already has public coarse/fine/quiet stages, an ordered bounded stream executor, admission limits, and an optional fixed-weight evidence hook. Add a model only where the propagation/noise branches identify a missing physical observable. Do not put a second modem or a channel-truth side channel in the receiver.

**For time-varying complex path gains, make the covariance/PSD a named model property.** Exact OU transitions are a cheap useful law only when exponential correlation is intended. They are not a substitute for a Jakes/Bessel or Gaussian/bimodal Doppler spectrum. Classical deterministic Jakes oscillators are also not a universal cheap answer: the literature reports ensemble nonstationarity and correlation defects. Compare (1) the already implemented OU and Gaussian state laws, (2) a properly randomized finite sum-of-sinusoids model for stationary isotropic scattering, and (3) a short shaped-innovation filter for measured or selected PSD shapes. Use regime changes as continuous latent-state changes with physical-time updates; do not re-seed at chunk, slot, or public-frame boundaries.

**Keep receiver work observation driven.** Preserve the current blind/public hypothesis calendar and full public coverage. If colored receiver noise is measured, estimate its covariance from valid quiet/pilot observations and apply one bounded whitening/filter stage or a compact low-order likelihood; avoid dense covariance inversion for every frequency/delay candidate. Preserve the current ambiguous/unsupported result on holes and keep the fixed-prior/fixed-weight supplement disabled in ordinary operation until its call path, missing-mass semantics, and cost are independently established.

**Benchmark complete paths per candidate geometry.** A few complex operations per sample are not a capacity result. Count sample history, interpolation, coefficient generation, noise/front-end filtering, full receiver work, candidate counts, live lanes, and retained workspace together. The records available here do not include an executed throughput qualification; none is claimed. Existing documentation reports one scoped matched replay improvement but explicitly excludes all-configuration real-time capacity.

## Channel state generation: distinguish the process before choosing the cheap update

Bello writes a time-varying linear channel using dual time/frequency system functions, and then derives special WSS, US, and WSSUS cases. WSSUS is useful when channel correlation is stationary in time and distinct delay components are uncorrelated; it is an assumption, not a synonym for every time-varying channel. A practical local model can use

`y(t) = Σ_l a_l(t) exp(-j 2π f_c τ_l(t)) x(t - τ_l(t)) + n(t)`

with a per-path complex state, physical delay and optional polarization/front-end map. The form makes the seams explicit: delay acts on the continuous composite; path state varies in physical time; carrier phase from propagation is retained; noise is injected at its declared receiver boundary. For a stochastic model, define the delay-power profile and each path's temporal covariance/PSD separately. Independence among taps should be a selectable assumption only where the propagation evidence supports uncorrelated scattering. A shared latent motion/ionosphere state can induce correlation among path gains, Doppler, delay, and outage; independent draws for each path/carrier can erase that structure.

The familiar isotropic two-dimensional Rayleigh reference process has complex-envelope correlation proportional to `J0(2π f_D τ)`, which gives the classical U-shaped/Jakes Doppler spectrum on finite support. It is not an OU process. A first-order complex Gauss-Markov/OU update

`z[k+1] = ρ z[k] + sqrt(1 - |ρ|²) σ w[k]`, `ρ = exp(-Δt/T)`

is exact at its sample times for the selected exponential covariance and is very cheap, but has a different (Lorentzian-like) PSD. Matching a process's variance and its one-sample correlation does not match its long-lag correlation, Doppler edge, fades, or outage runs. The reproducible desk check `ar1_vs_jakes.py` matches AR(1)'s lag-one coefficient to Jakes and evaluates Bessel correlations. With `f_D Δt=0.05`, lag-10 correlation is `-0.304242` for Jakes and `+0.780142` for the matched AR(1), absolute difference `1.084384`; at lag 20 the difference is `0.388345`. At the slower `f_D Δt=0.01`, lag-20 difference is still `0.337938`. This rejects “OU with a fitted time constant” as a general Doppler model; it does not reject OU where an observed envelope process is actually exponential.

For finite isotropic scattering, Zheng and Xiao's 2003 stochastic sum-of-sinusoids construction adds random path gains, phases, and conditional Doppler frequencies. Their analysis states exact desired autocorrelation/cross-correlation for quadrature and complex envelope even with single-digit sinusoid count, while envelope/phase distributions, LCR, AFD, and squared-envelope correlation converge asymptotically, with good reported convergence for eight sinusoids in their evaluated model. This is a credible low-work option when those conditions match. The counterevidence is important: Pop and Beaulieu's 2001 analysis identifies time-dependent ensemble envelope statistics and other failures in classical Jakes and derivatives. Therefore “use Jakes” is not an adequate model contract; test stationarity and the exact statistics needed, across independent seeds and several windows. The stochastic SOS method also remains a finite approximation; its favorable 8-term result is not a universal guarantee for every process, near-zero crossing tail, multiple correlated channels, or a non-isotropic environment.

A filtered innovation process is the alternative when evidence gives a desired Gaussian, bi-Gaussian, or measured spectrum. Precompute a bounded FIR/IIR filter whose squared frequency response approximates the target PSD; generate complex Gaussian innovations only at a coefficient update rate that retains the supported Doppler band; interpolate coefficients to sample time with the chosen reconstruction filter. A short FIR is predictable and per-state cost is `O(P)` for `P` taps, while a frequency-domain block filter is `O(B log B)` and adds block latency/workspace. A low-order IIR is cheaper, but poles, PSD error and warm-up need checking. Those are alternatives, not simultaneous defaults: compare target PSD, ACF, LCR/AFD/outage and work over the maximum intended temporal span. Use multirate updates only after deriving a Nyquist/alias bound from the declared Doppler support; for Gaussian spectra with nonzero tails, state the tolerated tail-energy/observable error rather than claiming a hard bandlimit.

Nonstationary change belongs in a model above the stationary generator: smoothly vary the target path powers, delays, Doppler centers/widths or filter coefficients using a documented physical-time latent state; handle a real mode transition with a state-conditioned transition or overlap/crossfade, preserving continuity. Never refresh random phases per audio/DSP block. Existing stream code already advances path/noise state across reads and erased intervals; keep that invariant and check chunk-size equivalence. A sudden outage is a distinct state transition with a distribution/evidence source, not a gain clipped to a convenient floor. The current bounded two-path GUI law is useful for controlled stress, but its own documentation says it is not a measured HF law and cannot cover direct-path loss or diffuse multi-path.

## Delay sampling, interpolation, and front-end coupling

Use independent clock quantities: sample index and rate, physical elapsed time, symbol/public-calendar time, and RF/IF carrier phase. Preserve the existing separate TX/RX time bases and bounded history. A path with real delay varies the *source sample time* and the baseband carrier phase; simply multiplying each received carrier by an independently chosen fade does not implement a common delay. The correct discrete path needs integer history offset plus a fractional-delay reconstruction. Fixed fractional offsets can use precomputed coefficients/polyphase filters; polyphase gives only rational-delay branches for a finite phase count. Continuously variable delay can use a Farrow-type polynomial FIR or another validated variable fractional-delay filter. Laakso and Välimäki explain the trade: Farrow fixes subfilter coefficients and avoids coefficient updates when the delay changes often, while a direct FIR can be cheaper for a constant/infrequently updated delay; bandwidth, filter order and delay resolution trade against approximation error. Keep the existing interpolation first; replace or extend it only if passband, group-delay, and phase-error checks show a material defect over the declared occupied band. See the full author-uploaded [fractional-delay review chapter](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/246232971_Fractional_Delay_Filters-Design_and_Applications), §§3, 5 and discussion at pp. 53–55.

An interpolator is an approximation to bandlimited reconstruction. Linear interpolation is cheap but is not exact for near-Nyquist content; its amplitude and phase error depend on fractional offset and normalized input frequency. The appropriate bounded experiment is to sweep fractional offsets over `[0,1)`, relevant modulation tones across the declared passband, and delay motion at its upper bound, then compare amplitude, group delay, EVM/score and full-receiver decision against a high-precision sinc reference with matched bandlimited inputs. Evaluate out-of-band-energy/alias impacts at the actual stream rate; do not use a requested “spectral allowance” as proof all useful energy was captured. Preserve delayed tails and filter decay through the receiver's actual quiet completion.

For geometry candidates sharing the same transmitted composite, share immutable source history. Keep per-geometry delay, phase and path states isolated. The complex path matrix can include polarization only when antenna/feed/channel inputs define its reference axes and normalization; preserve the transmitter/receiver basis and conversion losses through the front end. Generate receiver noise after propagation (unless a named physical receiver element requires a different location), and keep external interference distinct from receiver thermal noise. Channel model “truth” remains diagnostic only: receiver search sees the resulting samples, erasure mask and declared public hypotheses.

ITU-R P.533-14 is a useful methodological precedent for HF, but its scope is skywave performance at 2–30 MHz and it calls out transmitting and receiving antenna performance data as needed inputs. Its procedure computes frequency availability, median field/power, noise/SNR, reliability, delay and digital performance; it is not a sample-by-sample fading law. It should inform the appraisal/check vocabulary, not be applied to sub-9 kHz, 1 MHz groundwave, or 5.8 GHz EME as if calibrated there; see the [official P.533-14 recommendation](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.533-14-201908-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf). Likewise, [3GPP TR 38.901 Release 19.5.0 TDL models](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_tr/138900_138999/138901/19.05.00_60/tr_138901v190500p.pdf) are explicitly 0.5–100 GHz, up to 2 GHz bandwidth, with per-tap Jakes spectrum and optional LOS/Rician component. This current (ETSI 2026-09) report demonstrates a clean link-level parameterization and delay scaling pattern, but does not validate its profile distributions below 30 MHz or in lunar EME.

## Inference and receiver behavior

Keep the full received composite in the native receiver. Extend the channel's observable output and separately declared public receiver bounds only when required by evidence; do not hand path delay, gain, outage or random seed to the decode/search routine. For unknown delay/Doppler, preserve present public coarse/fine search and add a bounded local estimator only after the propagation branch identifies a gap relative to those searches. A local refinement around surviving public candidates is cheaper and easier to audit than adding a second global scan; it must not prune public candidates based on hidden simulated truth.

For Gaussian colored noise with covariance `R`, the observation likelihood depends on the whitened residual, e.g. `eᴴ R⁻¹ e`, and on normalization/valid support. Kay's primary 1983 detection analysis for known signals in colored Gaussian noise with unknown covariance models noise as a known-order AR process and derives a GLRT; for large records, its detection performance matches an optimal prewhitener plus matched filter. See the [institutional full abstract and article citation](https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/ele_facpubs/1403/) (DOI 10.1109/TASSP.1983.1164156). This supports a compact AR/whitening branch as a candidate where measured noise is Gaussian and colored, not as a universal cure. If `R` is Toeplitz/stationary, a bounded causal AR/whitening filter or frequency-domain weighting can reuse the present matched-filter/search stage; a dense per-candidate `R⁻¹` or fresh Cholesky decomposition over a long slot scales cubically in sample dimension and is an unnecessary hot-path default. Estimate the noise PSD/covariance from the public quiet interval or appropriate known pilots, and cross-check it on held-out samples. When noise is impulsive, mixture/non-Gaussian interference is more appropriate than claiming Gaussian covariance cures it; use the separate noise/interference evidence branch before selecting it. Keep the signal channel (multipath) and additive receiver/interference noise as separate components so error causes remain interpretable.

Preserve current support/likelihood semantics around holes: a missing sample is not a zero-valued clean sample. Existing public behavior for partial validity is ambiguous/unsupported erasure; do not score or normalize a candidate as though all hypotheses had identical usable evidence unless the receiver's explicit mask-aware likelihood does that. The explicit supplement entry point is `score_slot_with_supplement`; the ordinary complete-stream factory calls `score_slot` without it. Before any adoption, define the prior/likelihood over all supported candidates, account for omitted posterior mass instead of redistributing it over survivors, and compare false acquisition, miss rate, and calibration on matched complete receiver cases. No task-specific ordinary-path experiment or test-callsite survey was run here.

## Numerical precision and low-work implementation

Use the project's qualified native arithmetic routes. `docs/robust-current.md` already has condition- and stage-dependent FP32/Q15/FP64 paths and guard/fallback reasoning. The canonical waveform type is `std::complex<double>` today, so do not narrow the whole path. For a new isolated path-gain or filter-state array, test FP32 as the low-cost candidate and preserve double for physical elapsed time, RF phase/clock coordinates, or a numerically sensitive estimator; adopt the narrower state only after paired channel-statistic and receiver-output checks show it meets the observables. A long running phase can accumulate rounding; maintain phase through a split/reduced cycle counter/remainder or test a recurrence against a higher-precision reference and periodically renormalize. Avoid blanket FP64 in every sample and blanket half/Q15 for a new filter. The weakest stage's actual observable requirement governs.

The 5.8-GHz EME phase path has a separate absolute-time hazard. A reproducible binary64 calculation in `phase_precision.py` uses UTC-like time `1793404800.123456789 s`: exact decimal multiplication by `5.8 GHz` has fractional cycle `0.2`, but binary64 rounds the time to `1793404800.1234567 s`, the product's ULP is 2,048 cycles, and the computed fractional phase becomes 0 (0.2-cycle error in this illustrative input). This is an example of multiplying a large absolute epoch by carrier frequency, not a measurement or a claim that the current code uses this operation. Keep the epoch/site geometry as explicit source data; compute RF cycles from a local elapsed-time/path-difference origin, reduce them modulo one before trig, and maintain integer/split cycle plus high-resolution fractional remainder or a continuously advanced phase state. Verify the exact platform's `long double` representation instead of assuming it is wider than `double`. A meter, clock, site-coordinate, ephemeris or atmosphere uncertainty may dominate tiny floating-point improvements; represent these as uncertainty inputs rather than spending FP64 throughout the sample path. Falsify a selected phase path with a high-precision oracle over the longest calendar and range of site/path deltas, then compare the receiver's public phase/frequency/score outputs.

Precompute filter/polyphase coefficients, per-window deterministic geometry, and model state/workspaces outside the per-sample loop; allocate bounded scratch once per leased stream/geometry. If profiling shows per-sample trig in phase updates is expensive, use phase increments/complex recurrence with periodic normalization, and compare over longest simulated duration because cheap recurrence can introduce drift. Batch/vectorize independent candidates only where data layout helps; variable fractional-delay gathers may limit contiguous SIMD, and over-batching creates copies, memory traffic, or latency. Keep single shared TX history, fixed per-candidate path-state arrays, and worker-local receiver scratch within the executor's existing lease/accounting model. No Python process belongs in this DSP path.

## Cost model, scheduling, and proof of capacity

The actual default `Format.sample_rate` is 960 complex samples/s. The native interpolator is a windowed-sinc kernel with offsets −7 through +8 (16 taps) for a fractional coordinate and a direct one-sample lookup when the coordinate is integral (`include/datapump/triad_modem_v3.hpp:31–36`; `src/triad_simulation_stream.cpp:193–218`). A current two-ray stress with 7 ms excess delay puts the delayed path 6.72 samples behind at 960 samples/s; an offset may be integer or fractional according to the TX/RX clocks. Under ideal integer-aligned direct sampling, that direct path takes the one-sample branch; under fractional clocks both paths may use 16 taps. For an illustrative per-output bound with both current rays fractional, that is `2 × 16 × 960 = 30,720` complex tap contributions/s per geometry. A deliberately expanded scenario of 12 candidates × 8 paths × 16 taps × 960 samples/s is `1,474,560` complex tap contributions/s aggregate if all paths are fractional. This is only an arithmetic/memory-traffic count: it excludes gain/phase generation, cache misses, front-end filtering, receiver search, allocation and scheduling. It is not a speed forecast or a proposal to multiply candidate geometries or taps by those counts. With 30 Hz Doppler support, a 960-Hz coefficient lattice has 32 samples per maximum-Doppler cycle; a 180-Hz bound has 5.33 samples/cycle and stays under the 480-Hz Nyquist limit, but higher internal update-rate/filter choices still need their own alias/error checks. Receiver-visible model outputs—not this operation count—decide acceptable fidelity.

Let `M` be channel output samples per complete receiver window, `G` simultaneously explored geometries, `L` physical paths/taps per geometry, `K` interpolation multiply-adds, `P` coefficient-filter taps, and `q` the coefficient update fraction of the output rate. A first-order simulation estimate is

`C_channel ≈ M·G·L·(K + C_gain_phase) + M·C_frontend + M·C_noise + M·q·G·L·P`.

This is an operation-count sketch, not a throughput measurement: memory gathers, transcendental functions, branches, FFTs, allocator behavior and cache misses can dominate. If every geometry invokes the full receiver before pruning, include `G·C_complete_receiver(M,H)` where `H` denotes the public hypothesis search shape; if only `G'` survivors invoke it, report the public screening rule and cost separately. A fixed-bank residual/whitener must also be charged. Report costs by observed candidate and tap count rather than compressing unlike tasks into one operations/sample number.

Preserve the existing bounded worker contract (`W=2H`, owner leases, ordered commit and admission). The channel may share immutable source history; don't share mutable RNG or per-candidate state unsafely. Bound memory for each active channel path, coefficient history and receiver workspace before dispatch. Resource admission should include in-flight sample arrays, thread stacks and retained results. On a GPU, channel synthesis, phase state, front-end IIR/FIR, and fine receiver stages may remain on CPU while bulk searches use existing backend leaves; measure transfer/synchronization and workspace lease as part of end-to-end time. A GPU's peak FLOPs or a smaller kernel time cannot prove a benefit when candidate sets are short or spread across separate full receivers.

Capacity checks should preserve demanding cases and public bounds. For matched baseline/corrected inputs and environmental seed lists, measure (a) channel throughput and bytes/sample by `(G,L,K,P)`, (b) complete receiver service time and output agreement, (c) p50/p95/p99 and worst repeated-window service time, (d) queue/backlog, admission denial, cancellation and memory peaks, and (e) model fidelity—PSD/ACF, fades/outages, delay/frequency observable, sample-mask behavior. Include quiet, helper acquisition, weak signal, close/coherent paths, partial holes, changing regime, long delay tails and recalibration/reacquisition. Use independent seeds and block-order/chunk-size metamorphic checks. Public output changes and evaluator/model truth statistics are reported separately; identical decode results do not demonstrate that two implementations received matched I/Q, mask, noise, or model parameters.

Keep bandwidth, maximum path delay/Doppler, candidate count and receiver search limits explicit. Increase channel resolution only until the channel observable and receiver decision stabilize against the high-precision/reference path; test practical clipping and noise placement separately. If channel fidelity is already stable but complete processing misses its time/resource bound, reduce filter/path count, coefficient update rate (after alias check), or candidate receiver work without dropping public coverage; if fidelity fails, retain the more faithful model even if its cost rises and reconsider the capacity tradeoff at the branch/lead boundary.

## Receiver-visible spectral support and a declared resource scenario

`B_data = 0.625 × inner_rate × added_symbols`.

The emitted waveform support additionally uses `full_bandwidth = B_data + 2/ramp_seconds` (`triad_stream.cpp:480–485`), and the configured spectral skirt adds `gate_bandwidth/2`. For a source-selected 2400-Hz high tone, full support requires `Fs > 2×(2400+100+360.52) = 5721.04 Hz`; for a 2500-Hz tone it requires `Fs >5921.04 Hz`. The helper plan also requires `Fs/2−250 ≥ f_H`, or 5300 / 5500 Hz respectively. In either branch the passband-plus-skirts bound is stricter, so the integer rate must be at least 5722 or 5922 samples/s. The fixture's 6000-Hz rate covers either accepted case. This is a deterministic feasibility bound from its source parameters, not a physical audio device qualification; GUI playback/capture still negotiates a separate rate (the Helper utility imposes a practical 48-kHz floor on real audio).

The receiver's public search structure also has a cost floor that channel microbenchmarks miss. Public epoch timing proposals are bounded to at most twice the 13 declared epoch offsets; segmented timing proposals are capped at eight (`triad_receiver.cpp:215–217`, `triad_receiver.hpp:19`). Coarse search uses the public frequency prior and clock grid, creates a bounded set of proposals, then fine search spans frequency bound plus atmospheric variation; DATA, Helper-only quiet, varying-channel, and retained-line branches are not equivalent workload classes (`triad_receiver.cpp:195–220,438–578`). An invalid sample anywhere preserves the existing Helper-only/unsupported-erasure outcome; the new stochastic channel must not hide or re-score that support. The fixed-prior evidence supplement remains an explicit `score_slot_with_supplement` entry point; it is not the ordinary complete-stream caller. Therefore a bounded fixed-bank route may only run after its public admission rule is specified, with prior mass and omitted candidates accounted for, and its incremental cost reported against the ordinary full-coverage receiver. The available evidence does not establish a production caller or benchmark for that path.

For a practical storage/work envelope, separate durable inputs, path history, output/validity, and leased receiver workspace. With one complex-double value plus one byte of validity per retained output sample, a saved receiver record is 17 bytes/sample; `N=Fs·T` samples therefore require `17 Fs T` bytes/lane before metadata and serialization overhead. The channel TX ring is instead `(ceil(max_delay_seconds × Fs × tx_scale)+64) × 16` bytes per lane per owner source report (64-sample interpolation/history halo; complex-double source values), plus path states, filter states and receiver-side buffers. The 16-tap interpolator is a read window, not 16 full output copies. Add input, output, mask, source history, per-lane/path states, front-end tail and all concurrently leased receiver workspaces when deriving admission. The owner reports `W=2H` worker/executor ownership, ordered commits and bounded input; do not multiply one lane's memory by `W` blindly because the exact sharing/lease layout must be obtained from the constructed stream and workspace. Apply backpressure before admitting more complete windows than the configured buffer and executor bounds, and record queue depth, lease denials, cancellation and peak resident bytes.

The controlled fixture uses `Fs=6000`; at 17 bytes per stored IQ+validity sample, a 10-second single-lane record would be 60,000 samples / 1.02 MB, while a 90-second record would be 540,000 samples / 9.18 MB. These are capture-buffer arithmetic, not a claim that this Helper-on fixture has a 90-second waveform or that 10 seconds covers its public window. In its 21-ms channel delay, the TX ring needs about `ceil(.021×6000×tx_scale)+64` complex values; with the near-unity clock scales in this fixture, this is about 191 values / 3056 bytes per source lane, before filter state and path coefficients. Any fractional two-ray output may read up to `2×16=32` complex taps; at 6000 output samples/s this is at most 192,000 tap contributions/s per geometry. Exact phase/gain work, actual fractional coordinates, and the count of path branches are not determined by that upper count.

For contrast, an explicitly **conditional** long low-rate versus wider EME-like accounting example holds duration and byte representation fixed, not carrier-frequency assumptions: 3600 seconds at 6000 complex samples/s is 21.6 million samples / 367.2 MB per lane; 10 seconds at a hypothetical 6 million complex samples/s is 60 million samples / 1.02 GB per lane. The former has 360× more input seconds but uses 0.36× the samples and raw storage of the latter; channel interpolation work follows samples × geometries × paths × actual kernel width. This does not claim that 6 Msamples/s is supported by a given GUI or that either rate describes a VLF/EME mode. VLF carrier/bandwidth and wide EME support must come from a jointly prepared source profile before a resource promise. At fixed duration, bytes and per-sample channel work scale with the declared complex sample rate; at fixed sample rate, path history scales with `ceil(delay × Fs)`; geometry/path count multiplies the per-output channel cost. No VLF or EME bandwidth, delay, Helper mode, public grid, or CPU/GPU throughput is supplied, so actual cross-regime resource values remain unresolved.

The capability decision is therefore conservative and explicit: retain CPU/native C++ as the canonical channel/state/receiver path and take `late_affine_clock_support`'s Helper-on `prepare_profile` path as the bounded next experiment's source of truth. Defer a generalized “GUI profile admitted” claim until the selected runtime settings, physical audio rate and returned Helper frequency/spectrum fields are captured and the exact slot plans fit admitted memory. Defer GPU use for the new channel and complete receiver; existing qualified stage-specific backend leaves remain usable only when the selected public workload passes their documented capability guards. Do not infer full-route GPU qualification from fixture source branches or device availability. A future route comparison must declare profile and precision, verify device support, compare identical I/Q/mask and receiver outputs, include upload/download, synchronization and leased workspace, and measure complete service/backlog/latency on named hardware. The policy acceptance target of under one second of complete-receiver service per input second remains a target, not a measured result. ## Paired complete-path cost envelope: canonical baseline and diagnostic shadow

The receiver lead's bounded comparison is a **diagnostic-only proper-complex AR(1) fine shadow scorer**, not a production whitening path. It reuses the first `J≤min(4, bank size)` already-admitted `(epoch, arrival, clock, common-frequency)` coordinates in the scorer's frozen native order and records their actual coordinates and weights. Epoch is part of the tuple; the four-coordinate cap is not multiplied by all 13 epoch alternatives. No new proposal grid or second complete receiver is created. Both bit hypotheses use a scalar proper-complex gain marginalized at a frozen prior. The comparison includes `a=0` as the identity control and an offline-fitted real `a` under a common held-out null; fit only from independent calibrated noise-only training separate from all four evaluation records; freeze for records 3/4. If no independent training data exist, omit the fitted arm; record1 remains the held-out null control and record2 remains the gain/phase fit. Diagnostic scores never enter the canonical white-noise e-values, quiet decision, codec, or message completion.

Before reading outcomes, predeclare eligible **complete-valid** windows and include representative ordinary DATA accept, quiet/ambiguous and retained-line/null cases. A mask hole stays on the baseline path. Preserve the raw-IQ quiet gate and every ordinary fallback. For the Helper-on source fixture above, mark raw shadow outcomes `unqualified_auxiliary` unless an identical frozen Helper and line-residual treatment is applied to every hypothesis; count the diagnostic arithmetic but infer no BER or quiet improvement from those raw scores. Do not fit `a` or select coordinates on the held-out result.

For the lead's operation accounting, form shared `Wy` once at `4N` real operations for `N` complex observation samples, ignoring the first-row adjustment. Each raw template/bit row of `n_j` samples adds `16 n_j` operations for whitening, inner product and norm, plus template rendering and final scalar `log1p`. With four coordinates × two bit hypotheses, an upper count where each `n_j≤n` is `4N + 128n`. Paired identity statistics reuse the rendered template and raw iterate, adding 12 operations per template row; this makes the complete `J=4` paired diagnostic bound `4N + 224n`. Include coefficient/normalization, first-row convention and render cost in the measurement record. The serial diagnostic uses one `16N`-byte whitened scratch under the ordinary stage lease; template state/receipt have separately quoted bounds. Serial execution avoids another W-sized worker pool or materialized per-template vectors. These are code-informed work bounds, not observed runtime or throughput.
