## Primary standards and technical procedures

1. **ITU-R P.368-10, “Ground-wave propagation prediction method for frequencies between 10 kHz and 30 MHz,” approved 2022-08-24; current page marks it in force.** [Official PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.368-10-202208-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf). Read pp. 2–4 and Annexes 1–2/figures (web PDF lines 103–157, 161–296, 299–331, 334–398). Decisive clauses: use only when ionospheric reflection amplitude is negligible; do not use when receiver is well above Earth; smooth homogeneous spherical earth; near-surface short vertical monopole reference, 1kW and 300mV/m at1km; vertical far-field E; frequency 10kHz–30MHz; field-to-basic-loss conversion; 3–4dB nearby spatial level standard deviation and seasonal groundwave effect; reciprocal mixed-path Millington formula. Contains Figures 1–2 with multi-frequency curves including 1 and 30MHz over low/average-salinity water and Annex figures/curves. Figure chart inspection was text-description only; no curve digitization or program execution was done.
2. **ITU-R P.527-6, “Electrical characteristics of the surface of the Earth,” 2021 revision, official page/publishing copy posted 2026.** [Official PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.527-6-202109-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf). Read scope and formulation (pp.1–4; lines 61–91, 97–172), soil conditions (lines 247–265), Figure 24 and P.368/P.832 conventional parameter attachment (lines 1930–1979). Supports complex relative permittivity, material/frequency/temperature dependence, moisture and freeze effects, illustrative conductivity range, map/measurement sensitivity. Figure-curve values are approximate visual magnitudes, not a material database. The 2026 “editorial amendment” note is recorded in the source; it does not identify a new technical edition.
3. **ITU-R P.526-16, “Propagation by diffraction,” approved 2025-11.** [Official PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.526-16-202511-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf). Read first Fresnel zone (pp.3–4; lines 145–176), smooth spherical-earth diffraction and recommendation to use P.368 LFMF-SmoothEarth below 10MHz (lines 317–370), and terrestrial obstacle methods. Useful discriminator for 30MHz terrain/obstruction and horizon/refraction; not the P.368 surface-wave phase model.
4. **ITU-R P.1812-8 (2025-09), path-specific point-to-area terrestrial method 30MHz–6GHz.** [Official recommendation page](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.1812-8-202509-I/en), [official scope summary](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.1812-8-202509-I%21%21SUM-HTM-E.htm). Guide table says field/basic-loss output, 0.25–about 3000km, time 1–50%, location 1–99%, path-profile inputs; use P.1546 if detailed profiles are unavailable. This is an alternative for 30MHz service coverage, not a field-specific complex impulse response. P.1546 applies from 30MHz to 4GHz point-to-area and is likewise not interchangeable as an additive loss.
5. **ITU-R Handbook on Ground Wave Propagation (2014).** [Official PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-r/opb/hdb/R-HDB-59-2014-PDF-E.pdf). Read mixed-path section 7.2 (pp.16–17; lines 474–493), which explains forward/reverse Millington, averaging fields in dB, and Annex 2 steps. The same handbook says P.368 curves are GRWAVE calculations and that smooth-earth prediction can remain useful when surface irregularities are small relative to wavelength and gradual. Distinguishes practical interpolation from terrain-resolving full-wave methods.
6. **ITU-R guide to prediction methods.** [Official guide table](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/study-groups/rsg3/rwp3m/Pages/recp1144tables.aspx). Read the row for P.368 (point-to-point field-strength, 10kHz–30MHz, 1–10,000km, ground-based; frequency/heights/power/refractivity/distance/σ/ε/polarization inputs), plus rows for P.1812/P.1546. This is a scope index, not validation.

## Original measurements, model comparisons and contemporary evidence

7. **Kirke, “Calculation of Ground-Wave Field Strength Over a Composite Land and Sea Path,” Proc. IRE, May 1949.** [Scanned original article](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-IRE/40s/IRE-1949-05.pdf). Read pp.489–490 (web PDF lines 3321–3345). Supports the mechanism that energy distribution above ground/wave tilt depends on preceding ground and can cause recovery after poor land when the path enters good-conductivity land/sea. The experiment is a limited early validation and explicitly discusses imperfect effective conductivity estimates.
8. **Aono & Muramatsu, “Measurement of Field Intensity of Ground Wave over Mixed Paths,” Journal of the Radio Research Laboratories, Vol.2 No.7, Jan.1955, pp.51–58.** [Original NICT journal PDF](https://www.nict.go.jp/publication/journal/02/007/Journal_Vol02_No007_pp051-058.pdf). Read pp.53–58 (web PDF lines 119–179 and figures 7–10). At 1.85, 2.5, and 4MHz, measurements across land/sea found field “fill-in” into sea; Millington fitted better than one-way methods in some cases, while measured values sometimes lay below Millington and a theoretically developed Furutsu approach agreed better for specific courses. A different course after sea showed sharp sea-to-land field drop. The study’s authors call for more paths and theory; this is conditional route evidence, not a universal model result.
9. **Adams et al., NTIA TR-84-151, “Measurements and Predictions of HF Ground Wave Radio Propagation Over Irregular, Inhomogeneous Terrain,” July 1984.** [NTIA report landing page](https://www.its.ntia.gov/publications/details?pub=2158), [full PDF/OCR](https://www.its.ntia.gov/publications/download/84-151_ocr.pdf). Read pp.1–4, 14–15, 61–86, 89–90, especially the measured median/decile tables, prediction comparisons and conclusions (web PDF lines 533–571; 2803–2898; 2926–3100; 3101–3155). Four daylight paths, tests at 5.25/7.5/11.5/28MHz to45km. Receiver used active and passive monopoles atop a moving van; transmitter used a vertical monopole over an elongated ground screen. Absolute transmitter/receiver systems were calibrated, including receiver antenna-range pattern measurements and known-field calibration (p.3); wave-tilt ground constants were measured at transmitter and modeled soil values reported. 28MHz estimates σ=0.005–0.015 S/m, εr=10–30. Path medians differed by roughly 20dB across routes; smooth vs profile WAGNER predictions differed up to15dB at28MHz; one model case missed measured median by up to25dB; the WAGNER residual is path-specific, not universal accuracy. Skywave contribution was not directly determined (p.70). Daylight was chosen to make groundwave dominate, but exact skywave share and transfer to different antennas/terrain remain limitations. This is strong 28MHz site evidence against a frequency-only LOS rule, not exact30MHz or 1MHz validation.
10. **Fockens, Vogt-Ardatjew & Leferink, “Propagation Measurements and Analysis on MF and HF Bands in Urban Areas in The Netherlands,” IEEE Trans. EMC 64(3), 650–660, published 2022-06-01, DOI 10.1109/TEMC.2021.3137237.** [University of Twente record and abstract](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/propagation-measurements-and-analysis-on-mf-and-hf-bands-in-urban/). Primary paper/abstract reports measurements at 16 residential sites from1.8–28MHz and higher loss than ITU groundwave prediction throughout the measured band, with roll-off increasing in dB per decade with frequency. On 2026-11-14, opening the record’s final-published-version link targeted [the repository PDF](https://research.utwente.nl/files/300194531/Propagation_Measurements.pdf) and returned **403 Forbidden** (no article body). Therefore setup, exact frequencies, distributions and numerical residuals are not verified and are not used for quantification.
11. **Hehenkamp, Rizzi, Grundhöfer & Gewies, “Prediction of Ground Wave Propagation Delay for MF R-Mode,” Sensors 24(1):282, version of record 2024-01-03.** [Full open article](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/24/1/282), [DLR manuscript copy](https://elib.dlr.de/201747/1/sensors-24-00282-1.pdf). Read §2, §3 and §4 (MDPI web lines 194–203, 295–303, 353–445). Method treats attenuation as complex amplitude+phase; modifies C++ LFMF to calculate complex attenuation and adds variable-surface-impedance integral equation for mixed path. Its 2020 four-day Baltic ship campaign reports improved range/position after phase correction generally, but instances of overcompensation due ground conductivity/salinity map errors; evening skywave creates strong range distortion. Strong support for separating phase delay, map error and skywave, but experiment is MF R-Mode at283.5–325kHz; no numerical transfer to1MHz is claimed.
12. **Valčić, Škrobonja, Žuškin & Brčić, “Anomalies in NAVTEX Signal Reception in the Northern Adriatic: Discrepancies between Measurements and Propagation Models,” Naše More 73(1), 12 February 2026.** [Full open article and metadata](https://hrcak.srce.hr/index.php/en/clanak/499098). Preliminary communication; read article methods/comparisons (web lines 316–338, 343–416, 535–578) and title/date (lines 37–60, 106–139). At490kHz a shore station observed daytime failures from Split/Piombino but reliable Mondolfo reception; model settings were partly assumed (unknown receive gain set to 0dBi; rough ground constants/antenna inputs), making apparent prediction-vs-reception disagreement confounded. Their uniform model vs mixed-path calculations changed attenuation by ~10–30dB. Their MoM+SSPE/FEMPE estimates were more optimistic given coarse terrain approximations. Treat as site-specific warning about missing local inputs and receiver/station chain, not as evidence P.368 universally fails or an exact 1MHz measurement.
13. **ITU-R P.1147-4, “Prediction of sky-wave field strength at frequencies between about 150 and 1 700 kHz,” approved 2007-08-31; official current page marks it in force.** [Official PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.1147-4-200708-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf). Read §§1–3, Annex 1 (web PDF lines 2–70, 229–287, 392–417). Predicts night-time field for one/more vertical transmit antennas with a ground-level loop Rx aligned along the great circle; 50–12,000km; annual median half-hourly median field and day-to-day variation, with Annex 2 daytime discussion. It is a statistical broadcast/planning method and is not a channel impulse response or minute-by-minute skywave power forecast. Useful separate skywave scenario evidence near1MHz; not applicable at30MHz.
14. **ITU-R P.832-4, “World Atlas of Ground Conductivities,” approved 2015-07; current page marks it in force.** [Official PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.832-4-201507-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf) and [official current page](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.832/en). §3.2 explicitly says the MF conductivity maps (Figs.7–42) are standardized to 1MHz; they contain no seasonal variation. The maps give effective conductivity, not permittivity. Therefore they are in-band at the 1MHz center and can seed a regional σ estimate; do not describe them as “below 1MHz only.” They do not directly specify a site's ε or a 30MHz property's frequency variation. §3.1's VLF maps, by contrast, are limited to 30kHz, are not season-adjusted, and include terrain in the effective values. Search/PDF access on 2026-11-14 confirmed this; web search result excerpt quoted the governing language and map scope.
15. **NTIA/ITS LFMF v10 C++ reference implementation for P.368-10.** [Current official ITU software-product table](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/study-groups/rsg3/rwp3m/Pages/digprod.aspx) lines 224–248 identifies the P.368 LFMF-SmoothEarth C++ implementation as integral/authoritative version 10. [NTIA source repository](https://github.com/NTIA/LFMF), pinned source revision [`57886e9d0a29fd8f04a6416dc3f782322f5a3589`](https://github.com/NTIA/LFMF/tree/57886e9d0a29fd8f04a6416dc3f782322f5a3589), model source `src/LFMF.cpp` and interface `include/LFMF.h`. Reproducible code acquisition provenance, Git blob hashes, byte sizes, compile and run record are in [acquisition-log.md](acquisition-log.md), with wrapper/output under [`tmp/LFMF`](tmp/LFMF). The public source's project version is 1.1; the ITU product is version 10. Inputs include tx/rx heights, MHz, watts, Ns, km, relative permittivity, conductivity, polarization; returns basic transmission loss, E(dBµV/m), receive power dBm, and method (flat-Earth curve or residue series). The source uses 4.77dBi reference TX/RX antenna gains. Numerical results below are official-library outputs for declared conditional inputs, not measured sites and not a separate flat-Earth approximation.

16. **Nishioka, Fukuda, Furukawa & Hata, “Comparison of measured and theoretically predicted results of ground-wave propagation characteristics at MF band,” IEICE Communications Express 3(5), 182–186 (2014), DOI 10.1587/comex.3.182.** [J-STAGE full PDF](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/comex/3/5/3_182/_pdf). Read §§2–4 and Figs.2–3. The 1.242MHz vertical AM carrier route is 19km to coast and 90km to Oshima. Calculation uses land εr=15/σ=1mS/m, sea εr=70/σ=5S/m, zero heights and standard atmosphere. Rx monopole gain/incident-E conversion were unknown, so theory was fitted to the measured sea section. Paper reports deep urban/mountain land fading and agreement of relative envelope/recovery, but no tabulated numeric trace; screenshot returned no image, so no points were digitized. Accessed 2026-11-14.
17. **Aono & Muramatsu, “Measurement of Field Intensity of Ground Wave over Mixed Paths,” Journal of the Radio Research Laboratories 2(7), 51–58 (1955).** [NICT original PDF](https://www.nict.go.jp/publication/journal/02/007/Journal_Vol02_No007_pp051-058.pdf). Read Table 1, §§2–3.3 and Figs.4–10. Adjacent 1.85MHz Oshima Loran courses at160kW, impulse-separated surface component, calibrated receiver, 1.5m loop ~50m from vehicle and ~4h midday measurement. The IV/V routes begin with ~2.5km land then sea; predicted/observed values agree at the landing, but post-sea land measurements are mostly below three mixed-path methods, and the sharp sea-to-land drop is unexplained. Separate 2.5/4MHz route III shows sea fill-in; Millington closest of simple methods but higher than measurements, Furutsu closer; its shallow-sea endpoint showed no tide effect. Conditions were chosen to suppress skywave. OCR text provides no tabulated field samples, and historical figures are not calibrated modern complex response. Accessed 2026-11-14.
18. **Bourlier & Kubické, “Numerical Study on the Propagation of the Surface Waves Above a Rough Sea for Mixed Paths,” IEEE TGRS 49(4) (2011), DOI 10.1109/TGRS.2010.2077302.** [Author-hosted full PDF](https://christophe-bourlier.fr/Articles/2011_TGRS_49_4.pdf); [DOI landing page](https://doi.org/10.1109/TGRS.2010.2077302). Read §§IV.B–C and conclusion (author-hosted PDF pp.1378–1381). Rough Gaussian sea in the 10–30MHz results, ten-surface complex-field ensemble, flat-Earth/near-surface limits; ~1dB difference is analytic roughness approximation vs own numerical rough reference. At `u10=10m/s`, RMS height0.63m, `σz/λ=0.021–0.063` at10–30MHz. Wind-driven attenuation difference reaches5dB at800km. Separate rough-sea/island mixed-path `ΔF3` vs homogeneous-sea baseline examples are −5.2/−1.8/−0.2dB at1000km for first-sea lengths20/50/100km; those are not a roughness-only loss. Model output, not sea-trial evidence or short30MHz validation, and not transferable to1MHz. Accessed 2026-11-14.
19. **Halverson et al., “Influence of Environmental Parameters on the Range and Availability of HF Radar Sea Echo,” Journal of Atmospheric and Oceanic Technology 34 (2017), 447–462.** [CODAR-hosted full PDF](https://www.codar.com/images/about/2017_Halverson_JAOT.pdf). Read §§2–4. Its 25MHz, 1.6-year coastal HF-radar endpoint is Bragg-echo working range: 19.4km at0.9S/m versus37.4km at3.5S/m, plus sea-state/tide effects. This endpoint includes two-way scatter/cross-section and processing availability; it is not one-way attenuation or a calibrated channel. Accessed 2026-11-14.
20. **Kirincich & Emery, “Revisiting HF Ground Wave Propagation Losses Over the Ocean: A Comparison of Long-Term Observations and Models,” Radio Science 58(4) (2023), DOI 10.1029/2022RS007550.** [Wiley full text](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2022RS007550). Read §§2–4, Fig.8 and Table1. Eight-month 16.188MHz one-way relative-power records at7km/45km, distinct receivers and unknown Tx/Rx gains. 50% conductivity change did not produce the expected modeled seasonal sign/magnitude; synoptic 2–10day timescales dominated, high-pass SD0.8/1.3dB, and multivariable regressions explain r²0.53/0.55. This is measured temporal variability, not absolute path loss or an exact30MHz transfer. Accessed 2026-11-14.
21. **ITU-R P.1812-8, approved 2025-09; current P.1812 page marks it in force.** [Official current page](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.1812/en). Accessed 2026-11-14; the page gives 30MHz–6GHz scope, current revision and a P.1812-7 note that its inherited troposcatter equation had validation for paths >66km and angles >2.6mrad eq.; shorter/smaller-angle cases may incur significant loss error. It is a point-to-area planning method, not a complex impulse response.
22. **ITU-R P.534-6, “Method for calculating sporadic-E field strength,” approved 2021-09; current page marks it in force.** [Official current page](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.534/en). Accessed 2026-11-14. This is a separate sporadic-E field-strength method for an intermittent 30MHz skywave mechanism, not a persistent propagation floor or exact time series.
23. **ITU-R P.372-17, “Radio noise,” 2024-08 predecessor edition.** [Official PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.372-17-202408-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf). Source details and category arithmetic were reviewed in coordination with the noise research branch; §6/Table 1 defines the man-made curves and external-noise reference convention. Use the numbers only as predecessor conditional examples; P.372-18 is current and no continuity with those exact values is asserted.

## Search scope, failure and exclusions

Queries were run in metered batches covering: official P.368/P.527/P.452/P.526 revisions; MF soil conductivity measurements; 30MHz/HF groundwave measured path loss, terrain, P.368/GRWAVE, and Millington mixed-path tests; current P.368 software; 1MHz and 30MHz contemporary measurement papers; mixed-path phase/delay; groundwave urban measurements; current P.1812/P.1546 scope. Followed the official ITU and NTIA primary-source trails, the older BBC/NICT field trails, and modern R-Mode study references. The newest usable contrary urban result (Fockens 2022) was only abstract-accessible because the full paper returned 403. Search also surfaced unrelated mmWave paths and 2026 quarry/subsurface observations; excluded from design claims because those mechanisms/geometries are not the 1MHz/30MHz surface path requested. No evidence of universal 30MHz “LOS-only” behavior was inferred from a search result or community source.

## Quantitative check references and output

* P.368 normalization formula and reference field: P.368-10 pp.2–4, ledger item 1. P.832 correction/scope: ledger item 14, §3.2.
* Power-to-IQ amplitude relation: for a fixed impedance and complex-voltage sample, amplitude is the square root of power ratio; the script checks a 20dB illustrative loss against the standard dB definitions. It does not claim a calibrated receiver transfer.
* Complex conductivity/permittivity: P.527-6 eq. (1a), pp.2–4; moisture/temp notes pp.5–6; ledger item 2.
* Earth/fresnel geometry: P.526-16 §§1–2; ledger item 3. Horizon numbers use the conventional 4/3 Earth radius factor; they are geometry-only and are **not** sourced as a P.368 groundwave prediction.

* Conditional C/N0 arithmetic uses P.372-17 predecessor category curves (ledger item23), the LFMF output's 4.77dBi terminal gain and an explicitly assumed NF=5dB. It is a transparent same-reference calculation, not physical receiver evidence.
