## Calculation artifact

`hf_calcs.py` is a standard-library script with no external downloads or data. `hf_calcs.txt` is its actual stdout. It calculates ideal Gaussian width/correlation conversions and free-space delay-to-excess-path conversion. `hf_observation_check.py` / `.txt` independently recompute path-length differences from Dear et al.'s published relative CIR delays and the number of nominal 15-min slots in seven days, documenting why that article's availability fraction cannot be computed. `gillies_absorption_check.py` / `.txt` does arithmetic on Gillies et al.'s published aggregate loss summaries: 80–90% minus the pooled 62% baseline is 18–28 percentage points; relative to 38% pooled baseline reception this is 47.4–73.7% of remaining reception converted to loss. Its identity (p(A,f)=p_{baseline,f}+q_f(1-p_{baseline,f})) shows why the normalized q cannot be read as absolute loss without each frequency's baseline. A hypothetical 0/5/10/20% of scheduled opportunities actually offline gives 62.0/60.0/57.8/52.5% loss among operating opportunities under the explicit mixture assumption; these are scenarios, not observed logs or source-count reconstructions. Schedule rates imply 96/192/576 probes per carrier per 24 hours at 4/8/24 per hour. These bounded calculations are neither raw-data analysis nor product check.

`tep_geometry_check.py` / `.txt` recomputes spherical great-circle distance and initial bearing from Maruyama & Kawamura's published endpoint coordinates. With R=6371 km it returns 8,076.2 km and 3.97° east of due south, consistent with their approximate 8,000 km and 4.3°. It only checks terminal geometry; it does not infer the ionospheric reflection point, hops, or channel response.

## Access gaps and contradictions preserved

- The original 1969 Watterson report OCR is now accessible via supported browser retrieval (transcript and locator map above), so selected primary tables were transcribed and reconstructed. The distinct 1970 IEEE full article remains uninspected; the 1969 report tables, not abstract snippets, carry the ordinary-midlatitude baseline here. Selected intervals do not establish geomagnetic-quiet classification, general frequency/season occurrence, modem availability or an unconditioned long-duration distribution.
- Full numerical tables in Basler et al. 1988 were not exposed; only the primary abstract was usable, so the reported “hundreds of microseconds / tens of hertz” stays explicitly abstract-level.
- P.531-16 was identified as current official ITU source but not opened beyond official current page/contents; it is used only to keep trans-ionospheric effects separate from HF skywave.
- High-latitude paper observation bins offer conditional P95s with mismatched measurement/threshold definitions. They do not establish a joint extreme delay-Doppler occurrence. F.1487 occurrence statement also lists marginal thresholds. The controlled stress product and the observed conditional path data must stay separate.
- Ordinary-midlatitude evidence is now anchored by Watterson et al.’s three selected near-stationary 10–13-minute modal tables on a 1,294-km 5.864/9.259-MHz midlatitude circuit. This is direct measured baseline evidence, but not an annual/modern climatology: intervals were selected for stationarity, only twelve were identified and three analyzed in detail, and no geomagnetic-index or service-outage denominator is given. Warrington & Stocker (2003) supplies a separate full-year 4–18-MHz trough-conditioned population, not a quiet-midlatitude substitute. Walden’s 23-month ordinary UK NVIS study measures seasonal received level, not CIR delay/Doppler. The supplied 2011 Stocker/Warrington Figshare URL was inaccessible in this metered run; no data from it are claimed.
- The O/X communication measurement is a 12-day single-frequency, 97-km NVIS campaign with a per-frame detection denominator; it is not a multi-region O/X population. It reports conditional delay and relative Eb/N0/BER, but does not expose calibrated absolute mode powers or a usable ionospheric Doppler sample because SDR clock instability dominated.
- Maruyama & Kawamura provide genuine cross-equator RF path/arrival direction observations, but not a TEP delay-power-response ensemble. Their inferred drift uses the assumed geographic-equator point; a magnetic-equator alternative changes the value, and the authors request a fixed-frequency dedicated transmitter plus simultaneous observations near conjugate endpoints.
- Primary measured channel evidence is available for quiet equatorial NVIS (Dear et al.) and disturbed high-latitude long-haul (Nissen & Bello), but their different sounders, delay/Doppler definitions, sample selection and geometries prohibit pooling them as one distribution. Dear's raw-data links were inaccessible and its article gives no success/missing slot counts. Gillies et al. add schedule-based expected/decoded probes on a different operational circuit, with inferred continuous endpoint operation rather than audited uptime; it is not a target-modem denominator. Use published CIR metrics and link reception rates only under their stated conditional definitions. Known-source calibrated power captures and executed/missing target-modem slots plus endpoint-up records remain needed for outage calibration.
- Walden's 23-month midlatitude network contributes received-power seasonal variation but no CIR or availability denominator in the accessible article. Supporting tables were inaccessible; keep this link-level evidence separate from channel-spread sounders.
- P.1147 corrects the earlier shorthand that there was no useful MF method near 1 MHz: 150–1700 kHz skywave field-strength prediction is within its scope. It is not a waveform simulator, and there is a 1.7–2.0 MHz method gap before P.533's 2-MHz lower limit.
- Project source code's chosen `HF Worst` knob combination is a deliberate stress profile. Agreement with some F.1487 numerical values does not prove equivalence to its Gaussian two-tap model or establish that nature produces the tuple at a known frequency.
