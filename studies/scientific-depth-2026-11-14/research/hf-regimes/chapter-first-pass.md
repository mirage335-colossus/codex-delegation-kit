## Physical link and regime map

### Geometry, modes and received level

The 1–30 MHz range crosses changing propagation regimes. Skywave follows a refracted path in the ionosphere rather than a literal specular bounce at a fixed shell. Electron-density profile, incident frequency/elevation, geomagnetic field and absorption determine whether ordinary/extraordinary magnetoionic modes refract/return or escape. One-hop and multihop E/F-region modes may coexist; the ground reflection, antenna launch/receive patterns, polarization response, ground conductivity and path geometry change path loss and relative mode power. Low-angle long-distance links and near-vertical incidence have different mode sets and delays. At the lower edge (about 1–2 MHz), groundwave/skywave transitions, D-region attenuation and ionospheric support make extrapolating the P.533 2–30 MHz method particularly inappropriate.

ITU-R P.533 is a *circuit prediction method*: monthly medians and specified time percentages for usable frequency, field strength/receiver power, noise and reliability, with antenna performance as an explicit practical input. For digital modes, the recommendation's simplified service test uses the receiver's time window `T_w`, frequency window `F_w`, required S/N, and amplitude ratio of dominant/subdominant paths. This is valuable because it treats the level of an out-of-window mode as important; delay spread alone is not enough. Its median/monthly outputs are not a synthetic time-series channel, don't resolve a particular waveform's fades, and are not field measurements. See P.533-14 §§ 1–10, especially §§ 5–10.2 (2019 edition; official URL in source ledger).

A useful waveform-level representation is a time-varying impulse response

`r(t) = Σ_i g_i(t) exp(j 2π ν_i t) s(t − τ_i(t)) + n(t) + i(t)`,

with actual normalization and phase conventions declared. The components may represent discrete refracted modes, scattered diffuse power, O/X subcomponents, groundwave, or other paths. Delay `τ_i` is an envelope/group delay relative to a chosen emission/reference plane; Doppler `ν_i` is the rate of phase change relative to source and receiver frequency references. A changing propagation path changes phase at the carrier and can therefore produce Doppler even for slow group-delay change. For a physical path length `R(t)`, a first-order geometric term is `ν ≈ −f_c Ṙ/c`; the sign depends on convention. In a dispersive magnetized plasma, phase/group index and O/X modes add terms beyond simple geometric motion. A changing measured delay is not itself a Doppler measurement, and an observed carrier offset also contains independent TX/RX oscillator error.

The output signal is not generally a stationary random process over an entire link session. A channel may be locally stationary over a short coherent block yet change its mode set, attenuation, scatter distribution, center shift and power over minutes, local-time transitions, storms or solar flares. “Slow fading,” “fast fading,” “Doppler spread,” and “bulk frequency offset” must therefore be separate declared measurements/parameters.

### Quiet and changing midlatitude

In quiet or ordinary midlatitude cases the principal channel may be a small number of dominant modes with stable delay/power over short observation windows and lower Doppler spread. This is a defensible starting point for public F.1487/Watterson Gaussian-tap testing and for receiver algorithm verification. It is not a universal one-ray channel: interference between modes causes deep frequency-selective nulls, and mode availability changes with frequency, local time and season. Midlatitude time/delay tails and rare scatter events remain possible.

Changes on useful engineering timescales include changing layer heights/virtual path length, solar day/night transitions, mode appearing/disappearing near frequency-availability boundaries, multipath interference, ionospheric irregularities, travelling disturbances, and gradual oscillator/path motion. A stationary Watterson model averages a stable statistical interval; a model with piecewise-changing state or a measured replay is more appropriate for change detection, reacquisition and session-level persistence.

### Equatorial and low latitude

Equatorial plasma bubbles / spread-F, E/F scattering, and unusual low-latitude geomagnetic conditions create scattering and reflected modes whose delay, Doppler and direction can be diffuse or structured. ITU-R P.533-14 explicitly contains an equatorial-scattering addendum: a half-normal excess-delay power distribution with 1-ms standard deviation, symmetric frequency-scatter power distribution with 3-Hz standard deviation, and occurrence probability conditioned on magnetic dip latitude, control-point local time, smoothed sunspot number and season. The procedure gates the signal's scattered levels against the circuit's time/frequency window and an expected mode-amplitude margin; it is not a claim that every equatorial link simultaneously has 1 ms and 3 Hz or that all equatorial mechanisms obey those distributions.

Primary recent observation: Marew et al. used three-transmitter continuous Doppler sounders at 4.63 and 6.80 MHz in Tucumán, Argentina, and contemporaneous airglow to analyze six 2022–23 ESF events. They report eastward ESF drift, typically about 130 m/s after sunset versus about 80 m/s around/post-midnight, with one event where 4.63 and 6.80 MHz estimates differed (possibly wind shear). This constrains moving-structure context, not a communication modem's delay-spread tail. Basler et al.'s earlier oblique sounding across polar-cap, auroral and equatorial regions found one-hop F-path distortions on the order of hundreds of microseconds in propagation time and tens of hertz in Doppler; its 20-kHz PN sounding/calculated scattering-function methodology is relevant but not a universal event distribution. The old quantitative P.533 addendum and these actual but limited observations are complementary, not interchangeable.

### High latitude, auroral and polar cap

At northerly latitudes, nonuniform ionosphere and moving irregularities can produce off-great-circle arrival, significant angular spread, multiple delayed components and a broad/non-Gaussian Doppler distribution. Auroral convection, patches, arcs, poleward trough walls and polar cap structures are distinct physical configurations. One dominant on-great-circle mode may coexist with weak delayed scattered modes; a small component can matter when it falls outside the receiver's coherent delay/frequency window. Directional spread can defeat small-number specular-array assumptions even if a single scalar receiver can still fit an effective channel.

Stocker et al. (2013) distinguish a subauroral trough path from a polar-cap path and show conditional seasonal/frequency behavior over a solar-cycle segment. On the measured QN polar-cap path, 95th percentile Doppler spread was about 1–13 Hz by frequency/season bin, while composite multipath spread was about 0.1–1.5 ms. The high Doppler at 14.36 MHz winter-day had 0.3-ms P95 composite delay; lower frequency bins could have larger delays. Some high-spread samples came from very few days. The study also reports occasional values >20 Hz and relates delay/shift excursions to convecting patches. For the NB subauroral path, P95 Doppler spreads were no more than 2 Hz while composite delays could exceed 4 ms. These examples falsify “delay and Doppler grow together as a single severity scalar.” They are samples from particular paths, not a global climatology or simultaneous worst-case guarantee.

Warrington et al. (2006) measured two northerly paths with multi-element arrays and found mode energy distributed over azimuth/elevation; a scalar tapped-delay model is inadequate if antenna-array spatial processing is being assessed. Their conclusion also emphasizes that available angular statistics were not enough to specify a universal scattering function. For a one-channel scalar receiver, defer angle-of-arrival implementation unless measured spatial/polarization inputs or antenna processing will be tested.

### Disturbance, storm, absorption, and outage

“Geomagnetic storm” is not one channel law. Positive ionospheric storm effects can raise F-region critical frequency / usable frequency; negative effects can reduce MUF and persist after onset. Auroral electron precipitation increases D-region electron density/absorption and causes smaller/local MLT-structured events. Solar proton events increase polar D-region ionization over large regions and can last days (PCA). Solar flares' X-ray/EUV cause prompt lower-ionosphere/day-side shortwave fadeout (SWF), constrained to paths whose ionospheric path interacts with sunlit atmosphere. Storm-time irregularities/patches also change paths, shift, spread and directional structure. Their coincidence with absorption varies by event and geometry.

Bland et al. (2018) examined two September 2017 solar-proton events with SuperDARN routine noise and backscatter measurements. They observed near-total polar-radar backscatter loss for several days; attenuation reached 10 dB (north) / 14 dB (south) at 12 MHz. M-/X-class flares separately produced 2–4-h midlatitude attenuation enhancements. The measurement is radar/environment evidence; it establishes an observed absorption/outage case, not the probability of failure for a specific modem. Ghaly et al. (2024) show the value of measuring narrowband known terrestrial skywave signals simultaneously with CNA: a riometer is a relative cosmic-noise opacity meter, not a direct calibration of every oblique communication ray. Their review of literature reports D-region enhancement footprints ranging from tens to hundreds of km and event-to-event variability. A point gain-loss curve may stress a receiver but should not be claimed to represent the spatial intersection of an actual circuit without path-specific observations.

Outage should be represented in at least two distinct ways: (a) a multipath fade/null with energy still present in a recoverable/in-band channel; (b) a sustained path-loss/availability interval where all useful modes are below the demodulation threshold or no mode propagates. The former tests tracking/interleaving/diversity; the latter tests fallback, outage declaration, reacquisition and robust service accounting. A “no signal” simulation should retain noise/interference, advance channel time state and expose exactly when path loss crosses a receiver threshold. Do not create event incidence by combining independent stress knobs unless data supports their dependence.

### Polarization, amplitude and noise

The ionosphere is magnetized and dispersive. Its ordinary/extraordinary modes may differ in index, refraction, delay, phase/Doppler, attenuation and polarization. Faraday rotation depends on electron content and geomagnetic field; it may rotate a linearly polarized field, create an elliptical state, or alter received power for a single linearly polarized antenna. The F.1487 tap spectrum can include two separated magnetoionic components. ITU-R P.531 (trans-ionospheric scope, primarily satellite paths) describes TEC group delay/phase advance, Faraday rotation and scintillation, and is useful as physics context only: its transionospheric TEC/scintillation laws should not be inserted unmodified as a reflected 1–30 MHz circuit model. A receiver with one complex sample stream has already collapsed polarization through its antenna/front end; it cannot claim to simulate vector polarization diversity. Add a Jones/vector channel only if a dual-polarized front end and an observable diversity decision require it.

Amplitude laws are conditional. A single dominant ray with weak perturbations is not Rayleigh fading; a zero-mean proper complex Gaussian sum of many weak independent components yields Rayleigh envelope/Rician if a deterministic component is present. Watterson's model explicitly adopts stationary Gaussian tap gains as a useful measured approximation in its stated bandwidth/time conditions. Coherent discrete two-mode interference can give a non-Rayleigh distribution and regular frequency nulls. Severe high-latitude diffuse and time-varying angular structure can depart from stationarity and simple Gaussian assumptions. Report empirical CDF, fade depth/duration, level crossing rate, autocorrelation and outage fraction conditional on source SNR/threshold, with calibration floor and censoring. Do not infer amplitude law from Doppler-spread label alone.

HF noise is often external/environmental: lightning atmospheric noise, man-made receiver-site noise and other emitters; it varies by frequency, geography, season, local time, antenna and bandwidth. Interference may be an identifiable narrowband/structured signal or aggregate occupancy, not AWGN. ITU-R P.372 gives external noise figures / temperature distributions at a stated reference antenna; it excludes receiver internal noise and interference attributed to known services, and its received level depends on antenna/site and feeder. Keep received signal power, external noise density, receiver noise figure, occupied interference and receiver-input calibration in consistent planes/units. The companion noise branch owns detailed noise recommendations; this context is included because absorption and fading cannot be converted to SNR without it.

## Model equivalences and failure conditions

### P.533 link calculation vs physical channel

P.533 supplies frequency support, median field strength/power, path-mode contributions, noise and reliability estimates for circuit-planning decisions. Its time-frequency-window logic provides a receiver-useful functional abstraction and can suggest which delayed/shifted modes matter. It does not supply a unique time-varying waveform, phase, coherent cross-mode covariance, complex polarization, sample-by-sample fading or ADC response. Use it to select and describe candidate operating strata; keep the native continuous waveform renderer as the receiver execution path.

### F.1487 / Watterson subset vs current two-ray and Gaussian laws

F.1487/Watterson uses a tap-delay line. Each path/mode has its own complex Gaussian tap gain (and hence Rayleigh envelope if zero-mean), a declared mean-power split/attenuation, center shift and spectral width. It can use two Gaussian magnetoionic spectra on a tap when O/X components differ; if delay separation is large, represent those as distinct taps. The model assumes stationary Gaussian scatter within a test interval and explains how to compare modems over delay, Doppler and S/N surfaces. F.1487 says its model is valid in 3-kHz channels and may apply up to 12 kHz, always asking that front-end filters and gain control be considered.

The current two-ray family is a useful degenerate deterministic/equal-power case: two delays, equal ray amplitudes, a fixed center separation and a phase producing coherent notches. It cannot reproduce configurable path-power profiles, three or more resolved modes, Gaussian per-ray PSD by center-separation alone, two-component O/X tap spectra, mode loss/arrival/departure, diffuse angular spread, or a stated occurrence distribution. Existing `gaussian_doppler_width_hz` / `random_doppler_width_hz` are closer to the Gaussian tap-gain part because they apply independent per-ray Gaussian processes with `B=2σ`; combined with delay and per-path gain, this can form a restricted two-tap Watterson-like realization. The equal underlying two-ray gain and fixed two-ray count remain material constraints; the existing APIs are not an arbitrary path table.

The relations must not be confused:

- F.1487's `B=2σ` is a PSD width around each path's center, with Gaussian `σ`; 2σ encloses 68.27% of that one-dimensional Gaussian power. Its FWHM is 1.1774B, and the central 80%-power width is 1.2816B.
- A center-frequency difference between two taps adds deterministic differential phase rotation; it is not either tap's PSD width. One center can move commonly, while relative centers move differently.
- A path's absolute observed center shift also contains TX/RX oscillator offset/drift and other reference errors. Preserve calibration/reference-plane records to distinguish them.
- F.1487 Annex 3's high-latitude 7-ms/30-Hz row is a controlled equal-power two-tap stress value pair; the separately given occurrence indication says 5-ms delay and 25-Hz frequency spread are individually exceeded about 5% of the time. It does not say both are exceeded together 5% of the time.

### Time-frequency stationarity and WSSUS

The WSSUS assumption (wide-sense stationary uncorrelated scattering) is a working local model: over a specified analysis interval the second-order statistics are time invariant, and distinct excess delays are uncorrelated. It supports a scattering function `S(τ,ν)`, power-delay profile and Doppler spectrum. It is useful for stationary simulator profiles and for communicating receiver tolerances, not an assertion about a multi-hour HF session. Coherent modes can be correlated; two magnetoionic components, common plasma motion, deterministic specular paths, common shadowing/absorption and slowly migrating modes violate uncorrelated independent taps. Rapid bursts, changing state, and correlated ionospheric structures violate stationarity. A local WSSUS approximation should state its window, carrier/frequency, coordinate and event context, and report residuals/tests of stationarity.

A Gaussian PSD for each complex tap makes a proper Gaussian fading process and Rayleigh magnitude in the zero-mean case. Gaussianity is a useful comparison law, not a worst-case bound. A strong specular tap gives Rician/nonzero-mean behavior; two coherent deterministic taps give frequency selective fading with potentially deep stable nulls; multi-ray sums with unequal powers and changing geometry are not adequately summarized by a Rayleigh law; mixtures over multiple regimes can have heavy-tailed/nonstationary statistics. If a measured normalized PSD is strongly asymmetric/multimodal, the center-and-width pair is too small a descriptor. Preserve a measured scattering surface or event replay only when trustworthy captures exist and the receiver test needs it.

### Occurrence probability vs stress settings

Three evidence layers must stay separate:

1. **Controlled coverage:** F.1487 modem test profiles vary delay, Doppler and S/N independently to map a performance surface; this is a reproducible sensitivity experiment.
2. **Conditional climatological prediction:** P.533-style percentage/occurrence outputs are conditional on defined circuit geography, frequency, date/time/season and model family. The predicted median is not an event trace or guaranteed bound.
3. **Observed realization:** calibrated field sounder or known-signal record with event, path, instrument and censoring provenance.

A severe stress test belongs to layer 1 unless it is explicitly selected by an observed profile or calibrated conditional model. It may ask “does this receiver survive this tuple?” but not “this is a 95th percentile worst case.” For 2-D extremes, retain simultaneous paired samples from one time/path measurement. If only marginal exceedances are available, Fréchet bounds are the only general joint bounds: `max(0, pA+pB−1) ≤ p(A∩B) ≤ min(pA,pB)`. For two 5%-exceedance marginals, their joint exceedance could be anywhere from 0 to 5% absent dependence data.

## Measurements, estimators, provenance

### Calibration and reference planes

Each observation must retain: transmitted probe/modulation waveform and exact emission timing; antenna and feed reference plane; RF/IF conversion and TX/RX frequency references; gain/AGC state; receiver bandwidth and filters; sample rate/resolution; dynamic range/clip state; noise and interference floor; path-endpoint coordinates and antenna patterns/polarization; UTC/local-time and geomagnetic coordinates; frequency, season, solar/geomagnetic/event indices; instrument/site IDs and calibration; processing version; and whether each component is observed, modeled, censored or below a threshold. A sounder output calibrated at its receiver input is not directly the received RF power at the antenna; account for antenna gain, feeder loss, impedance, preselector, LNA/noise figure and analog/digital reference-plane changes.

No observed measurement is expected from the engineering owner in this assignment, so the report does not claim calibration. Before applying a field gain/noise floor, the owner must supply actual receiver/front-end ranges or a measured capture with a declared plane. Conditional sensitivity calculations should cover a range without pretending it is calibrated.

### Sounding and estimator conventions

A synchronized wideband probe can estimate the complex channel impulse response versus time by matched filtering/correlation. A common approach forms a delay-Doppler scattering estimate `|H(τ,ν)|²` across a declared coherent/integration window. Pulse compression sidelobes, receiver noise, interferers, estimator windowing, delay grid, Doppler resolution, aliasing and dynamic range affect estimated weak taps. Report the estimator and resolution, not just a phrase such as “delay spread.”

The literature's “spread” values have incompatible conventions:

- F.1487 Annex 3: two-mode **differential delay**; frequency spread is 2σ for the Gaussian tap spectrum.
- Stocker et al. (2013): composite multipath spread (CMPS) is first detected mode leading edge to last detected mode falling edge, corrected for probe width; modes >12 dB below strongest integrated power are excluded. “Doppler spread” is the narrowest spectral interval containing 80% of received power after correcting for base noise. SNR >=6 dB in 3 kHz and manual interferer rejection are selection conditions; their time-of-flight and Doppler estimates were on two-/three-minute snapshots with 0.5-Hz Doppler resolution. This is not RMS delay, RMS Doppler, or F.1487 2σ.
- Any new delay RMS calculation must name the weighting `P(τ)` and support threshold: `τ_rms = sqrt(ΣP_i(τ_i−τ̄)^2/ΣP_i)`. An absolute “last tap” depends on threshold and calibration floor. The excess delay of the latest significant mode is more reproducible if threshold and probe response are stated.
- Any percentile/outage must report the analysis window, selection/conditioned SNR, missing/failed soundings, number of independent intervals and the unit of independent trial. Adjacent short windows during one storm are not independent event replications.

Doppler can be estimated as phase slope or spectral power distribution, but oscillator error must be estimated/calibrated separately (frequency reference, path/clock drift). A single trace cannot separate systematic oscillator offset from common ionospheric shift without references/controls. Record absolute center and path-relative spread independently. Phase unwrap or a PLL estimate is not the path's full Doppler PSD.

For a communication receiver, report received complex signal and power in a named input plane, noise PSD/variance and measurement bandwidth, SNR/C/N0 and estimator, interference spectra/occupancy, clipping/AGC, correlation and packet/bit outcomes. Fade statistics should include mean/median and percentiles, CDF, fade depth/time below the receiver's actual threshold, fade-duration distribution, and outage fraction with exact threshold/reference. A physically valid signal-loss test cannot use future whole-capture power normalization because it erases absolute attenuation.

### Public calculation (performed)

`hf_calcs.py` in this chapter directory is a stdlib-only reproducible desk check; actual stdout is retained in `hf_calcs.txt`. It applies the F.1487 Gaussian PSD definition `B=2σ` to an ideal proper-complex stationary Gaussian gain. For `S(ν)∝exp(−ν²/(2σ²))`, normalized autocorrelation is `R(Δt)=exp(−2π²σ²Δt²)`, so the e-fold correlation time is `√2/(πB)`. With `B=30 Hz`, `σ=15 Hz`, FWHM is 35.32 Hz, central 80%-power width is 38.45 Hz, and ideal correlation e-fold time is 15.0 ms. With `B=0.5 Hz`, it is 0.900 s. These are analytical transformations, not an observed ionosphere or exact finite-FIR native runtime check; actual kernels/finite interpolation slightly alter the ideal covariance and should be measured from generated values if calibration matters.

The same script calculates free-space excess path `c·Δτ`: 7 ms corresponds to 2,098.5 km of excess ray length and 0.5 ms to 149.9 km. This is a *difference in path length*, not ionospheric reflection altitude, vertical layer separation, or direct horizontal ground distance. It is an order-of-geometry check against treating millisecond path separation as a few hundred meters of height.

## Implementation and evaluation decisions

1. **Keep exact Off baseline and current synthetic stresses.** Their value is matched receiver behavior and continuous timing/state. Label source controls vs realized path report. Do not silently change existing public hypotheses, chronology, waveform/source calendar or full receiver path.
2. **Expose a narrow stationary mode table.** A first useful extension is 2–4 public fixed paths: `delay`, mean power/amplitude and phase convention, center offset, per-path `2σ` (optional single/two-Gaussian magnetoionic PSD), and a common/independent correlation selector. Normalize by explicit total/reference path power only when requested; do not normalize away missing modes. Use arbitrary independent seeds per physical process. Preserve finite tails and captured bandwidth from declared spectra. Reuse streaming delay-line, existing Gaussian gain process and source composition. Current APIs are two-ray; adding N taps must be bounded and budgeted, independent of message duration.
3. **Separate path loss/availability from fading.** Add configured link gain / path attenuation with a declared reference time/value and optional external event series only when the data is licensed/available and calibrated. Zero/no useful mode is a valid outcome, with noise still entering and state advancing. Distinguish common attenuation of all paths from loss of one path and from random coherent fade. Preserve power/reference-plane arithmetic through quantization/AGC.
4. **Use conditional profile names, not automatic universal frequency/latitude laws yet.** Provide `quiet/midlatitude`, `low-latitude-scatter`, `high-latitude-quiet`, `high-latitude-disturbed`, `absorption/PCA`, and `SWF` as *candidate cases* with complete references/parameter provenance. Keep regularistic stress dimensions orthogonal for sensitivity mapping. Only map a profile automatically from endpoints/time/solar indices when the provider's validated range and model output are available; no undocumented one-size numerical parameters.
5. **Profile combination should preserve causality and dependence.** Multiple physical factors can coexist, but only causal combinations should be represented: e.g., SPE+sunlit PCA, X-ray SWF+sunlit reflection paths, auroral precip+auroral absorption and local structured modes. Do not draw delay-tail, Doppler-spread, gain-loss and noise changes independently and call them one storm. If event-conditioned joint distribution is unknown, expose scenarios as named controlled combinations and avoid probabilistic claims.
6. **Receiver comparison should be paired and complete.** Against identical emitted bytes, seed roots, start/calendar, front-end settings, noise/interference and receiver run, compare Off, current two-ray, F.1487 restricted profile and a field replay if allowed. Report mode acquisition, SNR, estimates, outage time, packet completion and resource cost. Test channel estimation under wrong assumed covariance and mismatch. Include quiet/changed sequence, reacquisition/outage recovery, but separately label synthetic receiver outcomes from model fit/field validation.
7. **Escalate model complexity only on discriminating evidence.** Full magnetoionic/ray tracing is warranted if predicted mode path gains/delay/polarization must follow changing measured electron density; spatial/vector models if array, polarization or direction diversity is evaluated; non-WSSUS/event-specific replay if measured statistics reject stationarity; waveform-/front-end measurement if a link decision depends on dB-level performance. Favor a small, parameterized scalar table first, since it permits direct connection to F.1487 profiles and the production receiver with low operational complexity.

### Falsifiers and decision triggers

- A Gaussian-tap profile is insufficient if held-out calibrated soundings show multimodal/skewed spectra, persistent coherent modes, nonstationary PSD within the chosen block, or delay-dependent Doppler/power that changes outcomes relative to Gaussian model. First increase path/spectral descriptors; move to measured replay or physics model only if these explain the differences.
- A 2-path stress is insufficient if soundings resolve 3+ components above the declared floor and a three-path receiver response materially differs. Increase `N` only if the actual canonical workspace/CPU bound permits it.
- A scalar fading model is insufficient if simultaneous dual-pol measurements show polarization switching/nulls changing decode outcome and the intended hardware can receive independent polarizations. Add vector path only with those samples/hardware.
- Current high-latitude stress values are not a coverage guarantee; the standard's path-conditional percentile data refutes assuming one fixed distribution. Keep controlled extremes; gather local records and compare performance surface to observed tails.
- Any “storm profile” should be rejected if its event-conditioned absorption/path modes cannot be traced to the local source evidence, or if its event prediction claims exceed the measurement product's geography/frequency resolution.
