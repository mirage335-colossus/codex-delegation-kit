# 5.760 GHz EME: angular scattering, capture and native representation

Keep **a scalar reference, a bounded distributed synthetic stress, and calibrated IQ replay as distinct tools**. The scalar reference checks scheduling, frequency precompensation, absolute noise and front end. It cannot stand for lunar multipath. A compact joint delay–Doppler *power* model suffices to calculate cut-window losses. Static centroid taps do not in general retain the corresponding complex fading. Be cautious about promoting a particular compact waveform generator: use replay as the first evidence for receiver changes where coherent fading matters, and put any accepted compact propagation behind the existing native channel interface. A lunar facet renderer is an offline reference for geometry and pointing; its hot-path cost, lack of target-band phase inputs, and failures of much simpler centroid compression do not support a per-sample facet option in the complete receiver.

This chapter extends the existing [link budget and coupled-window checks](../eme/chapter-continuation.md); it does not replace them. [Source inspection, reproducibility and limits](source-ledger.md) accompany [the combined calculator](angular_eme.py), [executed output](angular_eme.out) and [a fine-grid repeat](angular_eme.fine.out). All new numbers are conditional calculations. No field capture, receiver qualification, runtime benchmark, product build or pumpModem change was made.

## Matched geometric and scattering alternatives

Hold the receive plane at the antenna terminals and TX power at the delivered antenna feed. Use the prior symmetrical co-located station classes, 37 W / 3 m and 70 W / 5 m, 0.55 aperture efficiency, 384,400 km range and nominal 0.25° disk radius. This retains the original synthetic 30 m/s radial, 1,022 m/s transverse center, with a two-leg retarded delay from the same point function. The leading reference returns at 2.552850334 s, with −1152.93 Hz center shift, before atmospheric excess path and station clocks. The retained `Zv` branch uses the prior 90 Hz / 1,118 km *predicted* gradient from Sun et al., scaled by 5.760/7.209125 and extrapolated linearly over the disk in `x`. This is a conditional stress axis, not a measured 5.760 GHz gradient. Pair exactly the same delays and beams with the previous orthogonal-spin two-leg residual as a more severe support control. Neither is an actual epoch; the prior synchronous control gave only about ±0.21 Hz, so this study does not set a general minimum or maximum Doppler allowance.

With incidence angle `i` between nominal surface normal and the look vector, `μ=cos(i)`, depth coordinate `u=1−μ` and projected radius `r/R=√(1−μ²)`, the spherical mean-surface element is `dA = R² du dφ`. Its projected element is `dA_proj=μ dA`. The prior plane-wave orthographic hemisphere is retained, using the exact retarded point times for propagation. At 5 m, the fine grid assigns only 0.023% of centered mixture power to the behind-horizon sliver `μ<R/D≈0.00452`. This checks the rim contribution only; actual incidence angles, apparent disk and baselines need topocentric geometry. Terrain, range-amplitude variation over the body, and exact finite-range beam shape are deliberately outside the matched comparison. All shapes are scaled to an illustrative unilluminated, opposite circular-sense (OC) full-disk cross section `σ_ref=0.06 πR²`, carried from the earlier conditional sensitivity, not a 5.760 GHz full-disk measurement.

[Sultan-Salem and Tyler, 2006](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1029/2005JE002489), §2 Eqs. (1)–(5) and Appendix B, define an area-normalized mean-square scattered-field RCS. The Hagfors backscatter law in the expected polarization has shape
`h_C(μ)=(C/2)[μ⁴+C(1−μ²)]^(−3/2)`, with a Fresnel **power** factor `ρ`. The supported diffuse alternative has `d_n(μ)=μ^n`; choosing equal incidence/scatter exponents of one in their bistatic diffuse description gives our monostatic `n=2` case. For linear TX the expected polarization is parallel; for circular TX smooth backscatter reverses the rotational sense, given the propagation convention. Its orthogonal partner (same circular sense, SC) has only a diffuse term in this formulation: a quasi-specular shape cannot tell how much power is in a phase-stable mean, nor determine cross-channel voltage correlations. Eqs. (1)–(2) distinguish `|E[E_s]|²` from `E[|E_s|²]`. [Evans, 1965](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19660006877/downloads/19660006877.pdf), Appendix III §V and Figs. 17–18, separates components and finds illustrative fitted `C=32` at 3.6 cm and `C≈102` at 68 cm after subtraction of stated diffuse terms. At the shorter wavelength the measured limb region was consistent with approximately uniform brightness, with poorer resolution/certainty at the limb. This motivates retaining a broad projected control and a steeply tapered diffuse control; it does not calibrate `C`, the cusp, or the tail for this station. The historical height-correlation exponential behind Hagfors is unrelated to the prior exponential in *lunar disk depth*.

Normalize each shape to unit integral under `dA/(πR²)=2 du dφ/(2π)` before multiplying by `σ_ref`. In particular, U is `s=μ` on surface area, yielding uniform projected power, E is `μ exp(−Ru/L)` with the prior dish-specific `L`, and D2 is `μ²`, with analytic integral `2/3`. H32 and H100 use `h_C`, then normalize; the numerical bare integrals for them are 1.02181 and 1.00806. Hfit fits just the prior centered 53.68 μs early fraction, using the equivalent depth cutoff (Hfit `C≈307` and `285` for the 3 m/5 m arms). H100+D20 combines the individually normalized H100 and D2 with .8 and .2 weights **before** beam weighting. These are integrated monostatic mixture powers, not the source's `K_e/K_o`, nor a hemispheric reflectance, power-conservation fit, physical roughness inference, or station parameters. The paper's Eq. (12) integrates over outgoing solid angle at one patch; our disk normalization integrates over patches at one look and does not repair the paper's finite-surface/shadowing/multiple-scattering caveats. The appendix's bistatic law would be needed in place of this monostatic form for materially different incident/outgoing angles.

The beam-weighted power is `P_i ∝ w_i B_T B_R`, where each `B` is one *power* gain relative to peak. Use `√P_i` for a fixed-magnitude phasor, or field coefficient variance `P_i` for a zero-mean independent proper-complex scatter cell. Using `P_i` as voltage or adding fields with the same unreferenced phase would give a different (incorrect) normalized power budget. Beam retention for centered 5 m U is 0.7344, reproducing −143.38 dBm at the earlier uniform reference. H100+D20 makes 0.8950, or −142.52 dBm, with the same unilluminated RCS. Renormalizing again after pointing would erase a real conditional signal loss.

## Pointing, noise and speckle are different observables

The approximate Gaussian power beam has HPBW 0.729° for the 5 m dish. Its main-beam solid angle `Ω_G=π θ_HPBW²/(4 ln 2)` and peak antenna gain give main fraction `η_MB=G_peak Ω_G/(4π)=0.731` for this model. The rest is a declared partition of cold sidelobes and ground spill; under fixed peak gain, redirect 0.10 of the beam's total normalized weight from cold sky to ground in a separate stress. This is a hypothetical receive pattern placement, not a design change that would automatically keep aperture gain constant after modifying the feed. Integrate a uniform Moon at 210 K replacing 5 K sky against the one-way RX beam alone. The off-Moon anchor uses receiver-equivalent 80.75 K, 5% ground at 290 K, rest at 5 K. This gives the earlier 100 K off-Moon sensitivity with no atmosphere. Using one unit-normalized Gaussian for *all* antenna directions (as in the earlier 158.5 K case) would ignore the new partition; the resulting higher Moon coupling is a different assumption, not a discrepancy with the same pattern.

In the equal Gaussian model the beam product is a Gaussian in the midpoint boresight, multiplied by a loss depending on beam separation. Opposed offsets keep the original normalized profile but lose total echo; a shared offset can pull the profile toward later/asymmetric echo. RX-only +0.30° versus both +0.15° makes the same normalized profile, but different power and noise. In the pure Hfit arm at shared +0.30°, C/N₀ is 31.37 dBHz and joint power is 87.3%, versus 31.34 dBHz and 68.1% for the mixture. A scalar C/N₀ or a Moon-noise Y factor is therefore not enough to infer what the receiver captures; see source ledger for the earlier beam/G/T and lunar-brightness evidence. Measure Moon-on/Moon-off at several boresights and elevations, a separately characterized ground pattern and stationary calibrator or known echo. Record gain state, receiver temperature, time and RF monitors; retain RFI and overload as different causes.

For the short-time speckle discriminator, assign each surface cell an independent proper-complex zero-mean scattering coefficient held fixed during the comparison and evolve its phase with the same `τ,ν`. The normalized **centered field** covariance is the weighted characteristic function of `ν Δt − τ Δf − x Δb/(λD)`, expressed in cycles; `Δb` is a receive-only baseline along the gradient and `x` is physical projected displacement. This far-field baseline term is the relative RX phase at fixed TX, with center phase removed and beams/powers held fixed. The illustrative 10/50 m baselines give |ρ|=.91/.083 for E, .76/.21 for Hfit, .51/.059 for the mixture, .037/.0014 for D2. Cross-frequency |ρ| at 150 Hz separation is .997, .879, .697, .338 respectively. Independent three-carrier fades would miss real correlation in this constructed family, as would one shared phase for the fully diffuse arm.

With Gaussian zero-mean fields, instantaneous intensity correlation is `|ρ|²`; the variance of an M-look mean retains all `2(M−k)` cross terms at lag `k`, not just M independent samples. For four looks at 5/25/100 ms spacing, CV for H100+D20 is .696/.509/.500; the equivalent independent counts are about 2.07/3.87/4.00. E at 5 ms has CV .914 and only 1.20 equivalent looks. A separate Rician sensitivity assigns 50% of total power to a **truly stable, phase-referenced mean** at the subradar point and adjusts the centered scatter power. H100+D20 then gives CV .669/.471/.433 and 1.67/3.39/4.00 equivalent looks relative to that arm's own one-look CV .866. In the latter case cross terms include `2 P_coh P_sc Re(ρ)`, so negative correlations can even give an equivalent-count value slightly above M. This is a separation of hypotheses, not a claim that lunar quasi-specular power is a 50% coherent mean. Published 70 cm lunar radar maps from [Campbell et al.](https://repository.si.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7cd1b960-bbee-4452-8714-abadd538bef0/content), pp. 4032/4038–39, use multiple independently focused looks to lower speckle power variance. Their processing/station/band does not give our look spacings. Do not draw a fresh surface for each carrier, read or slot; decorrelation should result from overlapping footprints, relative phase and motion or a fitted joint stochastic law.

Geometric scales explain why geometry alone cannot predict coherence: a smooth spherical reflection gives quadratic two-leg excess path `ΔL≈r²(1/R+1/(D−R))` and a half-wave radius of about 212 m; a planar screen with identical ranges instead gives about 3.16 km. A 13 mm radial relief/phase-center error gives about half a cycle at 5.760 GHz. None of these are the 1,737 km lunar radius, the antenna's illuminated footprint, the statistical surface correlation length, or a measured speckle cell; solar illumination or a narrower receive aperture changes weighting, not the velocity or phase geometry by definition. A coherent terrain calculation must resolve subfacet phase or explicitly model its residual covariance. An arbitrary global phase per replay is harmless to a receiver with fitted common phase; arbitrary delay/Doppler fields or changing per-cell phases each sample are not. A useful falsifier is repeated calibrated IQ with coherent TX and reference, measuring lagged field covariance on held-out intervals; compare long-look variance and carrier responses before/after derotation, on verified independent looks and, if useful, among simultaneously timed RX apertures. A common LO or gain drift can masquerade as lunar covariance; blanks and a separate reference distinguish them.

## A compatible, severe visible endpoint case

Both RF legs are present. Use the same hypothetical co-located 5 m / 70 W station, range, reference geometry and OC mixture. Change the *local atmospheric and equipment scenario* from 60° apparent, clear and centered to 10° apparent, same 1 km vertical wet slab on both legs (continuous over the 2.55 s round trip, with at least 5.67 km horizontal coverage plus beam-footprint margin in the slab), both boresights +0.30° along `x`, and RX ground fraction .15. This keeps the synthetic lunar velocity law fixed to isolate the loss budget, not as a joint forecast for two real lunar elevations. The telescope remains above an assumed 8° obstruction mask for the whole Moon. For separated TX/RX use weather and apparent visibility at **transmit** and **receive** epochs separately, a real bistatic surface and two beam centers. The [endpoint model](../eme-atmosphere/chapter.md) traces the approximately 0.04 dB zenith gas sensitivity to P.676-13, with P.838-3 at 100 mm/h and P.840-9 suspended cloud water of 0.20 g/m³ at 273.15 K. Use the co-located slab only as one physically possible severe combination, with distinct cloud droplets and rain drops. No timescale distribution, precipitation percentage, co-occurrence estimate, or universal worst case follows.

The wet scenario also specifies a hypothetical 75/25 receive projection into the expected and orthogonal circular channels, e.g. a separate feed/combiner configuration. It does not prescribe a rain-induced feed change. Under independent OC/SC voltages, the received power is `0.75 P_OC + 0.25 P_SC`; SC is a separate D2 shape at 0.20 unilluminated OC RCS. This is a feed/polarization response assumption, not the 7.209 GHz reported circular-polarization ratio, and not a second independent weather loss. Use the circular average in the precipitation law for both orthogonal modes and ignore rain depolarization in this case; changing Jones matrices require an explicit new measurement/model. The projected echo's actual per-cell mix is used before computing the joint rectangle, not applied as an independent capture percentage. Thermal Moon/sky is treated as unpolarized in one normalized RX mode; no signal mismatch multiplier is applied to noise.

Convert noise temperature to complex sample variance only after specifying the gain/reference scale and input filter's equivalent noise bandwidth; do not set ADC-level variance equal to `kT` or normalize from future whole-stream power.

In the wet stress, define absolute arrival as predicted subradar arrival plus 50 μs uncorrected offset and common +25 Hz after feed-forward wipeoff, then measure the same joint power with a public receiver window fixed at `[0,1 ms] × [−50,50] Hz`. 25 Hz corresponds to 0.00434 ppm at RF; it is not a sample-clock offset. One ppm of sample-clock difference separately accumulates 75 μs in 75 s. First-order refractivity gives about 18 versus 89 ns two-leg atmospheric excess; it cannot justify the 50 μs clock/calendar error. A wider ±112.5 Hz residual support at the same public origin recovers only about .646 of the mixture's total mean power in the wet 1 ms window. Do not use truth to recenter the receiver. When either endpoint's complete lunar patch is behind a 12° blocking skyline in the 10° scenario, model **physical signal absence** with recording/thermal and validity still present. Failure to receive valid samples is a separate mask. RFI or ADC blocking is another profile, not an implicit multiplier of the wet stress. The table's C/N₀ is an antenna-plane power statistic, and its captured variant is a diagnostic upper scale for that ideal window; no acquisition threshold, coding gain, BER or availability follows.

## Select the cheapest model that resolves the receiver question

We fitted per-cell integrated power and mean coordinates on the centered 5 m Zv grid. Against full quadrature, check the prior rectangles plus held-out `(0.4 ms, ±30 Hz)` and `(3 ms, ±75 Hz)`; check the **complex** characteristic function at 75/150 Hz, 5/20 ms, 100 ms/1 s and a joint 20 ms/150 Hz lag. Empty joint rectangles need no tap. These are checks on model agreement, with no project-mandated acceptance threshold. Use the step function to expose failure of static centroid summation, then a local Gaussian characteristic proxy using each cell's variance/cross moment. The proxy is only for covariance diagnostics: Gaussian extent outside a bin or physical support is not allowed to fabricate energy or delay in a synthetic stream.
