## Primary 7.209 GHz lunar echo measurement

**Sun et al., “Promoting Earth-Based Radar Astronomical Observations of the Moon,” Sensors 20(7), 1874 (2020), DOI 10.3390/s20071874.** Primary full text and figures: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7180880/>. Published 27 March 2020; study reports observations on 26 April 2019. The Sensors page/paper identifies the author list, date and title. The paper is openly accessible, including its figures; no raw-voltage data release was identified in the paper.

Useful full-text sections and evidence:

- §2.1–2.2, paper paragraphs around lines 149–180 in the PMC full-text rendering: 7,209.125 MHz CW LHCP transmission, 200 W; dual SC/OC circular reception at Kunming (40 m) and Zelenchukskaya (13.2 m); DE436 geometry; listed antenna and receiver parameters; their SNR convention `Pr/ΔPn` and `ΔPn=kT_r B/√(BΔτ)`; footprint estimates about 503 km and 1,118 km.
- §2.3, paragraphs around lines 181–186: 49-point DE436 radial-velocity model; predicted footprint Doppler spreads of 35 Hz (Km) and 90 Hz (Zv); *pre-observation* 10 s SNR predictions 167,000 and 185,000, using assumed OC radar albedo 0.07 and those predicted bandwidths. These are calculations/inputs, not observed results.
- §3, paragraphs around lines 191–208 and Fig. 1: an actual 60-minute detection at both stations; example 10 s spectra at 03:00:01–03:00:11 UT; spectral data evaluated with 0.5 s/0.2 s frames, giving 2 Hz/5 Hz frequency resolution; Fig. 1 spectrum widths are estimated at approximately 40 Hz/90 Hz; integrated SC+OC SNR reported as 177,000/203,000; peak offsets from the predicted CE3-position frequency of −15 Hz/−10 Hz. The paper does not state a spectral edge threshold, fitted width estimator or confidence interval for the approximate widths; treat them as observed-spectrum analysis widths, not a formally defined half-power/RMS bandwidth. It reports about 7 Hz standard deviation of the computed-minus-measured Doppler difference over the Km session (typical residual around 15 Hz). Do not conflate the 7 Hz session residual statistic with the modeled −13 to −16 kHz / −4 to −9 kHz hour-long Doppler sweeps, or make either a 5.8 GHz measurement.
- §3 polarization/local radar-property results: after measured-spectrum noise correction, local circular polarization ratio `μC=σSC/σOC` is 0.44 (Km) and 0.45 (Zv). Authors infer local OC albedo 0.06 for the Mare Imbrium footprint from the same session’s OC SNR (121,000/149,000), using observed widths 40/90 Hz, 10 s integration, and projected beam areas. This is a same-observation, model-assisted local estimate, not a globally calibrated lunar constant or cross-frequency calibration.

The numerical comparisons are not necessarily independent because the SNR/albedo inference and measured spectral widths share the same data and analysis conventions. The first-pass link-budget reproduction is in `link_budget.py`/`.out`; this continuation explicitly keeps the paper’s `Pr/ΔPn` statistic distinct from instantaneous `Pr/kTB` and channel `C/N`.

## Correct author identity and delay-distribution evidence

**T. B. A. Senior and K. M. Siegel, “A Theory of Radar Scattering by the Moon,” Journal of Research of the National Bureau of Standards D: Radio Propagation 64D(3), May–June 1960.** NASA-hosted scan of the original article: <https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20150020402/downloads/20150020402.pdf?attachment=true>. The original scan was accessible and its masthead inspected: first-page header/title/byline (PDF text around lines 0–8) names Senior and Siegel. This corrects the attribution “Evans & Hagfors” in the first-pass `chapter.md`; preserve that first pass and cite this continuation/ledger as its correction. Evans and Hagfors may be authors of a different lunar radar reference; they are not the authors of this 1960 title.

Relevant article text (original-scan PDF text line locators):

- Around lines 99–110: discussion of leading echo spikes and changing correlation as the illuminated region grows/deeper-return portions contribute.
- Around lines 111–115: the authors discuss a theoretical return that could persist for about 11.6 ms to the limb if scattering were distributed over the Moon; they cite observations that half the received power was within the first five miles of lunar depth, most distant echoes seen to about 100 miles, and a principal scattering radius roughly one third of the Moon. They state limb return can be 30–35 dB below the main scattering signal and is rarely detected.
- Around lines 117–135: long-pulse behavior/interference from multiple scattering regions; the paper cautions that observed peaks are not a simple one-to-one map of individual physical regions.

Use the 11.6 ms only as geometric support scale and the 50%-within-five-mile statement as a historical observed cumulative-power comparison reported by Senior and Siegel. The passage does not make this an observed 5.8 GHz delay profile; it does not identify a frequency for the cumulative-power observation in that discussion. Avoid promoting the historical remarks into a frequency-specific modern scattering law. The continuation’s idealized uniform-projected-area comparator is calculated independently in `eme_5760_sensitivity.py` and `eme_5760_sensitivity.out`.

## Conditional 5.760 GHz scale and antenna noise

**Operating evidence (not a calibrated link):**

- 432 and Above newsletter, December 2024, <https://eme.radio/432-and-above-newsletter/432-and-above-newsletter-2024-12>, relevant page paragraph around lines 510–514 reports YO8RHI operating at 5760 MHz with 37 W and a 3 m dish and working multiple stations. It is an operating record, not a controlled EIRP/echo calibration.
- ARRL 2008 EME contest results (official results PDF), <https://contests.arrl.org/ContestResults/2008/EME-2008-FinalFullResults.pdf>, pp. 217–219: W5LUA’s 5760 MHz entry reports a 5 m dish and 70 W at the feed; the narrative notes local 802.11a interference. This is a station record, not a matched bistatic-link measurement.

**Radiometer temperature reference:** ITU-R Recommendation P.372-15, <https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.372-15-202109-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf>, lunar radio-noise discussion/figure states an approximate Moon brightness temperature of 140 K near new Moon and 280 K near full Moon above 1 GHz (the continuation uses 210 K only as the midpoint). This is a brightness-temperature range, not a specified receiving dish’s antenna temperature. The conditional calculation integrates the uniform lunar disk against an ideal Gaussian beam: for a centered disk, `T_A/T_b=(Ω_Moon/Ω_beam)×<B>_disk`, with `<B>_disk=(1−e^(−q))/q`, `q=4ln(2)(θ_radius/θ_FWHM)^2` under the small-angle approximation. This Gaussian-average factor is needed in addition to the disk/beam solid-angle ratio; the script includes it. The resulting coupling is about 0.111 for the 3 m case and 0.278 for the 5 m case. Add the calculated Moon antenna temperature to an assumed 100 K off-Moon receiver/system baseline; actual sidelobes, phase-dependent surface brightness, station beam and receiver temperature can change the result.

**Calculation artifact:** `eme_5760_sensitivity.py` is a deterministic standard-library Python calculation; actual stdout is saved in `eme_5760_sensitivity.out`. Inputs are fully declared in the script and continuation: 5.760 GHz, equal TX/RX station classes, power at feed/antenna reference plane, aperture efficiency 0.55 assumed, 384,400 km, 100 Hz illustrative noise bandwidth, 100 K assumed off-Moon system temperature, no propagation or implementation losses, and OC albedo 0.06 carried from the local 7.209 GHz result solely as a conditional reference. The ±3 dB 0.03/0.06/0.12 albedo values are sensitivity cases, not confidence bounds. For the signal RCS, surface-element weighting is `⟨B_TX B_RX⟩`; with identical centered Gaussian beams, the script uses the analytic disk average `<B²>=(1−e^(−2q))/(2q)`, not the square of the mean one-way gain. The output estimates −154.18 dBm / 3.51 dB C/N per 100 Hz for the symmetric 37 W, 3 m case and −143.38 dBm / 13.22 dB C/N per 100 Hz for the symmetric 70 W, 5 m case, including the separately integrated Gaussian-average Moon-noise coupling. These are not predictions for the cited actual QSOs, not measured echo powers and not claims that the 7.209 GHz albedo transfers to 5.760 GHz.

The output also calculates delay-profile quantiles and moments. It assumes a spherical Moon, centered/co-boresighted transmitter and receiver with equal ideal Gaussian beams (a near-monostatic approximation), and `u=τ/τmax=depth/R_Moon`; actual separated Earth stations require the actual two-leg bistatic delay surface and both beam centers. For uniform scattering per projected area, `p(u)∝2(1−u)B_TX(u)B_RX(u)`, yielding 3 m / 5 m beam `t50=3.158/2.762ms`, `t90=7.725/7.333ms`, RMS-about-zero `4.565/4.268ms`, centered RMS-spread `2.697/2.618ms`, and first-five-mile fractions 1.04%/1.25%. The centered RMS-spread is `sqrt(E[(t−E[t])²])`; RMS-about-zero is `sqrt(E[t²])`. A second, expressly illustrative exponential depth weight `exp(−R_Moon u/L)` is fitted to a 50% first-five-mile cumulative fraction from the historical 1960 article; it gives `L≈11.7km`, `t50≈53.7μs`, `t90≈0.178ms`, mean≈0.077ms, RMS-about-zero≈0.109ms and RMS-spread≈0.077ms. Varying the fitted first-five-mile fraction to 0.4–0.6 changes `t90` to about 0.242–0.135ms. The source supplies no uncertainty interval, and these are not confidence bounds or target-frequency predictions. No 5.76 GHz time-delay echo profile was located; the nearby-band 2020 7.209 GHz measurement is CW Doppler/spectrum evidence, not delay-profile data. The stdout generated before the exact `⟨B_TX B_RX⟩` correction is retained as `eme_5760_sensitivity.pre_exact_beam.out` for comparison; current values are in `eme_5760_sensitivity.out`.

## Evidence limits and unresolved measurements

No 5.760 GHz calibrated radar cross section, polarization-resolved echo spectrum, station-specific measured beam pattern/pointing, measured off-Moon receiver temperature, or controlled target echo was obtained. The conditional computation therefore discriminates mechanism and plausible scale, not 5.760 GHz link feasibility for a specified pair or reliable mode threshold. A calibration at the target band and polarization, simultaneous off/on-Moon noise temperatures, beam and phase, plus time-tagged spectrum/delay observations are still needed to replace the declared sensitivity assumptions.

## Joint delay–Doppler desk calculation

**Source constraints:** Sun et al. §2.3, primary full text <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7180880/>, paragraphs 181–188, state that their 49-point DE436 surface-velocity calculation predicted 35 Hz across the 503 km Km illuminated footprint and 90 Hz across the 1,118 km Zv footprint, at 7,209.125 MHz; the paper leaves libration/target geometry tied to its epoch and stations. In §3 / Fig. 1 (paragraphs 194–208), it separately shows approximate observed spectra of 40/90 Hz using 2/5 Hz bins, without defining an edge threshold or width fit. The joint calculation uses the *predicted* spans for its source-anchored velocity-gradient bracket; it does not treat the figure extents as calibrated width data.

**Retarded-leg reference:** `../lead/comparisons.py::moon()` is the deterministic synthetic reference, not an ephemeris. It iterates distinct receive and transmit retarded times and compares `−f/C × dL/dt` against simultaneous-site and geocenter approximations for synthetic station/Moon motion. `eme_joint_delay_doppler.py` extends this path convention to a spherical Moon surface in a static observer frame: solve the uplink transmit-to-scatter and downlink scatter-to-receive legs for each projected point; relative delay is point arrival time minus subradar-point arrival for a pulse transmitted at the reference epoch; retarded point Doppler is the exact two-way radial frequency-ratio difference relative to that same center point. It subtracts the synthetic common center Doppler before applying the residual frequency window. The source-anchored alternative puts `Δf=Kx` on the same projected surface, with `K=(35 Hz/503 km)` or `(90 Hz/1118 km)`, scaled by `5.760/7.209125`; it extrapolates local slopes over a full projected disk as a conditional shape. The `x` axis is defined along the projected gradient; it is not recovered from an actual station or Moon orientation.

**Weights and estimator:** same centered ideal Gaussian TX/RX beam-product weight as the signal RCS, multiplied by either constant projected-area albedo or the previously declared `exp(−depth/L)` sensitivity, with `L=11.7207/11.7866 km` for 3/5m scenarios to fit the historical first-five-mile cumulative fraction. Projected-area annulus mass is `2(1−u) du dφ/(2π)` for `u=depth/R_Moon`; `u=s²` quadrature concentrates resolution at short delays. `F_delay`, `F_Doppler`, `F_joint` and `F_factorized=F_delay F_Doppler` are energy fractions under the stated rectangular windows. `t50/t90`, RMS-about-zero and centered RMS spread use the weighted point-delay distribution. This is a conditional surface density, not a phasor-coherent echo sum, receiver likelihood or observed signal.

**Window examples:** using the existing lead nominal 225 Hz three-carrier band (residual half-width 112.5 Hz) and a declared 1 ms delay sensitivity, the source-anchored diffuse 5m law gives `F_joint=0.2122`, exactly captured in-band, while the 50 Hz narrower-window case gives `F_joint=0.2122` versus independent-marginal product 0.1429 (Km gradient) or 0.1263 (Zv gradient). The 50%/first-five-mile exponential core gives 0.8999 joint power within 0.17832 ms and ±50 Hz; for its full marginal product it is also 0.8999 because that core's modeled Doppler support is within ±50 Hz. The script also reports a separate kinematic orientation stress, whose 1–99% spread exceeds the nominal band; do not confuse it with the source-anchored model or a target observation.

**Resolution and retained outputs:** fine radial grid `u=s²`, 2,000 radial by 360 azimuth cells (`Δu≈0.000068` near the 5-mi interval); a 1,000 radial-cell rerun and the fine run are retained as `eme_joint_delay_doppler.n1000.out` and `eme_joint_delay_doppler.out`. The first-53.68 μs capture is 0.49956 vs 0.49955 at those resolutions. The 1D model predicts exponential-core `t50≈0.0537 ms`, `t90≈0.1783 ms`; joint finite-range retarded geometry gives about 0.0542 and 0.1797 ms, respectively. The earlier coarse 160-cell stdout that exposed underresolution is preserved as `eme_joint_delay_doppler.initial_coarse.out`; do not use it as final quantile evidence. Run command for saved outputs: `python3 research/eme/eme_joint_delay_doppler.py 1000` and `python3 research/eme/eme_joint_delay_doppler.py 2000` from the study root.

The retarded-grid kinematic variants are explicitly not target-Moon results. The orthogonal spin/orbit orientation creates about ±234 Hz 1–99% differential support in a diffuse model; an idealized prograde synchronous spin case nearly cancels the transverse orbital term because terrestrial station rotation/libration are omitted. Both demonstrate sensitivity to unobserved orientation under a toy model. No full-disk target-frequency spectrum, lunar surface cross-section map, station site/reference frame, epoch or measured bistatic capture was supplied. The source-anchored gradient is the bounded near-band comparator; synchronized, time-tagged delay-coded or multifrequency range sounding plus independently referenced station ephemerides/capture is needed before setting an EME-specific narrow receive window. Ordinary calibrated CW spectra can test the Doppler marginal/centroid across epochs, but do not resolve delay or validate the joint occupancy.
