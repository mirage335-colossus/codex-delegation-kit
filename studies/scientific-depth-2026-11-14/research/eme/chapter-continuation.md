# EME research continuation: observed 7.209 GHz results and conditional 5.76 GHz check

This is an additive continuation to `chapter.md`; it preserves the original first-pass chapter and calculation. It clarifies the publication’s model-versus-measurement split, corrects one citation attribution, and adds a same-reference-plane 5.760 GHz sensitivity and a delay-profile discriminator.

## Corrections and direct measured-versus-predicted comparison

**Citation correction to the first pass:** the 1960 paper “A Theory of Radar Scattering by the Moon” is by **T. B. A. Senior and K. M. Siegel**, not Evans and Hagfors. The source was a 1960 *Journal of Research of the National Bureau of Standards D* paper. The NASA-hosted original scan was opened and its article masthead inspected; see the appended source ledger.

The 2020 *Sensors* paper reports both a-priori model estimates and an actual bistatic observation ([Sun et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7180880/)). Keep these categories separate:

The result is encouraging validation at one 7.209 GHz target/epoch, but source text provides no raw-voltage release, error bars, exact station calibration covariance, or 5.8 GHz comparison. It does not justify treating these model inputs as known constants for amateur links.

## Conditional 5.760 GHz same-plane budget and lunar noise

`eme_5760_sensitivity.py` and its captured stdout, `eme_5760_sensitivity.out`, give an explicit conditional link estimate anchored to the two reported 5.760 GHz station classes: 37 W / 3 m dish and 70 W / 5 m dish. The calculation assumes *balanced transmit and receive stations of each class*; those source reports describe individual station operating records, so the calculation is **not a reconstruction of either reported QSO**.

Both transmitters’ power is defined as delivered at their feed/antenna reference plane. The predicted signal `Pr` is at the receiving antenna terminals, using the two-leg bistatic radar equation. The baseline off-Moon system temperature is assumed to be 100 K at that same receiver reference plane, before the Moon’s main-beam contribution. Other stated assumptions are aperture efficiency 0.55, circular-polarization coupling factor 1, mean Earth–Moon range 384,400 km, 100 Hz noise bandwidth and no feedline loss, transmit/receive mismatch, weather, tracking error, local RFI, compression, clock error or coding/integration gain. The angular disk-average Gaussian beam factor is included in both lunar illumination and reception.

The radar albedo `σ̂_OC=0.06` is taken from the measured/inferred 7.209 GHz Mare Imbrium region in Sun et al.’s paper only as a **conditional sensitivity input**. It is not claimed calibrated at 5.760 GHz, globally representative, or appropriate to an unspecified EME polarization pair. The script treats it as uniform across the projected lunar disk to compare scale. It shows `σ̂_OC=0.03/0.06/0.12` as ±3 dB power sensitivities, not confidence bounds. Real terrain, polarization, frequency and beam illumination can move outside them.

The Moon-noise coupling uses the antenna-temperature integral, not just a solid-angle ratio. For a centered uniform disk and ideal Gaussian beam, `T_A/T_b=(Ω_Moon/Ω_beam) × <B>_disk`, where `<B>_disk=(1−exp(−q))/q`, `q=4 ln(2)(θ_radius/θ_FWHM)²`. The resulting couplings are about 0.111 and 0.278 (rather than the flat-beam solid-angle ratios 0.118 and 0.326). Signal cross-section weighting is the two-way product `⟨B_TX B_RX⟩`; for the identical co-boresighted Gaussian beams used here it is `<B²>_disk=(1−exp(−2q))/(2q)`, not `<B>_disk²`. Its values are 0.891/0.734 for the two dish classes. The difference from squaring the mean beam gain is small for these broad beams, but the calculation now uses a consistent surface-element weighting. These are small-angle Gaussian main-beam integrals; sidelobes, pointing offset, detailed limb brightness and measured beam departures are omitted.

This is a lossless comparison at the stated reference planes, not a communication threshold. With lunar brightness from the ITU-R P.372-15 range (140–280 K), the modeled antenna temperature is 15.5–31.0 K for the 3 m beam and 39.0–78.0 K for the 5 m beam. The resulting C/N ranges are 3.79–3.24 dB and 13.79–12.71 dB, respectively. The 5 m representative case gives a 2.00 dB noise rise, near but not independently reproducing the reported >2 dB 5.76 GHz Moon-noise observation; that station’s dish, beam, baseline system temperature and protocol were unavailable. `C/N0` and the 100 Hz `C/N` are physical RF ratios before demodulation and integration, not the 2020 paper’s `Pr/ΔPn` statistic or an inferred QSO threshold.

The full-disk albedo calculation matters for identifiability. It assumes the beam illuminates the lunar disk and weights that disk with a uniform albedo. A narrow-beam radar equation instead uses its *illuminated footprint area* and local albedo; mixing full-disk area with the narrow-beam 503 km albedo would overcount cross section by the area ratio. The 2020 paper’s measured 0.06 is local to its footprint; extending it over the lunar disk here is a declared sensitivity, not evidence for an all-Moon albedo. An absolute 5.76 GHz prediction still requires a measured station antenna pattern/polarization and an appropriate band/terrain RCS or echo calibration.

## Delay-profile comparison and simplest channel recommendation

A uniform diffuse disk is a useful *geometric support* comparator but a poor sole lunar echo-power model. On a near-side sphere, the maximum two-leg subradar-to-limb delay support is about 11.6 ms. Under uniform power per projected disk area, a 5-mile near-center depth contains 0.924% of the projected area. This is not the same as uniform power per delay: for delay fraction `u=τ/τmax`, the cumulative projected-area fraction is `2u−u²`, because successive equal-delay annuli have decreasing area toward the limb. The source-matched arithmetic in `eme_5760_sensitivity.py` compares this 0.924% geometric comparator with Senior and Siegel’s historical observation that half the return power was received within the first 5 miles of lunar depth (53.68 μs two-way delay). The measured cumulative fraction is about 54 times (17.3 dB) the uniform-projected-area fraction in that early interval. Their report also says substantial leading spikes/correlation diminish farther into the echo and that limb returns, when detected, can be 30–35 dB below the strongest scattering regions and rare. These are historical observations, not a 5.8 GHz empirical profile, and the passage does not identify one exact radar frequency for the cumulative-power observation.

The script also computes delay quantiles and moments for two conditional spatial-scattering laws, weighting both TX and RX by the same ideal Gaussian main beam for the 3 m and 5 m symmetric cases. This is a centered, co-boresighted/near-monostatic sphere approximation; a real bistatic link needs the actual two-station delay surface and beam centers. For the uniform-albedo-on-projected-disk model, `t50/t90/RMS-about-zero/RMS-spread` is 3.16/7.72/4.56/2.70 ms (3 m beam) and 2.76/7.33/4.27/2.62 ms (5 m beam); only 1.04%/1.25% of power falls in the first 5 miles. Here RMS-about-zero is `sqrt(E[t²])`; RMS-spread is the centered standard deviation `sqrt(E[(t−E[t])²])`. Beam narrowing shifts these quantiles by a few tenths of a millisecond, not orders of magnitude.

As a distinct *historical-shape scenario*, the script weights the projected-area profile by `exp(−depth/L)` and fits `L` so that 50% of integrated power lies in the first 5 miles. This gives `L≈11.7 km`, `t50≈0.0537 ms`, `t90≈0.178 ms`, mean delay `≈0.077 ms`, RMS-about-zero `≈0.109 ms`, and RMS-spread `≈0.077 ms`; beam choice changes those values negligibly. Varying the assumed early cumulative fraction from 0.4 to 0.6 (illustrative sensitivity only; no source error bars) changes `t90` from about 0.242 to 0.135 ms. These are conditional profile outputs under the near-monostatic geometry, not observations. A single historical cumulative datum does not establish an exponential law; the calculation demonstrates a roughly fortyfold difference in `t90` between that illustrative surface-weighted core and the diffuse full-disk response. The historical article also reports leading spikes/correlation decreasing farther into the echo and rare, weak limb returns.

This comparison argues against a uniformly bright 11.6 ms channel as the default but does **not** identify the exact tail law, which is still unmeasured at 5.8 GHz. Use a strong near-subradar coherent cluster plus a weaker optional distributed tail whose delay support is geometrically bounded and whose total fraction/spectral weighting remains an explicit sensitivity. Keep center Doppler/path trend and differential scatterer Doppler separate; add complex amplitude variation for coherent scatterer cancellation only where a fading decision requires it. A single delayed ray with equal power, uniform full-disk scatter or a dense synthetic multipath train is not supported as the default physical lunar response.

## Coupled delay–Doppler window discriminator

`eme_joint_delay_doppler.py` evaluates a bounded surface grid with the same two-way beam-product weight and projected-area scattering profiles used above. It retains pointwise excess delay and differential Doppler in the same record, then measures energy inside rectangular receiver windows. The baseline geometry solves the two propagation legs for a pulse sent at `t=0`, subtracts the subradar-point arrival time and common two-leg carrier shift, and derives point Doppler from the corresponding two-way radial frequency ratio. The synthetic center motion (30 m/s radial, 1,022 m/s transverse) is inherited from the lead’s retarded-leg desk example. This is a point-grid extension of that diagnostic, not an actual Earth-site/ephemeris solution.

For a near-band, primary-anchored projected law, use the 2020 paper’s *predicted* DE436 spans, not its approximate observed Fig. 1 spectrum extents, to estimate a conditional local gradient: 35 Hz over the 503 km Km footprint, or 90 Hz over the 1,118 km Zv footprint, at 7.209125 GHz. Scale those gradients linearly with carrier to 5.760 GHz and set `Δf=K x` on an assumed projected gradient axis. This gives `K=0.05560` or `0.06432 Hz/km`; extending either local law over a full lunar disk is an explicit sensitivity extrapolation. The source’s observed widths were instead approximate 40/90 Hz figure extents at 2/5 Hz bins, without a defined width edge; they are not converted into calibration points. The link therefore stays conditional on target orientation, station geometry, libration, exact bistatic dewarp and which projected coordinate carries the local velocity gradient.

| Conditional echo weighting and frequency law | Window | Joint retained power | Product of independent delay and Doppler marginals |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 5 m beam, uniform projected albedo, Km-like projected gradient | `τ≤1 ms`, `|Δf|≤50 Hz` | 21.22% | 14.29% |
| 5 m beam, uniform projected albedo, Zv-like projected gradient | `τ≤1 ms`, `|Δf|≤50 Hz` | 21.22% | 12.63% |
| 5 m beam, historical exponential-core sensitivity, either gradient | `τ≤0.17832 ms`, `|Δf|≤50 Hz` | 89.99% | 89.99% |
| 5 m beam, historical exponential-core sensitivity, either gradient | `τ≤0.17832 ms`, `|Δf|≤112.5 Hz` | 89.99% | 89.99% |

The 225 Hz three-carrier example in the lead calculation has a nominal ±112.5 Hz half-width. Under either full-disk linear gradient extrapolation, that wide window contains the entire modeled differential-Doppler support (high-gradient full-disk edge is about ±112 Hz, leaving little margin); for the exponential-core sensitivity, the 1–99% differential-Doppler interval is only about ±18–21 Hz. Thus the simplest source-anchored core plus bounded tail is spectrally compatible with that broad nominal window, but a narrower 50 Hz receive window is not interchangeable: under a diffuse full-disk model, taking the Doppler marginal fraction independently of a 1 ms delay gate underestimates the actual joint retained power by about 6.9–8.6 percentage points. The point-ray alternative predicts 100% retention in all positive windows and erases this geometric coupling.

The script also retains a deliberately constructed kinematic stress: lunar-center motion plus an orthogonal spin-axis projection, with the observer fixed at Earth center, yields an approximately ±234 Hz 1–99% differential-Doppler span and only 56–59% retained by ±112.5 Hz for a diffuse disk. An ideal prograde synchronous spin-only case nearly cancels the transverse orbital gradient; because these variants omit rotating terrestrial stations, libration and the actual ephemeris, neither is a target-Moon prediction or a recommended law. Their value is to expose how unmeasured projection/orientation can change the joint window result. The primary-anchored gradient is the more useful conditional baseline; do not shrink a live search from it without matching station-specific geometry and measurements.

Resolution check: a 1,000-cell transformed radial grid and 2,000-cell grid give the historical exponential model `t50=0.05463/0.05423 ms` and `t90=0.18046/0.17973 ms`. For the 5 m core model, the first-53.68 μs joint capture at ±112.5 Hz is 0.49956 at 1,000 cells and 0.49955 at 2,000 cells, consistent with the one-dimensional target of 50%; the 2,000-cell grid has `Δu≈0.000068` near that interval. The simpler one-dimensional range-only calculation gives `t50≈0.0537 ms` and `t90≈0.1783 ms`; the roughly 1 μs difference is the finite-range, retarded two-leg geometry correction in this desk model, not new observational precision. Initial coarse 160-cell output and both later grids are retained in separate `.out` files for audit.

Falsify this conditional joint map with synchronized, time-tagged delay-coded/pseudonoise or approved multi-frequency range sounding and independent station/time/ephemeris references across multiple pointings. Those observations can compare joint delay–frequency occupancy with the factorized marginal product after the same pointing, beam, polarization and noise corrections. Calibrated CW spectra across epochs can separately test the Doppler marginal and geometry residual, but plain CW does not resolve range delay and cannot validate joint delay–frequency occupancy. If the joint result is indistinguishable within measurement uncertainty, retain separate simple marginals; if coupled support near the actual receive-window edge is repeatable, use a low-order joint surface-weighted kernel before widening the receiver state model.

The actual stream-rate receiver need not process 11.6 ms of equally useful echoes merely because the geometry permits them. The primary measurable task is whether the expected core signal and chosen low-level tail fit the receiver's declared delay/search margin and decision latency; capture/processing must retain the actual supported tail when enabled. At 7.209 GHz, distinguish the 35/90 Hz model-predicted footprint spans from the approximate 40/90 Hz Fig. 1 spectrum extents observed at 2/5 Hz resolution; neither is a threshold-defined or RMS/FWHM bandwidth. Use these only as cross-band context alongside the approximately 7 Hz session Doppler residual statistic. At 5.760 GHz, local station inputs and at least one measured echo spectrum are required before any width or fade law is treated as empirical.
