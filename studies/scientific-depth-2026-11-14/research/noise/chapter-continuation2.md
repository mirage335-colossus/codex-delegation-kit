# Noise continuation 2: standard aggregation, time structure, and campaign disagreement

This is an additional review response; it preserves `chapter.md`, `chapter-continuation.md`, and both earlier ledgers. Calculations and output are in `noise_quantile_timechecks.py/.out`. Current standard identity remains in the preserved first-pass chapter: P.372-18 is the edition approved 2026-09-08, but its 30.4MB official PDF was not accessible to the research reader. This continuation therefore carefully separates the inspected P.372-17 text and P.372-15 numerical method from current v18 compliance.

## What P.372 means by combining noise levels and deciles

ITU-R P.372-17 says the receiving system noise must be considered at the input of an equivalent lossless receiving antenna. External source factors are defined by available noise power divided by `k T0 b`, with `T0=290 K` and the receiver's noise-power bandwidth; antenna-circuit and feed losses/internal receiver noise enter separately (v17 §1.2, equations 1–9). It says average noise power is seldom enough for performance except the white Gaussian background case; use envelope APD/exceedance. For impulsive noise above about 1 GHz, it calls out peak over a specified time period, high-level exceedance probability and pulse count separately (v17 §1.2.4). P.372 also explicitly excludes identifiable radio-service signals from its estimated noise levels and says it covers only signals entering through antenna/feed; cable-structure pickup and inadequate screening/balance lie outside the tabulated external-noise model. Thus WGN category medians, received-service occupancy, receiver-added noise and feed pickup are different data objects.

P.372-17 §6.1 treats outdoor man-made WGN medians and upper/lower time deciles (Table 1 and 2). For HF, its §7.1 explains that multiple components are often comparable. Each component's noise figure in dB is modeled as two half-normal distributions on either side of the median, with upper/lower standard deviations `D_u/1.282` and `D_l/1.282`; the corresponding linear noise factors have lognormal distributions. Its equations approximate the distribution of a *linear power sum* by matching moments of the lognormal sum and then report total median and upper/lower decile deviations. They do not state that one should add category dBm values or sum separate source quantiles. The approach effectively omits covariance terms for source-to-source variability; v17 §6.1 says time/location variations “may be assumed uncorrelated.” That independence is a model assumption, not guaranteed for lightning, a shared transmitter/clock, common propagation/ionospheric modulation, or same-site switching equipment. Also these are WGN/environmental factor statistics; SCN, identifiable service signals, burst event timelines and antenna coupling require separate measurement or a model.

### Conditional numerical check: independent versus common variation

`noise_quantile_timechecks.py` performs a reproducible standard-library Monte Carlo with two equal component lognormal powers, each having a 0 dB median and 9 dB standard deviation in dB (individual upper decile 11.53 dB, below P.372's 12 dB upper-deviation guard). It compares sample-by-sample linear addition and quantiles to (a) naively summing individual P90 powers, (b) the P.372-15 moment-matched lognormal-sum equations, and (c) a perfectly common dB fluctuation with the same marginal distributions. Results, in dB relative to one component median:

- Independent source draws: simulated total median 5.80 dB, Q90 15.09 dB.
- Sum of each separate Q90 power: 14.54 dB. It underestimates independent-sum Q90 by 0.55 dB in this example.
- P.372-15 moment-matched approximation: median 4.49 dB, Q90 15.07 dB. It approximates Q90 closely in this case, but its approximate median is 1.31 dB below the empirical simulation. It is a statistical approximation, not direct convolution or an exact tail law.
- Perfectly shared dB fluctuation: Q90 of total is 14.54 dB, exactly the sum of individual Q90 powers because both components occupy the same rank simultaneously. The same marginals, different dependence, move the Q90 by ~0.55 dB relative to independent variation here.

This is a deliberately conditional model test, not empirical component values and not a current P.372-18 computation. It shows why report `F_a` median, upper/lower decile, source-dependence assumption, bandwidth and reference plane. For physical voltages `v_total(t)=Σv_i(t)`, instantaneous power always contains cross terms `2Re{v_i(t)v_j*(t)}`. Mean power/PSD sums in expectation (or an averaged frequency/time bin) when cross-spectral terms average to zero; otherwise include `2 Re{S_ij}` / covariance. The Monte Carlo script directly adds per-component equivalent-power random variables, not phasor voltage samples, and therefore assumes that zero-cross-correlation condition rather than proving it. A deterministic line, coherent paths, common-mode spur, or coupled transmit leakage must not be reduced to independent powers. Even when linear powers do add, separate source exceedance percentiles cannot be added as if they were the same-time total percentile unless dependence is explicitly modeled.

### Edition/equation anomaly to resolve before implementation

The official P.372-17 PDF is accessible as parsed text through the ITU search/open cache, at `https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.372-17-202408-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf`, §7.1, lines 1490–1527. That parsed v17 equation (21) displays `α_i=exp[F_ami/c − σ_i²/(2c²)]`; the corresponding retrieved P.372-15 equation (19) displays a `+` sign. Literal substitution of v17's displayed minus sign into its immediately following equations (18)–(19) for only one component with median 0 dB and σ=9 dB returns −18.65 dB rather than the input 0 dB median. This fails the single-component identity that a combination rule should preserve. Attempts to obtain a screenshot of the official v17 PDF pages returned HTTP 404, so it may be a v17 typesetting/extraction error, an equation change requiring another term, or another unresolved issue. The P.372-18 PDF is separately too large for the research fetch. **Do not copy the retrieved v17 lognormal moment equation into code without visual verification against an authorized official document or formal correction.** The numerical moment demonstration above uses the internally consistent P.372-15 plus-sign expression as a clearly identified predecessor method; it does not silently repair or override v17.

## Time-order discriminator: measured records and matched-marginal chronology

NTIA TR-91-277 Part I provides a useful primary observed temporal record in MF/HF rather than merely naming APD alternatives. It analyzed 42 one-second complex I/Q captures from Bedford, Massachusetts in March 1989, center frequencies 3–30 MHz. Capture chain was horizontally polarized log-periodic antenna (~10 dBi), RF-to-baseband, analog LPF 400 kHz on each I/Q leg (800 kHz equivalent RF span), 1.024 MS/s, 8-bit ADC and a 0–31 dB variable attenuator; data were raw before narrowband excision. Each printed time-domain figure shows 4 ms (4096 samples), but the report did not publish the raw ADC records in the source we accessed.

Two measured examples distinguish mechanisms which an envelope APD alone does not identify:

- In §2.3.1, the authors say the 5.936MHz Case Study 1 data were obtained 10 March 1989 09:58:10 UT (Figure 1, printed p.7). The I marginal is described as resembling Gaussian; conditional on a complex zero-mean Gaussian process the authors note the envelope should be Rayleigh, and Figure 3 envelope histogram “does indeed resemble” that form. However Figure 5’s transformed power-envelope histogram has a pronounced low-power dip and is described as more consistent with Gaussian noise plus narrowband sine(s), i.e. Rician-like. These are approximate fit statements about linked transformed observables, not exact simultaneous Rayleigh and Rician distributions; the report does not establish a single consistent parametric law for this record. The contrast is evidence that the I histogram alone is insufficient evidence for clean AWGN or uniform frequency occupancy. Appendix A-1 separately lists record #5 at 5.936MHz on 15 March 09:46:34 UT with 22dB variable attenuation; it does not list an entry matching the Case Study 1 time. Treat the dated case passage/figure as the locator and do not identify it with Appendix record #5.
- In §2.3.5, the authors say Case Study 5 was obtained 15 March 1989 19:22:31 UT at 23.862MHz (Figure 29, printed p.42). One of the 42 records was visibly impulsive. The pulses appeared quasi-periodic, typically about 0.5ms apart in Figure 30, another segment of the same one-second record (printed p.43); the authors infer likely man-made rather than atmospheric origin because arrivals were not random. They report reduced gain to avoid pulse saturation made the weaker background quantization apparent. Appendix A-1 row #8 instead lists 23.862MHz on 15 March 19:22:32 UT and 6dB attenuation, a one-second difference from the case text. Preserve the discrepancy; no cause is established. This record separates event-source timing, front-end headroom and ADC floor: a clipped or quantized trace would alter the observed APD. It is a one-case observation; authors do not establish a population distribution or source identity.

The NTIA authors specifically state that raw voltage/amplitude/phase PDFs, average level-crossing rate, power spectra and frequency-domain occupancy do not completely characterize noise: higher-order timing statistics are needed to describe pulse-width/interarrival distributions and nonstationarity. That is directly relevant to cyclostationary/periodic interference and outages. An envelope CDF can tell how often a given amplitude occurs, but not whether impairments are spread across all receiver windows, clustered into one blackout, synchronized with symbol timing, or phase-coherent.

A bounded exact-marginal chronology counterexample is in the script: across 1,000 hypothetical windows of 1,600 samples, both processes have exactly 1% event-valued samples with the same event amplitude and hence identical APD histogram. In the “distributed” ordering each of 1,000 windows has 16 event samples. In the “clustered” ordering ten windows are fully event-valued and 990 contain no event samples. Both mean 16 event samples per window; the clustered event-count standard deviation is 159 samples/window versus zero for the distributed construction. Thus event-window incidence is 100% versus 1% under identical marginal APD. This is a synthetic data-equivalent, not a calibrated receiver outage probability; it demonstrates precisely which temporal statistic is missing. For a target chain, measure event-arrival times, pulse widths, interarrival/cluster distributions, periodicity/cyclic spectrum, and cross-band coincidence alongside APD and clipping/AGC chronology. Replay only through the production receiver and do not interpret an event coincidence as a slot loss until matched modulation/phase/channel and receiver trials establish it.

## Measured campaign disagreements: why reference category is not a local truth

### Netherlands man-made noise campaign versus P.372 median field curves

Fockens, Zwamborn and Leferink's Netherlands VERON/TNO/University of Twente campaign (article first published 2018; *IEEE Transactions on Electromagnetic Compatibility* 61(2), Apr 2019) compared new measurements with P.372 environmental curves. Its Figure 3 is a field-strength graph at 2.7 kHz bandwidth over 0.1–100 MHz; approximate graph-read measured means include city/residential ~27/18 dBµV/m at 1.8 MHz and ~7/−2 dBµV/m at 30 MHz. Sample counts by category were city n=8, residential 36, rural 10, quiet rural 3. The receiver was a self-calibrating R&S ESH2 with datalogger and separate active/tuned loops; below 30m the measurement used tuned loop antennas; positions were outdoors/about 10m from premises walls when applicable. The figure reports means and standard deviations, and the paper warns the worst measured MMN values were more than twice the plotted standard deviation above the averages. The measurement metric is field strength in 2.7 kHz, not receiver input dBm/Hz; this is an antenna/calibration/environment population record.

For a reproducible rough comparison, converting the P.372-17 median `F_a=c−d log f` (short vertical lossless monopole above perfect ground) to field strength with its equation (7), `E_n=F_a+20log f_MHz+10log(2700)−95.5`, gives ~13.65 dBµV/m for city and 9.35 dBµV/m residential at 1.8 MHz; ~4.24 and −0.06 dBµV/m at 30 MHz. Approximate plotted measured-minus-reference differences are therefore +13.35/+8.65 dB at 1.8 MHz but +2.76/−1.94 dB at 30 MHz. These are graph reads, not tabulated paired residuals or significance tests; the reference monopole and measurement loops/site populations are not identical. The disagreement is frequency/category-dependent, so applying one universal “modern site is +X dB” correction would be unsupported.

### Separately reported 1.65 MHz magnetic-field comparison; data overlap unresolved

The 2024 ITU-R SM.2449-1 report presents a Netherlands man-made-noise comparison at 1.65 MHz (magnetic-field basis, converted consistently within that analysis): city `−37.56 dBµA/m` (SD 8.4) reference versus `−26.23` (SD 6.4) measured; residential `−41.86` (5.8) reference versus `−32.34` (5.5) measured—approximately +11.33 and +9.52 dB shifts. Those values are not directly subtractable from Fockens' dBµV/m graph, because field component, antenna reference/conversion, observation set and units differ. Both refer to Netherlands observations and the report’s data provenance does not establish whether its tabulated values reuse the Fockens campaign or overlapping measurements. Treat this as a separately reported comparison with possible data overlap unresolved, not independent replication. It still shows that the category reference and measured field levels differ in this reported analysis; do not average the two reported shifts or carry them to another country.

The Fockens campaign, the separately reported SM.2449 comparison (possible overlap unresolved), and v17 man-made reference curves answer different questions: reference median for a defined ideal antenna/category; site-population field data with actual loops, floors, nearby electronics, geography and a finite sample; and a separately converted H-field comparison. Keep each provenance and reference plane. The uncertainty is already large enough to materially change a noise margin at 1–2 MHz, while at 30 MHz a different quiet/galactic/ionospheric and local-EMI composition applies.

## Decisions and measurement changes

- **Reuse** P.372 external-noise factor/temperature only at its declared reference plane and as background. Do not add service signals or feed/common-mode pickup into its outdoor WGN median category.
- **Adapt** P.372 time/location deciles as statistical descriptors only under their stated half-lognormal and independence assumptions. Resolve the v17 sign anomaly and v18 update before implementing a quantile combiner.
- **Measure** component powers linearly at a shared reference plane; check complex cross-spectrum and cross-band/event coincidence if correlations or shared paths are plausible. To estimate total `Qp`, combine simultaneous samples or a validated joint model, not same-number individual percentiles.
- **Add** event run length, arrival/interarrival, burst occupancy by receiver decision window, cyclic/autocorrelation/spectral-line checks, and AGC/clip/quantization chronology to calibrated raw-IQ analysis. An APD regression fixture must keep both sample histogram and chronological version; the latter can differ while its APD is identical.
- **Keep conditional confidence**: NTIA's 1989 chain is 800kHz, 1.024MS/s, horizontal log-periodic Bedford capture; it demonstrates mechanisms and instrument constraints but cannot parameterize the modern 225Hz modem channel. Same-MHz-appropriate calibrated site captures remain required for transfer.
