# Noise branch continuation: measured tails, dependence, occupancy resolution, and front-end scaling

This continuation preserves the first-pass `chapter.md` and `source-ledger.md`. It adds source-backed numerical discrimination requested after the independent review. No receiver or pump code was run or changed. The reproducible desk checks are in `noise_continuation.py` and `noise_continuation.out`.

## Decision handoff

1. **Retain Gaussian AWGN as a decoder baseline, not an environmental law.** A measured HF distribution can be heavy-tailed before dawn and become more Gaussian as distributed man-made transmitters wake up. Therefore retain at least (i) Gaussian same-PSD baseline, (ii) captured/reconstructed measured APD plus temporal structure where the capture supports it, and (iii) explicit isolated/bursty events. A PDF alone does not determine pulse timing, event clustering or slot outcomes.
2. **Sum independent source powers only at a defined plane and only for uncorrelated components.** For equal variance contributors, uncorrelated average powers add (+3.01 dB for two equal components); the same-power perfectly correlated pair is +6.02 dB and anti-correlated components may cancel. If atmospheric pulses, man-made emissions, a receiver spur, and desired leakage are coherent/coupled, measure cross spectra/coherence and simultaneous time records rather than adding marginal dBm values. External antenna noise and receiver-added noise are ordinarily modeled as independent at the input only after checking the receiver/source setup.
3. **Do not infer modem slot loss from published occupancy.** A Cyprus HF monitoring study reports a congestion statistic `Q`: percentage of 1 kHz channels inside a service allocation whose 100 ms RMS signal level exceeded a stated field-strength threshold, with measurements sampled on an hourly cycle and a full HF sweep in under one hour. Its 1 kHz cells are 4.44 times the modem's calculated 225 Hz occupied width, so they do not resolve the modem's exact channel or estimate event probability in a 16 s bit window. They do establish that HF interference occupancy varies with frequency and local time, with band-dependent day/night direction.
4. **Condition anti-alias/filter requirements on actual dynamic range and actual blocker distribution.** The modem defaults summarized in the project source are 960 complex samples/s and a calculated 225 Hz occupied width. The idealized Nyquist edge is ±480 Hz. Using the Analog Devices AN-282 first-order pole/octave rule, transition from 112.5 Hz to 480 Hz spans 2.093 octaves; the illustrative order is 4.78 poles at 60 dB stopband goal, about 6.37 at 80 dB. These are planning approximations, not a filter prescription; an out-of-band blocker can alias before the digital modem sees it, and blocking/compression/intermod can happen even when it is later digitally filtered.

## Observed heavy-tail HF records versus Gaussian alternatives

### Giesbrecht et al. (2006): waveform-derived signed-field PDF at 6.1–6.3 MHz

The inspected primary paper reports measurements at an Adelaide site over 48 hours (16–17 February 2005), with examples shortly before/after local sunrise. A vertically polarized Rohde & Schwarz HE011 active antenna sensed the vertical E-field; the study's raw baseband/DDC stream had about 4 kHz bandwidth, nominal 10 kHz sampling (footnote gives exact 31,250/3 Hz), and the receiver swept 2–25 MHz at 125 kHz/s every 15 minutes. It formed candidate signal-free noise samples by dividing approximately 200 kHz regions into approximately 1 kHz subchannels, selecting subchannels by their 90th percentile and a 6% threshold, and converting receiver output through antenna factor/gain calibration to field units. That procedure reduces obvious occupied signals but cannot establish that every remaining sample is environmental noise rather than weak or transient interference; this was a noisy site and source separation is explicitly difficult.

Their Figure 4 gives a useful measured model discriminator, not a universal fit claim: for a 4 kHz band near 6.1–6.3 MHz at 07:00 LT just after sunrise, measured signed-field standard deviation was 100 µV/m while fitted Bi-Kappa scale σ was 20 µV/m; the Bi-Kappa curve matched the central interval only, approximately −100 to +100 µV/m, and not the whole observed distribution. At 07:45 LT, measured SD and the Bi-Kappa σ were both 46 µV/m and that fit was substantially closer. The neighboring Figure 3 describes the measured PDFs shifting toward Gaussian over the morning. In the same 15-minute sequence, D-region absorption suppresses remote skywave contributions while local man-made sources increase as activity starts: a Gaussian approximation can improve because many sources accumulate, not because the HF band has one stationary Gaussian law.

This directly distinguishes candidates: a single Gaussian with variance fitted to the 07:00 record misses either the large observed scale/tails or center, while the named Bi-Kappa family still misses the full 07:00 shape; at 07:45, Bi-Kappa is more plausible for the observed marginal. A static one-parameter impulsive mixture also cannot reproduce both time-varying samples without changing event fraction/scale and adding filtering/temporal structure. Use a measured short capture as a trace/APD test, preserving its band, antenna calibration, times and event chronology. This is receiver-input distribution evidence, not a calibrated antenna-terminal dBm/Hz floor or a modem BER/slot-loss observation.

### Older atmospheric APD records and distribution interpretation

The inspected Beckmann/NIST Journal of Research 68D (1964) article analyzes and overlays Crichlow et al. measured APDs. Its described physical model treats lightning atmospherics as randomly arriving impulses with exponential rise/decay, Poisson occurrence and approximately lognormal peak distribution, vector-added to Rayleigh background. In this model the high-probability/small-signal portion can resemble Rayleigh/thermal noise, while rare high amplitudes diverge; propagation attenuation also changes the normalized APD parameters. A plotted measured example at 113 kHz/Boulder (12 September 1958) is approximately +22 dB over RMS at 1% exceedance and +40 dB at 0.0001% (read from the published figure; its curve has empirical points and an interpolated segment). This historical low-frequency APD must not be transferred numerically to 6 MHz or to a 225 Hz modem receiver without matching frequency, detector/filter bandwidth, antenna, location, and observation epoch. It does show why a mean PSD/RMS match cannot certify tail equivalence: +22 dB voltage is 12.59 RMS, whereas a proper complex-Gaussian Rayleigh envelope with the same RMS-amplitude convention has exceedance `exp(−12.59²) ≈ 1.5×10⁻⁶⁹` (the script also reports the different signed-real Gaussian convention explicitly). This calculation is an analytic counterfactual, not a claim that the historical points are independent samples.

For one deliberately simple same-mean-square stress, `noise_continuation.py` constructs a zero-between-events mixture with event fraction `q=0.001` and event magnitude `1/sqrt(q)=31.62` RMS. It gives 0.001 exceedance at 5 and 10 RMS, against Gaussian 5σ probability 5.73×10⁻⁷ and 10σ probability 1.52×10⁻²³. At 4,000 samples/s and a 16 s interval it has 64 expected events and essentially unit probability of at least one event. This stylized mixture is not a fitted lightning law: its central PDF is poor, and filtering/burst correlation determines the event duration and independent count. Its purpose is to prove that equal average power does not constrain overload-tail or event presence. For environmental realism use observed APD plus pulse-arrival/width/clustering measures or recorded samples; do not present this fixed-q example as an operating frequency.

Below 9 kHz, the existing VLF research adds direct contrary evidence to a fixed-distribution assumption: Reuveni et al.'s 386-day Negev loop record reports day/hour and seasonal changes in their envelope-deviation statistic. That Vd statistic is not the same as PSD or instantaneous signed-field PDF; retain separate measures. At 5.8 GHz the EME handoff's lunar brightness is an antenna-scene term (beam-convolved into antenna temperature), not receiver noise temperature; preserve `T_A`, loss temperatures, and `T_rx` separately at a named reference plane. Neither the VLF impulse evidence nor HF lightning APDs apply to EME.

### Component noise combination: explicit cross term

At a receiver reference plane, let zero-mean real components have powers `P1`, `P2` and normalized correlation `ρ`; then `Psum=P1+P2+2ρ√(P1 P2)`. For proper complex components the corresponding complex cross spectrum is `S12(f)` and PSD is `S11+S22+2Re(S12)`; a frequency-dependent complex coherence must be retained for colored/filtered paths. For equal component powers, the script yields: `ρ=0` → +3.010 dB over one source; `ρ=+0.5` → +4.771 dB; `ρ=+1` → +6.021 dB; `ρ=−0.9` → −6.990 dB. This is a conditional second-moment calculation; actual fields may be spatially coherent before antenna coupling or spur/leakage may share an oscillator. Powers from independent thermal/resistor sources add in expectation, but deterministic tones, common clock spurs, coupled transmit leakage, or same-origin multipath need phase/coherence or a joint record. A power sum also loses the tails: the mixture example has exactly the same RMS as Gaussian yet differs by many orders of magnitude above 5σ.

**Measure jointly:** calibrated receiver-input PSD by resolution/equivalent-noise bandwidth; calibrated antenna-terminal RMS and APD; complex cross-spectrum/coherence for simultaneous channels or reference sensors; event threshold/count/width/inter-arrival and overlap; and ADC/AGC state on the same clock. For multiple carriers, estimate `S_ij(f)` and simultaneous threshold/event coincidence rather than multiplying marginal exceedance probabilities. Tail independence can be tested with coincident event counts and a contingency table by band/state; it cannot be inferred from marginal APDs. A noise-only equivalent temperature cannot represent deterministic cochannel waveform or an overload/recovery mechanism.

## HF occupancy evidence mapped to the modem measurement scale

Haralambous et al., “HF Spectral Occupancy over the Eastern Mediterranean” (2015; station record beginning in 2012), describes a Cyprus measurement system using a vertically polarized R&S HE016 and EM510 wideband receiver. It measured user allocations separately because service powers, bandwidths, modulation and procedures differ. Its statistic is `Q`, the fraction/percentage of 1 kHz channels in an ITU allocation with RMS field level above a selected threshold. An example plot uses a threshold 20 dBµV/m. Receiver measurement interval was 100 ms; all HF allocations were swept in under an hour, and stored interval RMS values were later processed. The primary text reports lower-HF allocation occupancy peaking at night and upper-HF occupancy often peaking by day; less D-layer absorption at night permits longer-distance co-channel signals, while an upper/lower usable-frequency window shifts with ionospheric support. Their article emphasizes the time, allocation, geography and solar-cycle conditioning; an hourly snapshot is not a continuous full-band simultaneous recording.

Scale comparison: modem source summary gives 225 Hz calculated occupied bandwidth (`3 × 1.25 × (36 Hz / 6 × 10 symbols)`) at 960 complex samples/s and a 16 s bit interval. The Cyprus measurement's 1 kHz channel is 4.44× wider, so a single strong narrowband interferer may be diluted/averaged or a neighboring transmission may contaminate the cell; it does not reveal how much interference lies inside a particular 225 Hz tone. Its 100 ms sample interval is 160× shorter than 16 s, and the frequency sweep revisits a given allocation on a much slower cycle. Its published `Q` is occupancy by field-strength threshold, not modem C/N0, noncoherent detector statistic, or chance of losing a slot. For an aligned swept analyzer, ITU-R SM.2256-1 recommends RBW no greater than one tenth of narrowest channel spacing: that would be 22.5 Hz for a 225 Hz evaluation cell; FFT-bin centers should align to channel centers and spacing should be <=225 Hz (and <112.5 Hz if not aligned). This is measurement design guidance, not a requirement that modem DSP use that RBW.

Use this study as evidence that HF interference occupancy is structured in time/frequency and that fixed generic interference probability is not supported. To estimate occupancy of this modem-shaped channel, retain raw calibrated IQ or PSD with <=22.5 Hz analyzer RBW (or aligned finer FFT) and collect an uninterrupted simultaneous time series whose revisit/acquisition covers the 16 s message window; classify signal-present using a threshold matched to target receive bandwidth and sensitivity. Preserve both per-frequency and time-domain `Q`, but do not infer slot-loss without replay through the modem under matched channel, waveform, receiver and input level. The study's single regional/site record and 2012–2013 era conditions do not establish global/current 2026 occupancy.

## Front-end blocker, alias, filter and clock consequences

The current synthetic modem settings are 960 complex samples/s, a 225 Hz computed occupied width, and 16 s/bit from the inspected project baseline; they are not an analog acquisition chain. The required analog filter is therefore unknown until the ADC input bandwidth, desired passband, out-of-band blocker level and effective dynamic range are specified. AN-282's illustrative estimate is `M ≈ DR/[6 log2(fs/(2 fa))]` poles for an analog low-pass with pass edge `fa`, stop edge `fs/2` and idealized 6 dB/octave per pole. Applying it conditionally with `fa=112.5 Hz`, `fs=960 Hz`: transition ratio 4.267 (2.093 octaves), giving roughly 3.19/4.78/6.37 poles for 40/60/80 dB. Compare AN-282's published worked example: 3 kHz signal bandwidth, 12 kHz sample rate, 60 dB range, 2:1 transition ratio, hence 10 poles. The smaller required width relative to Nyquist increases available transition-octaves; this approximate outcome does not specify RF/IF preselection, phase linearity, ripple, alias rejection across wide RF input, or anti-blocking behavior. A weak analog baseband chain with 80 dB blocker/noise ratio is not protected by the 5th pole estimate alone.

ITU-R SM.2256-1 provides a concrete receiver-measurement failure example: an over-wide measurement filter makes a strong signal appear to occupy adjacent channels; receiver overload can raise apparent noise until all channels appear occupied, and raising threshold can then miss the real weak channel. It specifically warns that below 30 MHz a fixed nearby-channel noise estimate is generally not advisable because noise changes with time/propagation; revisiting a noise channel or per-channel adaptive estimate is preferred, with final threshold typically 3–5 dB above estimated noise to avoid transient-noise false occupancy. This applies directly to calibration/occupancy measurement method. It does not mean adaptive threshold is always the modem's correct detector.

Clock scaling is also concrete: 1 ppm sample-rate error at 960 samples/s over the 16 s bit interval is 16 µs timing drift, 0.01536 sample; at 100 ppm it is 1.6 ms / 1.536 samples. If the RF synthesizer shares that ppm reference, carrier-frequency errors scale with RF carrier (e.g. 30 Hz at 30 MHz for 1 ppm), but sample clock and RF LO can use different references and be separately calibrated; do not treat this as a guaranteed frequency offset. The capture must log sample/LO reference, resampling and passband transfer together. The actual receiver's coarse/fine drift tolerance still requires source-level receiver probing, explicitly outside this research branch's no-receiver-run authority.

For blocking/order, use the assigned receiver/native findings (including the owner's existing clipping/two-tone work) rather than duplicating a second two-tone experiment. The decisive physical evaluation remains a calibrated antenna-port/preselector/LNA/ADC-plane single-blocker and matched wanted-signal sweep: blocker frequency/level, wanted-power loss, noise-rise, gain/AGC state, clips and recovery, and intermod products. A post-ADC software notch cannot undo front-end compression, AGC suppression or aliased energy. If the ADC is preceded by a direct-sampling RF stage, the needed anti-alias attenuation spans unwanted Nyquist zones and is much stricter than the low-rate equivalent-baseband pole estimate above. Distinguish this analog capture from the simulated digital one-pole filter in the existing model.
