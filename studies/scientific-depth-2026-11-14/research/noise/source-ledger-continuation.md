# Noise continuation source ledger

Only sources opened/inspected during the noise continuation are listed here. The prior `source-ledger.md` remains intact.

| Source | Access and exact locator | Observed claim/data and applicability |
|---|---|---|
| Giesbrecht, Clarke & Abbott, “An Empirical Study of the Probability Density Function of HF Noise,” *Fluctuation and Noise Letters* 6(2), L117–L125 (2006; measurements Adelaide, Feb 16–17, 2005). [Publisher/author-hosted full PDF](https://www.eleceng.adelaide.edu.au/personal/dabbott/publications/FNL_giesbrecht2006.pdf) | Primary full text, §§2–4, PDF lines 149–204; Figs. 2–4 and conclusion lines 489–509, 564–572. Measurement setup: HE011 vertical E-field antenna, about 4kHz DDC bandwidth, exact Fs=31,250/3 Hz, 2–25MHz sweep at 125kHz/s each 15min; examples chosen near sunrise; approx 200kHz groups/subchannels ~1kHz and 16,000 samples; selection by 90th percentile/6% threshold; calibration via antenna factor. Quantitative distribution comparison in Fig.4: 4kHz-band 6.1–6.3MHz; 07:00 measured SD=100µV/m while Bi-Kappa fit σ=20 only matches −100..+100µV/m; at 07:45 fit σ=46 equals measured SD=46µV/m and fits much closer. | Empirical site/time-specific signed E-field PDF; small sample of sunrise examples at a relatively noisy site. Selection algorithm's “noise samples” are not independently proven free of all signals/interference; antenna/calibration differs from a modem antenna. Supports time-varying alternatives, not a generic HF law or receiver BER. |
| Beckmann, “Amplitude-Probability Distribution of Atmospheric Radio Noise,” *Journal of Research of the National Bureau of Standards D* 68D(6), 723–? (1964). [Accessible full scan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/40/Amplitude-probability_distribution_of_atmospheric_radio_noise_%28IA_jresv68Dn6p723%29.pdf); DOI 10.6028/jres.068D.066. | Primary full article; model description §§1–2 and equations (5.17)–(5.19), lines 580–626; Figure 5/observational caption lines 667–697. Figure 5 points are described approximate; Crichlow et al. measured 113kHz at Boulder, Sep 12 1958; +22dB over RMS at 1% exceedance, +40dB at 0.0001%, interpolated curve portions marked separately. Article describes Poisson spike arrivals, exponential transient rise/decay, lognormal peak and vector sum with Rayleigh component. | Historical low frequency atmospheric APD; empirical tail illustration and mechanism model, not a numeric HF 2026 prior. Figure measurement bandwidth/reference detector details were not recoverable in the inspected caption; no application bandwidth transfer assumed. |
| Haralambous et al., “HF Spectral Occupancy over the Eastern Mediterranean,” *Radio Science* (2015). [Full text accessible via ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/276274277_HF_Spectral_Occupancy_over_the_Eastern_Mediterranean) | Primary paper full text, §§2–4 lines 101–198, 202–258, figs. 2–5 lines 287–370. Cyprus station operated hourly; R&S HE016 vertical antenna, R&S EM510 receiver; 100ms measurement interval and full HF allocation scan <1h; `Q` is percent 1kHz channels per allocation exceeding chosen RMS field threshold; plot example threshold 20dBµV/m; lower HF often peaks occupancy at night, upper HF may peak day, with D-layer/usable-window explanation. | Occupancy is thresholded per-channel fraction for that location/service allocation, not modem packet loss. The study starts data collection in 2012 and represents one Eastern Mediterranean site/solar-cycle interval. A 1kHz measurement bin is too wide to resolve a 225Hz target channel. |
| ITU-R SM.2256-1, “Spectrum occupancy measurements and evaluation” (2016). [Official PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-r/opb/rep/R-REP-SM.2256-1-2016-PDF-E.pdf) | Primary procedural recommendation, §3.1 lines 432–457 (wide filter phantom adjacent occupancy; RBW <=1/10 narrowest channel spacing for swept analyzer; FFT bins aligned and spacing <= channel spacing); §§3.2–3.4 lines 458–540 (minimum S/N, overload spreads apparent noise/occupancy, threshold and dynamic-noise measurement); lines 519–540 note below 30MHz static nearby noise estimates generally inadvisable due propagation-dependent variation, dynamic threshold 3–5dB above measured noise. | Measurement design guidance. It documents analyzer failure modes and threshold dependence; it does not establish modem detection requirements or real site noise. |
| Analog Devices, AN-282, “Fundamentals of Sampled Data Systems.” [Official PDF](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/application-notes/an-282.pdf) | Primary vendor application note, anti-alias filter selection example in §3.7; search result exposes formula/figure: estimate pole count from desired dynamic range divided by approx. 6dB/octave per pole, with 3kHz signal bandwidth, 12kHz sample rate and 60dB dynamic range requiring about 10 poles. | Analytic first-pass filter scaling only. We applied same rule conditionally to the modem's 960sps/225Hz synthetic occupied width; actual analog RF/IF and blocker conditions are unknown, and filter family/phase/stopband are not specified by the formula. |
| Noise continuation desk script/output `noise_continuation.py` / `.out` | Local reproducible calculation, standard Python only; command `python3 studies/scientific-depth-2026-11-14/research/noise/noise_continuation.py`; output saved beside script. | Computes Gaussian exceedance at the historical 22dB-over-RMS level; same-mean-power sparse sample stress; two-source correlation power relation; filter-pole estimates for 40/60/80dB; sample-clock drift; occupancy-bin ratio. These are calculations, not measured modem outputs. |
