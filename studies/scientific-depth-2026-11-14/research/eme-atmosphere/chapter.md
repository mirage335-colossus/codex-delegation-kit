# Conditional 5.760 GHz EME atmospheric endpoint case

This is a bounded atmospheric case for the study's existing conditional 5 m / 70 W EME echo level. It is not a site calibration, rain-availability prediction, or representative joint weather probability. The reference echo before terrestrial atmosphere is **−143.38 dBm** at the antenna plane in the supplied `eme_5760_sensitivity.out`; that estimate assumes a 0.55 aperture efficiency and a uniform-disk albedo sensitivity borrowed from a different microwave band. Preserve those lunar-model uncertainties when using this weather comparison.

## Matched apparent-elevation endpoints

Both endpoints use the same reciprocal co-located TX/RX geometry and 5.760 GHz carrier, with separate signal losses on the outbound TX and inbound RX legs. The clear reference is at 60° apparent elevation. The stress case is at 10° apparent elevation through a horizontally broad, 1 km deep slab at 273.15 K containing rain at 100 mm/h and cloud liquid water content of 0.20 g/m³. The cloud liquid and precipitating rain are co-located but remain separate P.840 and P.838 terms. The low-elevation slab must extend about 5.67 km horizontally to supply the full secant path. This coincidence is a declared conditional scenario, not a joint-probability estimate or a universal bound.

P.838-3 Table 5 was linearly interpolated from 5.5 to 6.0 GHz, then used with its polarization-averaged circular form (tilt τ=45°), `γ_R = k R^α`. At 5.760 GHz, `k_c=4.7886e−4`, `α_c=1.602519`; at `R=100 mm/h`, `γ_R=0.767798 dB/km`. P.840-9's double-Debye liquid-water equations at 273.15 K give `K_l=0.0309165 (dB/km)/(g/m³)`, so the slab cloud attenuation is `K_l × LWC × path_length`. For both methods the 1 km vertical slab is extended using `1/sin(elevation)`; P.676-13 Annex 2 likewise uses cosecant elevation scaling for its approximate gas method at/above 5°.

The signal echo applies both transmission legs, so the conditional weather difference is about **9.29 dB round trip**. The endpoint powers above subtract one TX and one RX leg from −143.38 dBm; do not apply the RX loss a second time elsewhere. The rain term dominates this particular high-rate storm case; cloud and gas remain separate smaller terms.

For the RX main-beam sky/Moon brightness, the single-layer radiative transfer is `T_out = η T_in + T_layer (1−η)`, with `η = 10^(−A_oneway/10)` and the selected layer physical temperature 273.15 K. The calculated effective one-way transmissions are 0.989421 (60° clear) and 0.339818 (10° wet); corresponding layer emissions are 2.89 K and 180.33 K. Apply this atmospheric transformation once to incoming main-beam sky/Moon brightness. Ground spillover bypasses the layer, and receiver noise remains a separate term. If beginning from the prior 100 K aggregate off-Moon Tsys assumption, split its receiver, sky and ground terms before transforming sky: applying the layer to aggregate Tsys would incorrectly attenuate receiver/ground terms or count sky twice. Atmospheric emission is not the signal-path loss; it is the thermal source accompanying the RX layer attenuation.

Atmospheric delay is small beside the roughly 2.56 s lunar round trip but is not mathematically zero. P.676-13 Annex 1 Eq. (23) defines excess atmospheric path from the layer refractivity sum. For scale only, a flat exponential refractivity profile with surface `N≈315` and 7.35 km height scale gives about 2.3 m (7.7 ns) zenith excess one-way; secant scaling gives ~13.3 m (44 ns) at 10° and 2.7 m (8.9 ns) at 60°. The reciprocal EME route doubles those excess-delay estimates (~89 ns versus ~18 ns). These are coarse profile estimates, not an operational delay correction; use site weather/profile and ray bending for calibration.

## Traceability and limits

- ITU-R P.838-3 (2005, in force): [official recommendation](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.838/en), [official PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/r-rec-p.838-3-200503-i%21%21pdf-e.pdf). Table 5 supplies the 5.5 and 6 GHz coefficients. Equations (4)–(5) supply the rain-specific attenuation and polarization combination. Interpolation is shown in `endpoint_case.py`.
- ITU-R P.840-9 (2023, in force): [official recommendation](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.840-9-202308-I/en), [official PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.840-9-202308-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf). Equations (2)–(10) give the double-Debye liquid-water dielectric and specific attenuation. The input is the explicit uniform liquid content and slab depth, not a statistical cloud map.
- ITU-R P.676-13 (2022, in force): [official recommendation](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.676-13-202208-I/en), [official PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.676-13-202208-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf). Figure 4 plots zenith gas attenuation for the standard atmosphere (1013.25 hPa, 15 °C and 7.5 g/m³ water vapour); the 5.760 GHz value here is a deliberately coarse ~0.04 dB chart-read sensitivity input, not a precise line-by-line result or site weather calculation. Annex 1 is the suitable follow-up when a station pressure/temperature/humidity profile is available; P.676 also documents atmospheric brightness/noise-temperature methods.
- ITU-R P.618-14 (2023): [official PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.618-14-202308-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf). Its slant rain path geometry uses `(h_R−h_s)/sin θ` for elevation θ≥5°, and a curvature expression below 5°. P.618's annual exceedance prediction machinery is not used here: the example specifies an instantaneous uniform rain rate and finite slab.

Run `python3 studies/scientific-depth-2026-11-14/research/eme-atmosphere/endpoint_case.py` to reproduce the arithmetic. Output is captured in `endpoint_case.out`. The calculation does not model refraction to convert free-space to apparent elevation, nonuniform rain/cloud fields, rain-cell statistics, exact vertical gas profiles, antenna spillover, EME pointing, or any station-specific RCS/polarization. It does not claim absolute link feasibility.
