## 2. The common model is a contract, not a universal fading distribution

Use an epoch and a shared sample-clock basis. A frozen local narrowband projection for one measured receive port can be written

`H(t,f) = sum_k a_k(t,f) [u_r^H J_k(t,f) u_t] exp(j phi_k(t) - j 2 pi f tau_g,k(t))`,

where `f` is the complex envelope offset around actual RF/IF mapping, `tau_g` is the envelope/group delay relative to a declared time origin, `phi` is the already-referenced carrier phase at the band center, and `J` maps the specified polarization basis along that path. A scalar `a` is permitted once both projections and transfer plane are measured or explicitly conditional. For arbitrary near fields use a measured complex E/H tensor or a two-port instead of plane-wave `J` and `E/H=377 Ω`. For a resolved, dispersive model retain a short causal filter or frozen complex frequency table; do not infer delay solely from its magnitude. Bulk deterministic delay may be expressed as a public receive-epoch origin plus a bounded residual history; do not ring-buffer days or seconds of history solely because the arrival origin changed. Propagate used source/Helper indices on the canonical calendar and retain enough residual delay and filter transient before attributing the quiet aperture.

**Polarization is also observability.** On a one-port station no receiver can reconstruct arbitrary unobserved complex polarizations. On a calibrated multiport station both the wanted vector and simultaneous noise covariance matter: optimal local linear weights are proportional to `R_n⁻¹ h`. The executed two-port counterexample keeps total wanted power 1 and both per-port noise powers 1. With correlated noise .9 and opposite-phase wanted voltages, the apparent equal-sum receiver sees zero while a known, calibrated noise-subspace combiner would have normalized SNR 10. With co-phased signal the same arrangement gives .526, just above the known one-port .500. The large ideal value is geometry/known covariance and phasing, not evidence about available VLF or EME hardware or a robust near-singular inverse. Test scalar first, a second path only from simultaneously observed ports with covariance/phase uncertainty and regularization, as with crossed E/H components, HF O/X or EME physical antenna diversity. Signed axes and circular sense/wave direction must be part of the calibration.

For a true nondispersive path with known `tau=L/c`, `phi=-2π f_c tau` and `delta f_c= -f_c d(tau)/dt`. Evaluate retarded source position and phase consistently once. For dispersive channels estimate both the derivative of phase with frequency (`tau_g=-∂argH/(2π∂f)`, with instrument/reference time removed) and time phase derivative (`nu=∂phi/(2π∂t)`, with LO/timebase error marginalized or calibrated). They are not interchangeable. Do not apply double phase when a complex renderer already carries full RF phase, or double Doppler when moving-delay phase already produces it. In the current channel the legacy constant-delayed ray and moving (RF+IF) branch have intentionally different arithmetic; preserve legacy regression and name the convention when adding a typed path.

**Coherent weights versus tap power.** Coherent sum is a voltage/sample operation before receiver noise. Sum powers when components are resolved or uncorrelated in a stated averaging process; don't use that sum as instantaneous band-selective received power. Report separate average/apparent path gain, mean/RMS and thresholded/percentile delay/Doppler definitions and lower/upper confidence/censoring, as in [ITU-R P.1407-8 §2.2, §5.2–5.3](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.1407-8-202109-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf). Fades and true outages are signal effects; validity loss is missing observation. Mean receiver input level must specify available or delivered power/impedance and which noise plane. Correlation is defined for the specified local state and averaging window. `1/B` is conventional delay resolution for a simple PDP estimate, not an unconditional super-resolution bound. Fit amplitude+phase against held-out sounding frequencies/windows. Store what was below detectability, the phase-reliability floor and unresolved latency; report unresolved rather than treating noise as zero-power taps or giving infinite phase slope at a null.

Our executed counterexample uses `H=1−r exp(−j 2πf × 0.007)`: at `r=.99`, the center is 42.97 dB below the tap-power sum, the adjacent 75 Hz tone is 2.98 dB above, and the center phase slope implies apparent group delay −693 ms while actual tap support is 0/7 ms. At `r=1.01` it flips to +707 ms. This is interference near a notch, no backward propagation and no justification for a 700 ms receiver gate. Under a *separate* locally independent equal-tap model at 7 ms the 75 Hz adjacent correlation magnitude is .0785 and 150 Hz outer-to-outer magnitude .9877. A scalar frozen at each carrier center has expected relative power error 1.0785 at 37.5 Hz within the same carrier, while 100 µs gives .0003. Correlation need not fall monotonically across this small geometry; even identical correlations do not identify physical modes. Use full small covariance or measured few-response basis and error across **each used band**, together with dynamically shared clock/phase candidates; test independent gains as a useful mismatch control.

**Absolute noise.** Record three independent planes: (1) station delivered/available RF power and input PSD; (2) through calibrated antenna, feed, analog passband, LNA/gain/retune/blocking, converter reference and native filter, with headroom; (3) output dBFS/Hz/IQ and matched filter effective bandwidth, validity. Thermal one-sided RF density in watts per Hz is `kT`; the corresponding proper complex envelope convention gives `E|n|²=N0 Fs`, I and Q each half, over −Fs/2…Fs/2 before native channel filtering. Use measured integral `∫ S_n(f)|G(f)|²df` and cross-covariance when nonwhite; do not assign full-scale arbitrary impedance or equate E-field, volts, dBm and normalized simulation. `P_C` is wanted composite reference power, not strongest-carrier gain/estimated future power. Stop using broad-band noise rms directly as decoder-band variance. In the desk check, unit prefilter signal and 40 dB-Hz, Fs=960/1920/12000 require complex variances .096/.192/1.2; a 225 Hz rectangular noise bandwidth yields .0225 in all cases and 16.48 dB SNR. With one .8 dB lossy feed at 290 K and 65 K receiver, `T_in=Ta+(L−1)Tp+L Tr`, versus `T_out=Ta/L+(1−1/L)Tp+Tr`. Input versus receiver-plane C/N0 agrees to floating precision, whereas ignoring both costs 6.91 dB at Ta=35 K and only .006 dB at Ta=100000 K for otherwise matched example. Mechanisms vary by band even where units and conversion math do not. [ITU-R P.372-17 §1.2.1–4](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.372-17-202408-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf) supplies the lossless-reference convention and explains variability/APD; [edition 18 is in force](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.372-18-202609-I/en), and one must check its current normative body before shipping table values.

## 3. Mechanism choices across the physical envelope

### Sub-9 kHz: project the appropriate field first

Use transmitter power *through the relevant matching plane* plus a measured or modeled current/electric or magnetic moment and orientation. Short wire/loaded vertical, isolated or ground-referenced magnetic loop, galvanic/contact system, and grounded long conductors couple differently. A small loop can provide receive nulls against local interferers or strong magnetic coupling over local distance without being an efficient remote radiator; current alone is not usable remote power. In induction the components have different `r` scaling and field impedance; electrode complex Z and medium/electrode return dominate local transfer; splitting a wire+earth structure into isolated source plus an independent guide path may double count. Use measurable transfer against distance/orientation and near-conductor contamination checks, not a free-space Friis gain, guessed efficiency/current or fixed `E=cB` conversion. Record bandwidth, tolerable heating/current/voltage and detuning; compare receive antenna axes with injected calibration fields/two-loop substitution and on-air observations, distinguish sensor and antenna grounds. The VLF antennas branch carries the primary experimental alternatives, calibrated loop practices, direction counterexamples and hardware limits.

The center high-Q resonator if loaded is `H ≈ (1+j2Q_L Δf/f0)^{-1}`, with center group delay `Q_L/(π f0)` and approximately `4.605Q_L/(π f0)` for a 1% amplitude ringdown. The executed 8 kHz example at Q=10/50/200/1000 gives −.33/−4.74/−15.14/−28.99 dB at 112.5 Hz, and 1% settling 1.83/9.16/36.65/183.23 ms; shifting resonance 12 Hz worsens the high edge. At Q=200, fitting out even local center group delay gives a strongly non-linear unwrapped residual away from center, not evidence for a single phase shift. Matching at isolated tones is insufficient by [Fano’s original report §I.1, §I.3](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/5007/RLE-TR-041-08407050.pdf), which is a linear lossless matching theory with physically realizable load conditions, not a station measurement or an objection to carefully scoped time-varying matching. Retain measured complex transfer, passband/guard and gain/noise references, compare slower waveform if available, and revisit a narrower-band native profile only after whole Helper/DATA support is verified. Electrode coupling/loop near field and resonant radiation reference evidence are explicitly separate. Field observation after changing station current can discriminate under-launch from propagation only with repeatable reference and receiver calibration.

For wide area, take [ITU-R P.684-8 Annex 2, Table 2 / §§3–4](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.684-8-202208-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf) as a physics/field method, not an ultra-low-band reliability endorsement. Its mode excitation for electric dipoles distinguishes vertical, end-on horizontal and broadside horizontal; complex amplitudes are weighted projections in a direction and height, not an arbitrary omni antenna coefficient. In [Cummer (2000), §§II–IV, Fig. 3–5](https://people.ee.duke.edu/~cummer/reprints/023_Cummer00_IEEETap_EarthIonosphereWaveguide.pdf), reduced FDTD and mode solutions compare across wide lightning spectra including below 9 kHz; model includes lossy ground and anisotropic plasma, with finite grid and geometry limits. A lightning spectrum is evidence for frequency structure and mechanism, not a calibrated continuous-wave modem link. The newer [Gasdia and Marshall manuscript §III.C/IV.B](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10319646) compares a segmented mode solver and alternatives across a modeled 24 kHz day–night transition and states where reflected backward modes are omitted. Start with independently versioned offline frequency-by-time tables at actual centers, edges and within-band resolution. Validate on homogeneous and stepped path, day/night and local terminator with published limits, and compare held-out observed complex amplitude/phase at calibrated tones. Do not attach standalone surface/sky components to a complete waveguide solution. Reject blind free-space attenuation, a magic scalar diurnal phase sinusoid as a calibrated guide, and assigning cutoff/outage from band label alone. A first arrival, two-ray reduction or low-order causal FIR is sufficient only when its held-out response and transient/error support measured receiver decisions; defer solver-in-loop and real-time mode roots.

Direct sub-9 evidence sets a strong scale limit. [Rusch, J. Res. NBS §5–6](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/64D/jresv64Dn1p27_A1b.pdf) reported 8.4 kHz California to Cambridge reception and a distinct antenna orientation where vertical groundwave was not present; a large power-line excitation over favorable ground is not a portable reference. In [Nicholson's first-person report, 4 Jan 2015](https://klubnl.pl/rsgb_lf_group-archives/html/rsgb_lf_group/2015-01/msg00101.html), a 6194 km test at 8.822 kHz yielded 25 decoded characters in 3040 symbols at 8 s/symbol, estimated ERP ~150 µW, field ~0.12 fT with H and E channels combined, BPSK and prior carrier/timing/phase techniques. Its −13.2 dB at nominal 0.125 Hz symbol bandwidth implies about −22.23 dB-Hz *under that post-blanker equivalent noise convention*. The reported comparison −56 vs −76 dB in 2.5 kHz with/without blanker partly concerns a different background/APD, not a universal 20 dB detector gain. Spreading the same power/flat effective PSD across 225 Hz gives −45.75 dB raw SNR, 32.55 dB below the 0.125 Hz comparison, and yields no modem success claim. This makes it especially important to test station stability, bandwidth and blanking/coding conventions before adopting a receiver gain claim from a narrow multi-hour link.

The report states 24.6 information bits/hour, whereas 150 payload bits divided by all 3040 × 8-second signal symbols gives 22.20 bit/h. Its rate denominator is not reconciled; neither is a qualified native throughput or channel capacity. This mismatch does not affect the separate SNR/ENBW sensitivity calculation.

### About 1 MHz and 30 MHz: choose the comparable field solution

[ITU-R P.368-10 Annexes 1–2](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.368-10-202208-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf) supports vertical, near-surface, smooth spherical Earth with uniform electrical properties in its base case and recommends use when ionospheric term is negligible. Use the short vertical perfect-conductor reference field (specified radiated power conventions) or measured station field at a named reference distance, then calibrate with antenna effective length/transfer; a curve that includes spreading needs no additional free-space spreading term. Use ground constants by route, frequency and moisture uncertainty; mixed shoreline path is not an arithmetic average of conductivity or independent free-space segments. Evaluate geometrical heights, polarization, Fresnel clearance, antenna patterns and diffraction on the actual route. A measured groundwave component can coexist with a mobile two-path pattern or intermittent far later skywave, especially night at MF and at HF times with open paths. Compute a consistent coherent sum only for independently isolated terms or use a path solver that already includes them. Distinct phase-based and level-based test controls: fix endpoints, known antenna orientations, inject oscillator/time calibration, take delay-resolving sounder traces or night/day paired spectrum and observation; avoid calling every weather/AGC change Doppler. Stable single dominant path reasonably uses existing gain+arrival controls with narrowband check. Movement, terrain reflections and slow attenuation only deserve extra components if above measured support or relevant stress.

The [Adams et al. NTIA TR-84-151 report §8–9/Fig.49–55](https://www.its.bldrdoc.gov/publications/download/84-151_ocr.pdf) measures low mobile reception in terrain on routes up to 45 km including 28 MHz. With the same constants the rough-path smooth-earth vs WAGNER predictions differ by 15 dB in examples; WAGNER still differed from measured median by up to 25 dB on South path at 28 MHz. North smooth path disagreement was much lower. 20 dB variations over <50 m were reported with two-component ripple in one setting, contrary to assuming universal Rayleigh for terrain variability. This is near the requested upper boundary, not a direct 30.000 MHz calibration. Compare homogeneous baseline, ordered mixed ground, isolated near-LOS/diffraction/profile and measurements; if input geometry allows alternate paths, a mismatch is not automatically an incorrect ground conductivity. Report unsupported P.368 scope (notably sub-10 kHz or above 30 MHz); don't extend using legacy settings or a convenient model prediction. The groundwave branch carries numerical examples and route-specific field conversion.

Keep the radiated reference explicit. [ITU-R BS.561-2 §§2–4 & Annex 3](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bs/R-REC-BS.561-2-198607-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf#page=4) states that the P.368/P.1147 300 V cymomotive reference corresponds to 1 kW **EMRP** for a short vertical, or 300 mV/m at 1 km under the reference condition. The 1 kW HF **EIRP** reference instead corresponds to 173 mV/m in free space; under that directional normalization, 300 V is equivalent to 3 kW EIRP (+4.77 dB). This is a source normalization only; it does not set installed receiver antenna factor or justify repeating a conversion already inside a basic-loss formula. P.1147 reports a specified ground-loop skywave observation combining incident/reflected field effects, not the same antenna/arrival reference as per-mode HF isotropic power.

### HF skywave: conditional test processes and planning availability

Use antenna launch elevation/polarization and E/F layer propagation to select viable modes, including off-great-circle modes at high latitude. Where observation supports it, keep per-mode `tau_g`, center Doppler, diffuse spectrum and amplitude; use O/X measurements or bounded projection uncertainty and note hardware with only one IQ port cannot identify all Jones coefficients. The lowest useful realizations are fixed/coherent narrowband 1 or 2 delayed paths and locally stationary two-tap Gaussian. [F.1487 Annex 3](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf) defines Gaussian tap frequency spread `2 σ`, independent equal-mean-strength standard test paths with conventional equal spreads and center shifts for its profiles. Set native Gaussian width `B=2σ`, explicit delay and independent center separation; OU Lorentzian width or bounded smooth shifts with 30 Hz declared support are not equivalent. The existing seed-physical-grid Gaussian and continuous renderer are candidate controls; validate their empirical local covariance and profile conformance from the full native counterfactual before claiming conformance. Multiple magnetoionic components may produce shifted Gaussian double spectra, correlated center motion, changing path powers and unresolvable modes. Existing Gaussian options may be adequate teachers in a restricted case; two normalized rays with direct always present cannot fit mode-only dominance/disappearance or frequency-selective/directional/polarization evidence by construction.

For planning, [P.533-14 §§8,10.2 and Attachment 1](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.533-14-201908-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf) uses long-term conditioned mode/hours and derived design time/frequency/amplitude windows for circuit reliability. Its 2–30 MHz scope gives neither a calibrated short-time channel realization nor a 1 MHz result. Near 1 MHz, [P.1147-4 Scope/§§1–2/Annex 1](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.1147-4-200708-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf) offers LF/MF nighttime field planning in 150–1700 kHz and 50–12,000 km, ground-level loop aligned with the great circle and radiated vertical, annual medians of half-hour medians, with geomagnetic-latitude limits. Neither recommendation establishes 1.7–2 MHz coverage, an absolute modem noise floor or field tap covariance. Obtain appropriate instrument/site measurements for remaining gap. For slowly evolving F layers/TIDs, test mode drift/appearance from coherent sounder traces, leave echo amplitude/delay/Doppler independently parameterized where physically appropriate; for equatorial after-dark scatter and post-sunrise transition, condition on location/season/time and compare against distinct quiet hours. High-latitude propagation can become more directional or temporarily *available* through patches, not only degenerate into a scalar attenuation (see [Stocker et al., Radio Sci. 2013](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2013RS005264) and recent [Cameron et al. polar cap off-great-circle 2024](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2023RS007897)). Stocker Table 2 has P95 e.g. 13 Hz separate 80%-power Doppler width and 0.3 ms CMPS in one QN winter-day 14.36 MHz cell versus 1.5 Hz/4.1 ms at 4.64 MHz NB trough winter-day. These do not establish simultaneous extremes, independent axes, universal maxima, or equality to support or Gaussian `2σ`. A later variant should learn from localized delay × frequency × time state and mode membership, before fitting heavy statistics or separate skywave implementations. A [local scattering statistic](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=955b6f322ea892f59d0e5e7bb7b3828ef65f5278&repid=rep1&type=pdf) describes conditioned time/frequency-average scatterer power and stationarity extent; use it as a measurement/replay abstraction if raw data support, not a universal independent-tap generator.

The [HF planning reference calculations](../hf-reference/reference_checks.txt) demonstrate why a planning output should be an advisory input. For a conditional 419 km inland link at 1 MHz and R12=12, the P.1147 procedure gives 35.21 dBµV/m at sunset and 47.61 dBµV/m six hours later given the branch's time/geodesy and transmit assumptions. This is a model prediction at the specified loop plane, not measured receive power. In the separate historical D1 comparison, 16,268 matched measured/reference field cells (same path, frequency, year/month and hour at 1 kW EIRP reference) have overall predicted-minus-observed bias −0.06 dB yet 10.23 dB RMSE and 4,942 cells with |error|≥10 dB; another 22,444 possible cells lack observations. Rows are not independent and the databank informed evaluation of the recommendation, so do not use that error distribution as an independent qualification or label missing cells outages. A planning mean may bound a hypothesis or select investigation priorities, not select precise levels or silently prune the receiver grid. For a live implementation, pin source/version and separate physical time-delay and angular conventions from any reference code. The 686 km three-mode arithmetic comparison found different selected rays with the printed versus literal source geometry expression (`cos(Δ+ψ)` vs `cos(Δ−ψ)`) at boundary windows. Initial suspicion about time conversion was resolved: the code uses a light speed constant in m/s after conversion from km, so `Tw/1000` is correct for a millisecond input, while a printed `usec` wrapper label is misleading. This is a version-specific geometry and metadata comparison to reproduce before adopting a digital-reliability output; it is not a running C-library validation or evidence for a particular operating site.

For severe HF case build three different experiments: (i) severe but detectable multipath and changing support; (ii) fade/absorption/closed path with varying dwell and reacquisition under retained valid noise; (iii) credible analog/interference plus valid/invalid observation stress, recording when the environment makes reliable detection impossible. Reuse the existing single signal-only attenuation interval with cosine transitions in `PathEvolutionConfig` for isolated phase B checks; the existing bounded GUI two-ray variant cannot combine with moving delay, diurnal phase or that outage. A new multi-event and mode-specific state is justified only if independent conditioned observations or receiver-discriminating scenarios require mode disappearance/partial survival with those other effects. In [Bland et al. 2018, §3–4](https://munin.uit.no/bitstream/handle/10037/14662/article.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4), 2017 SPE-associated observations showed strong absorption and near-total radar backscatter loss over several days, estimated atmospheric noise attenuation up to 10 dB north/14 dB south at 12 MHz. That does not establish a two-station modem fade distribution, and why noise decreases matters: same absorbing path can attenuate some external backgrounds while transmitter power is lost independently of conducted local noise. Couple when physically conditioned, measure when available; single loss applied to all noise is wrong. Geometric delay-only Doppler imposes −.5 µs/s envelope motion for +5 Hz at 10 MHz; the alternative fixed group delay +5 Hz phase ramp is an exact desk counterexample. Treat geometry-linked synthesis as appropriate for some ray motion, not the assumption for reflecting/dispersive mode evolution. Offline ray/magnetoionic or full-wave calculations may generate predictions to test when angle/O-X, non-specular or near-turning behavior changes conclusion. Do not use straight plasma f⁻² calibration through reflective paths, and do not run full-wave solver per streamed sample or infer it from one scalar field.

The useful lower- and higher-latitude features differ even for receiver modes with the same NVIS label. In [Dear et al. 2024, Figs.6, 10–12](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023RS007877), the 87 km Java 7.12 MHz campaign's normalized 21 Dec 2021 CIR gives secondary arrivals at .213/.441/1.377/1.654 ms from the first; another cluster has 1.882 ms separation. The week reports mean .4627 ms RMS delay spread and 0.7–1.1 Hz estimated shifts, **not** widths; unsynchronized stations do not yield absolute TOF or modem availability. In the [Canadian DRDC sounder analysis §§2–3](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2019/rddc-drdc/D68-2-122-2018-eng.pdf), Harstad–Kiruna auroral NVIS observed up to the 12.5 ms DAMSON instrument limit and reported separate >60 Hz nighttime P90 Doppler widths; a different link Isfjord–Kiruna had values >40 Hz at selected P95 widths and near 9 ms delay marginals. Multiplicity and unequal mode powers matter: the report summarizes prior data where equal mode power is unsuitable in over one-third of bimodal cases and first arrival often is weaker. Treat clipping at the sounder delay limit as censored, and use these unlike source populations as discriminating profiles, without assuming simultaneous 12.5 ms / 60 Hz, proven uniform severity, or a stable phase for receiver proposals.

The [HF regimes chapter](../hf-regimes/chapter.md) also carries newer equatorial dusk/midnight drift measurements and [Walden's midlatitude 5.290 MHz beacon campaign](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2011RS004914), including 23 months at three emitters and five UK receiver sites. The latter measured seasonal medians and found model differences depend on season/likely sporadic-E or absorption; bound numerical conclusions to the paper access level (supporting numerical tables were unavailable to the branch). This is why the optics/geometry/circuit branch remains separate from single-mode within-window ACF tests. No available evidence supports a single all-HF gain/Doppler/availability distribution.

**Exact current generator choice matters across F.1487 widths.** `src/triad_gaussian_fading.hpp` drives an 8193-tap-limited receive-clock FIR, while `src/triad_random_process.hpp` uses a 53-tap FIR at physical `16B` knot/s, normalized cubic as used by `random_doppler_width_hz`. At nominal 960 complex samples/s, a 0.1 Hz quiet-profile numerical law requests 30,559 taps in the receive-clock version and actually rejects; .5/30 Hz cases allow 6,113/103 taps. A prospective standalone probe of the existing two headers verified rejection and direct ACF accuracy against the ideal, and precisely reproduced fixed-grid physical samples at shared instants under sparse and two dense query grids. Independent finite-kernel sums including interpolator and knot-phase effects put the fixed-grid mean ACF up to approximately 1.1×10⁻⁴ from ideal on the tested normalized lags and up to 1.75×10⁻⁴ phase-dependent spread. The sample probe is too noisy across seed roots to resolve this small difference; use the numerical kernel/subcase calculation for that claim. Do not select an easier sampling rate just to fit the legacy Gaussian teacher. Reuse the fixed grid for the quiet controlled fixture, retain legacy in valid cases as a tiny reference and compare spectral tails/level crossings across seed and case geography before declaring field or statistical equivalence. For strong dispersion compare low-cost models on relevant detector statistics, not only small ACF deviations or proposed term count. Existing header output is not evidence that the canonical stream configuration and app have been executed.

### Passive EME: deterministically remove only the predictable part

Use physical endpoints and metadata. For narrowband projected co-/cross-pol echo from a specified illuminated footprint, `P_r = P_t G_t G_r λ² σ /[(4π)^3 R_{tx}² R_{rx}² L_other]`, with reference plane, illumination/beam/horizon and thermal/surface assumptions. A bistatic cross section in one polarimetric channel is not another branch’s full-disk power or aggregate hemispheric albedo; include antenna fringes and realistic pointing/spillover/nonsynchronous stations. Near 5.8GHz use `λ≈5.17 cm`, coherent field weights and orientation for linear (−∞ dB ideal exact orthogonal), circular under specified transmit/receive handedness convention, and cross-polar lunar scattering independent of basis rotation. Opposite/same circular reflection labels and measurement convention must be stated; a fixed “polarization loss” is insufficient with unknown feed and geometry. Model Faraday terms and atmospheric attenuation/emission where material, smaller at microwave frequencies than severe HF effects; compare actual path/sky noise/TEC and if poorly constrained bound with known uncertainty. One needs distinct observables (hot/cold or Moon/sky `Y`, beacon or own echo, LO checks, signal/noise acquisition bandwidth, pointing scan) before positing gains. A beam may fill only part of the lunar disk for larger antennas; both echo power and brightness temperature contribution then change. Never apply Moon as an isotropic receiver 290 K or assume reducing decoder bandwidth reduces analog blocker exposure.

Geometrical round trip for geocenter 384.4 Mm is about 2.56 s. For echo depth, spherical plane-wave near-to-limb path difference and center-to-near-surface difference `2 R_m/c` are about 11.59 ms: the bound is not typical echo delay or RMS, and a particular bistatic footprint can illuminate different facets. The lunar observer must compute two topocentric **retarded legs** in compatible frame/time/reference with positive-receding sign. The [JPL Horizons Observer manual quantities 20/21](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/manual.html#obsquan) defines apparent range/rate and receive-epoch one-way down-leg light time; merely doubling this output at an arbitrary UTC/geocenter is not exact bistatic and not automatically exact monostatic. [WSJT-X User Guide §§12.6,21](https://wsjt.sourceforge.io/wsjtx-main_en.html) distinguishes Own Echo, Constant Frequency On Moon and DX range options, center residual vs full limb-to-limb width, and own echo estimates in defined system. Use as independent operational comparator, not matched sample truth or embedded code dependency. A metered numerical Horizons API attempt for three site cases failed with web inaccessible; the executed synthetic retarded-leg comparison in `comparisons.py` obeys the geometry/sign equations but is not an ephemeris claim. Same constructed configuration with +30° mono or +30°/−60° bistatic yields −9955.473 or +2013.225 Hz at 5.8 GHz; naive geocentric −1160.803 Hz even misses bistatic sign. Dropping retardation leaves ~1.5 Hz discrepancy with all same-epoch site geometry; whether important depends on coherent window, clocks, ephemeris error and residual fitting. Treat actual visibility/azimuth, proper time/frame/state, UTC/leap process and precision as prerequisites. 0.1 ppm RF error is 580 Hz at 5.8GHz, compared to .0008 Hz at 8kHz and 3 Hz at 30 MHz. The receiver cannot infer Hz origin from constancy alone; log native reference/calibration and allocate residual search no narrower than justified by uncertainty. Screen availability before committing compute but do not discard valid public hypotheses from model output.

**Capture order constrains the design.** A default 960 complex samples/s stream with a 225 Hz nominal DATA band can only hold its entirety for a centered shift strictly inside ±367.5 Hz in an ideal rectangular capture; the actual margin is smaller with Helper, phase-noise/scatter shelves, decimation rolloff and drift. A residual +390 Hz has a Nyquist-contained center but loses an edge, and +2013.225 Hz would appear at +93.225 Hz only if it were accidentally aliased. Actual anti-alias filtering instead removes energy before any late digital NCO. Apply RF tuning or wide-observation digital correction before the first narrowing that would remove the signal; `docs/robust-current.md:259–261` explicitly says existing FHSS profiles do **not** retune external radio hardware. Any live correction must either be a station front-end control with independent observed time/frequency/phase calibration or a digitally pre-narrowing path on appropriately wide valid data. Also keep trajectory slope where justified: correcting only constant CFO under a hypothetical residual .5 Hz/s leaves 16 cycles at edges of a 16 s aperture referenced to its middle. Test stationary beat and synthetic chirp through the actual front-end passband before comparing a detector; losing bandwidth is not a receiver model problem.

RF derotation and envelope dewarping solve related but quantitatively different problems. With 10 kHz of pure geometric shift at 5.8 GHz the fractional geometry rate is about 1.72 ppm. Over 16 s, the group-delay change is only 27.6 µs (~0.0265 sample at 960/s, 0.0031 cycles at the nominal band edge), so forcing a wideband resampler for the small DATA envelope may be redundant after correcting bulk arrival and carrier. Over 2048 s the same sustained rate changes envelope latency by 3.53 ms (~3.39 samples, ~0.397 cycles at the edge), so a longer coherent/retuned capture or joint clock error could need explicit sampling-map refinement. Determine support using measured waveform, public clock/delay prior, Helper frequencies and actual window; never replace a finite field check with this assumed rate, or equate the physical derivative with a device sample-clock ppm. By comparison, the [native integration desk check](../native/phase_precision.out) shows why forming absolute `UTC_seconds * 5.8 GHz` in binary64 can lose every phase-cycle remainder. Use split epoch plus local times/modulo cycles for physical phase; consider lower arithmetic for qualified post-isolation kernels and preserve clear precision/fallback receipts.

Lunar radar work and real radio links supply different evidence. The [EME branch](../eme/chapter.md) carries operational 5.760 GHz links and quantified 7.209 GHz bistatic observations with geometric beam-limited support and detector-noise normalization; 35/90 Hz support is predicted, not separately measured RMS bandwidth. Its source-matched desk `Pr/ΔPn` gives 52.50 dB with `ΔPn=kTB/√(B Tobs)` at 35 Hz and 10 s, near source 52.23 dB; the same signal's `Pr/kTB` gives 39.78 dB. This is a match under stated assumptions, not independent calibration. Use measured `H(t,f,pol)`/echo range–Doppler covariance if available, then a small set of weighted facets for residual delay/Doppler after common dewarp. Unknown target polarization, brightness, receiver input transfer and path calibration remain real; an attractive system C/N0 in a hypothetical large-dish case can't establish amateur hardware sensitivity. Model visibility or retuning blockage, wrong ephemerides/clock error and out-of-band interferer separately from residual fades and never let calibrated truth cover unavailable input samples. Local terminations/noise and finite spectral capture may dominate attainable reception even when passive geometric loss predictions are right. Conditional weather/pointing calculations are in the EME chapter, and [noise chapter](../noise/chapter.md) gives the distinct dish-pattern and foreground noise coupling checks.

The same independently inspected [7.209 GHz primary study, §§2–3 and Fig.2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7180880/) explicitly selected 2/5 Hz analysis bins at two receivers and measured residual spectral peaks −15/−10 Hz after ephemeris correction; the Km session's measured-minus-computed standard deviation was about 7 Hz. Their possible explanation is target beam-coordinate error; the offsets and spread do not identify the cause and do not prescribe a 5.8 GHz residual prior. In [Taylor's amateur self-echo trials, §3.1 and Figs.1–5](https://wsjt.sourceforge.io/LunarEchoes_QEX.pdf), 144/432 MHz return spectra and DE405-derived sightline corrections matched frequency within reported measurement uncertainty (~0.1 Hz under that instrumentation); chosen FFT bins were 0.37 Hz and most returns were averaged in groups of ten, with several interference rejections. The observer’s predicted limb-to-limb widths describe weak spectral wings as well as the stronger center; the same source’s microwave-calculation accuracy is a forecast. A practical benchmark should therefore compare (i) direct clock/IF test, (ii) same-site echo across Moon pass at set pointings and full spectrum plus independently calculated station coordinates/time, (iii) bistatic signed residuals for different sites. Measure the width/energy explicitly at chosen percentile/floor rather than taking plot extent as receiver noise bandwidth. Read frequency and clock estimates as uncertainty for an ephemeris proposal, and use a guard based on observed residuals and captures in future qualified configurations.

Existing incomplete slot can only be ambiguous; make mask-aware extension separate experiment |

There is a genuine identifiability limit at completion. For samples of a fully lost active transmitter and silence plus identical receiver noise, the observation law is identical. Even with optimistically known signal phase, matched Gaussian detection at 1% false DATA rate has only 3.0% detection at total residual E/N0=.1; at exactly zero the best achievable rate is 1%, identical to true quiet (executed comparison). It is impossible to both finish promptly after real transmitter silence and guarantee avoiding premature completion under arbitrarily long complete unobserved outages with no independent evidence. Keep two physically scored quiet slots as required and report the tested risk over matched blackout + later-resume cases; for field assurance, explore trustworthy external link-health or additional observations with explicit system authorization rather than pretending all such fades can be fully resolved by DSP. Distinguish false quiet before resumed frames from actual invalid/missing capture and from actual DATA-with-low-score ambiguity. Raw detector thresholds, 3GPP/Jakes channels from other bands, single ROI radar events, amplitude/spectrum variance alone, or aggressively using future frames cannot replace independent field evidence.

Even an exact match to marginal distributions and the fraction of time unavailable cannot qualify recovery. The executed independent two-state calculation holds the pointwise outage fraction 10% and conditional gain PDFs fixed; at one-second granularity, increasing `Pr(off next | off now)` from .2 to .9 to .98 gives `Pr(16 consecutive off from a stationary start)` of 3.28×10⁻¹², 0.0206 and 0.0739, respectively. The mean off episode changes from 1.25 s to 10 s to 50 s; this is not an empirical storm or receiver probability. Choose fade-duration and recovery criteria on matched independent event episodes, censor thresholds/available episodes honestly, and use one-factor and joint matched cases. A conditional fitted state process must earn inference beyond repeated windows in one event via source-identified clusters and physically comparable holdout state, relevant ACF/PSD and threshold/duration state-conditioned errors.
