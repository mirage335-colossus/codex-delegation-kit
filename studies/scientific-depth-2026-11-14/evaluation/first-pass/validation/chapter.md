# Validation science for channel appraisal and the complete receiver

## Decision handoff

Use an independently calibrated receive waveform and truth record to evaluate the complete receiver, with paired candidate/baseline runs on the same immutable raw samples. Name every level reference plane, bandwidth, polarization, timing/frequency convention and calibration uncertainty. Calibrate the injected/measured channel independently from receiver thresholds and keep ground truth inaccessible to operational receiver decisions. Count incomplete inputs and failed/helper outcomes in unconditional service endpoints; report conditional scores separately.

Treat an independent channel episode (or a genuinely independent field session) as the unit for failure-rate inference. Do not count adjacent frames, slots, seeds that share evolving channel state, or repeated receiver hypotheses as independent Bernoulli trials. Define fixed evaluation horizons and success criteria before measuring. If an endpoint is not reached before a declared horizon, classify it as a failure for the fixed-horizon endpoint; when estimating time to recovery, retain the observation as right-censored and state the censoring assumption. Report time-weighted availability separately from episode-weighted success, with explicit regimes and episode durations.

Keep two evidence lanes. **Representative profiles** estimate performance only for an explicitly sampled and documented population. **Stress profiles** probe a physical mechanism or known boundary and make no environmental prevalence claim. Retain joint measured behavior; marginal quantiles do not establish a joint severe channel. Distinguish receiver benefit on held-out measured waveforms from benefit on synthetic channel outputs and from model fit to channel observables.

No product calibration fixture, deployment duration, allowed error probability, or receiver acceptance probability was supplied. These must be specified by the owner before claiming calibrated physical levels or a field reliability target. The currently provided hard service point is complete-receiver processing time below one second per input second, measured on the full demanding workload; it is a capacity criterion, not a link reliability probability. The receiver source contract says missing-sample helper outcomes can be ambiguous with valid signal-only outage/noise, so use unnotched valid-noise quiet evidence in changing conditions and keep incomplete-input outcomes in a separate unconditional category.

## 1. Match measured quantities before comparing receiver decisions

### 1.1 Name the receiver reference plane and measurand

ITU-R P.372 defines the overall operating noise reference at the input of an equivalent loss-free receiving antenna, explicitly because the antenna terminal is a notional reference. It separates external noise from antenna-circuit, transmission-line and receiver contributions and includes their losses and temperatures in the system noise factor. Its available-noise-power convention is (P_n=F_a+B-204\) dBW at (T_0=290\) K, with (B=10\log_{10}(b/\mathrm{Hz})\). This is a useful physical bookkeeping convention for an RF link, but does not map ADC units to antenna-input units by itself. The transfer from that antenna reference to the ADC must be measured for the actual antenna/coupling, matching network, cable, filters, gain state, sample format and device.

Record at minimum: frequency and occupied/analysis bandwidth; available power versus delivered power and the impedance/reference plane; antenna factor/effective height or measured transfer response; polarization and orientation; gain and attenuation state; ADC full scale, code scale and clipping rule; sample rate and clock error; receiver passband and equivalent noise bandwidth; temperature; calibration-source/attenuator/sensor identities and calibration dates; and the uncertainty/covariance inputs. For dBFS/Hz, say which full-scale peak or RMS convention and window/PSD estimator was used. Keep dBm/Hz and dBFS/Hz as distinct measurements unless their transfer calibration is explicitly applied.

For noise, distinguish (C/N_0) from in-band SNR. Under flat, white noise and a specified equivalent noise bandwidth (B), (S/N=C/N_0-10\log_{10}B\). At 290 K, the conventional thermal density is about −173.98 dBm/Hz, so the thermal contribution over 2.4 kHz is about −140.18 dBm. This is a reference calculation, not an environmental noise prediction: antenna/coupling losses, external noise and non-white/intermittent interference can dominate. ITU-R P.372-15 states that noise power is seldom enough to predict performance except for white Gaussian background noise; it calls for probabilistic waveform descriptions and describes amplitude exceedance probability and, for high-frequency impulsive cases, pulse levels/counts. Thus a noise-floor scalar alone cannot validate an impulsive, colored or bursty interference model. The 2026 P.372-18 is now in force, but its 30,437,927-byte PDF was rejected by the web reader as too large; detailed equation/paragraph evidence here is from the superseded 2021 P.372-15, while P.372-18 edition status is verified separately. Recheck body details in the current edition before implementation.

Calibrate the channel independently from the receiver: use traceable known levels and frequencies for the source/capture chain, measure the receive transfer across the useful band and the actual filter/antenna chain, measure noise with source-off plus a known termination/reference source, and retain clipping, compression, AGC and recovery transitions. For a nonlinear front end or out-of-band blocker, the test input must be the complete composite waveform before that front end. Matching a baseband SNR after filtering can erase blocker-induced compression, intermodulation or ADC clipping. Use a separate check source/level not used to fit the calibration, and preserve the raw capture and calibration record together.

An independent absolute calibration does not mean unrelated candidate and baseline waveforms. For receiver comparison, feed both versions the exact same captured samples, masks, timestamps, public calendar and initial configuration. This paired design removes channel realization as a variance source and makes the observed delta interpretable. It does not remove absolute calibration uncertainty. If both measured levels share a common additive calibration error, it cancels from their difference but shifts both absolute levels; the desk check demonstrates this invariant. Preserve the calibration covariance, because shared standards, attenuators, clock references and receive paths induce correlated errors. JCGM 100 §5.2 explicitly requires covariance terms for correlated inputs and notes that using a common measurement standard can correlate estimates.

### 1.2 Keep physical truth independent of receiver evidence

Use two linked artifacts: (1) an immutable capture or generated sample stream plus publicly available protocol configuration; and (2) a separately access-controlled truth sidecar with channel realization, intended DATA payload, arrival/delay/frequency/polarization truth, path/noise parameters, environmental labels, and sample validity. The receiver and operational admission/scoring path must not read the sidecar. A separate evaluation tool can join records after the receiver emits its outputs. Store cryptographic hashes, source revision, waveform/profile/config hashes, independent seed roots and mapping version so candidate and baseline are demonstrably matched.

For synthetic tests, the generated truth is useful only if measured observables are independently checked. Before a receiver run, compare actual output against declared channel observables: received useful-signal level, delay/frequency trajectory, Doppler spread, polarization mixing where relevant, noise PSD and exceedance/APD, blocker level at the front end, filter response, clipping counts and tail/recovery length. Use a separate calibration set to fit model parameters and a channel-episode holdout to test them. A clean match in a model's own summary fields is not independent validation.

For physical data, keep raw samples and metadata, then freeze the evaluation record before comparing receiver versions. Separate waveform data used to tune thresholds/priors, data used to select algorithms, and held-out sessions used for confirmation. Split by whole path/session/site/day or ionospheric event, not random neighboring windows: adjacent observations share channel state and hardware drift, which would make leakage look like generalization. Include failures, missing captures, invalid timestamps and restarts in the cohort log.

## 2. Endpoints, dependence, rare failures and censoring

### 2.1 Predefine what “success” means and over what horizon

An estimate needs a declared measurand and decision rule. For each receiver profile specify a fixed initial state (epoch/ordinal/key/waveform), complete input span, expected delayed tail, required valid quiet span, deadline, and outcome definition. Report separate endpoints for:

* bit/frame integrity among complete decoded DATA;
* false admission or wrong-address/bit acceptance per complete quiet/noise challenge;
* premature completion when valid DATA resumes after a delay;
* missed/corrected DATA, erasure/helper, and reacquisition within the declared horizon;
* complete receiver runtime and peak workspace for all accepted input, including preparation, overlap and scheduled quiet work.

Give both the unconditional distribution over all eligible input episodes and conditional performance where the receiver produced a complete score. Do not drop missing/invalid episodes from the unconditional result. The source contract documents that invalid samples can force helper/incomplete status, whereas a physically silent or faded but valid waveform may contain noise and complete quiet is part of the end condition. Those are materially different observable states. A falsification test should hold the payload and channel fixed while varying only the validity mask, then separately vary physical signal amplitude to create a valid fade; an outcome classifier that conflates them is not reliable.

For time-varying environments, a block-success probability, fraction of elapsed time available, outage duration distribution, and probability of reacquisition by a deadline are different quantities. Define time-weighted availability as successful valid time divided by the total observed eligible time under a named operating profile; do not replace it with an unweighted average over equal-count episodes drawn from unequal durations. Conversely, an event-sampled challenge suite estimates event-conditional performance, not natural time availability. Report both when both matter. For nonstationary profiles, define the time origin, duration, transition schedule/trajectory, and whether thresholds are evaluated per epoch, per event, or over the full run. Keep quiet baseline, slowly varying, abrupt transition, disturbed and high-latitude holdouts distinct. Do not average these strata into an opaque “all conditions” score.

ITU-R P.533-14 (the ITU page still lists this 2019 edition in force as of this search) illustrates why endpoint conventions matter: its HF monthly medians and day-to-day/intra-hour deciles feed a basic-circuit reliability definition, and its digital reliability calculation uses time/frequency windows and a required SNR for a specified service criterion. It is a long-term circuit prediction method, not a ground-truth stochastic channel realization for the modem's millisecond-to-hour sequence. Reuse its observables and relevant windows only where assumptions match; validate short-term trajectories against actual measured episodes.

### 2.2 Use the right independent unit

The receiver's thousands of adjacent frames/slots in one evolving realization are repeated measurements, not thousands of independent environments. The natural unit for external failure probability is a separately generated/recorded channel episode, with independent seed domains or independent field sessions. For runtime precision, multiple runs in one fixed machine/input condition can be useful paired repeats, but they do not increase the count of independent channel/fade episodes. Preserve cluster identifiers (site, session, day, path, event, seed family) in every output. Bootstrap or resample at the highest independent cluster, not at the frame level.

For approximately stationary within-episode data, time-series estimators may use long blocks or model a dependence structure; do not use an IID interval over all slots. Künsch's original stationary-sequence bootstrap result constructs blocks for general stationary observations and explicitly assumes a stationary sequence/asymptotic block conditions. A quiet-to-storm transition or a one-off disturbance is not stationary over the whole record. Prefer independent episode-level comparisons or report each nonstationary trajectory as a stress case; use block resampling only within locally defensible stationary segments.

### 2.3 Bound rare failures with exact intervals, not zero-failure rhetoric

If (n) independent eligible episodes have zero failures, the one-sided exact 95% Clopper–Pearson upper confidence limit for a Bernoulli failure probability is (1-0.05^{1/n}). This gives 9.50% at (n=30), 2.95% at (n=100), 0.994% at (n=300), and 0.299% at (n=1000). To bound a failure probability below 1%, 0.1%, or 0.01% with zero observed failures requires 299, 2,995, or 29,956 independent trials respectively. These are useful planning values, not the modem's target failure rates; the owner has not specified such rates. Clopper–Pearson is conservative/exact for the binomial setting. If failures occur, report a suitable exact interval and all numerators/denominators rather than using a normal approximation near zero. A long capture with strongly dependent windows does not acquire the confidence of thousands of independent trials.

As an illustrative dependence sensitivity, an AR(1) sequence with lag-one correlation \(\rho=0.9\) and (n=1000) has mean-estimator effective sample size near 53 under the finite-sample variance formula, not 1000. This is not a universal failure-rate correction; it demonstrates that the independence assumption can change uncertainty by an order of magnitude. Estimate dependence from episode/time records and design the analysis accordingly. The script records this formula and output.

### 2.4 Censoring is outcome information

If a fixed-horizon evaluation ends before recovery, score “not recovered by deadline” as a binary endpoint failure (while retaining its actual time-to-event bound). If the study estimates time-to-recovery beyond varied observation cutoffs, record right censoring and censor reasons; Kaplan–Meier's product-limit estimator was developed for incomplete lifetimes, and its assumptions require scrutiny when event time may relate to potential loss-to-observation time. It is unsafe to treat a censored episode as recovered at its cutoff or delete it. If stopping is caused by receiver lock loss, observed bad channel, dropped capture or a manual abort triggered by poor performance, censoring may be informative; ordinary Kaplan–Meier is then biased unless modeled/stratified with suitable assumptions. Report restricted mean time-to-recovery up to the common horizon if that directly answers the operational question. In every case keep both the event indicator and censoring reason.

## 3. Physical models and the limits of reference standards

### 3.1 Do not use one model across disjoint regimes

The official ITU-R P.368-10 ground-wave method is explicitly for 10 kHz–30 MHz, near-surface terminals, a smooth homogeneous spherical Earth, a short vertical source, and a vertical far-field component; the recommendation says the method is generally for cases with negligible ionospheric reflections and not for receivers well above Earth. It supplies an important reference calculation for the approximately 1 MHz groundwave branch, but it is not a field calibration and it does not cover sub-9 kHz (below its frequency floor), 30 MHz skywave, or 5.8 GHz Earth–Moon–Earth. Use that reference to test the groundwave path-loss/field-strength convention and compare against measured paths with their actual ground/antenna conditions. Preserve any residuals; do not tune the model and declare agreement on the same paths.

Likewise, use HF prediction standards as circuit-level checks only where their assumptions/observable definitions line up. P.533's SNR, delay, frequency spread, and reliability concepts give useful reference quantities, while transient high-latitude/disturbed channel time histories need measured or otherwise independently justified episode data. Receiver evaluation should preserve the actual complex composite samples so that phase, multipath, fading, common drift, polarization and interference co-occurrence remain observable. Combining separate marginal worst cases can create a physically impossible joint channel; ignoring dependence can also miss a real coincident failure. Source-to-channel calibration should target joint, time-aligned quantities where downstream receiver decisions depend on co-occurrence.

For VLF electrically-small antennas and coupling, use the correct antenna/receiver transfer and reference plane instead of importing P.368 beyond its range. For 5.8 GHz EME, record both-hop/round-trip timing/frequency geometry, polarization, antenna/system gains and losses, echo level uncertainty and frontend noise/interference at the receiver. These details should be supplied by the respective science/model owners; this branch does not certify a propagation law. In both regimes, the same statistical requirements apply: independently calibrate, retain raw complex waveform, pair receiver comparisons, and hold out complete events/paths.

### 3.2 Vary conventions as a sensitivity, not a hidden knob

Document and sensitivity-check at least: received power plane (antenna equivalent-loss-free input, antenna terminals, post-filter, or ADC); reference impedance and mismatch; peak/RMS amplitude; one-sided/two-sided PSD; sample rate and FFT/window normalization; occupied versus noise-equivalent bandwidth; noise PSD versus integrated noise; (C/N_0) versus in-band SNR; frequency offset sign/reference clock; path delay relative to direct path; polarization basis and cross-polar loss; and whether channel gain is per-ray amplitude or total power normalized. Sweep plausible metrology uncertainty/correction alternatives from measured calibration information, not arbitrary broad ranges. Report which decisions are invariant and which flip near the receiver's decision boundary.

Avoid re-normalizing every receiver input to a target SNR after the channel/front end: that can erase a true change in path loss, coupling, blocker compression or noise. If a controlled link-margin sweep is desired, vary source power upstream in calibrated increments while holding noise and frontend state fixed, and preserve the resulting measurements. If a dimensionless normalized waveform is used, retain its explicit physical conversion separately and label it synthetic.

## 4. Discriminating validation design

### 4.1 Separate channel-model validation from receiver validation

**Channel calibration/validation:** on a calibration set, identify independent correction terms for source level, coupling/antenna factor, passband, noise density, timing/frequency reference and frontend behavior. Freeze these. On distinct holdout captures, compare complex transfer/impulse response, received level, frequency and delay trajectories, polarization response, noise PSD/APD, clipping, and recovery. Include independent fixture references/check standards. Report uncertainty budget, traceability, repeatability/reproducibility and covariance. A channel model failing its own holdout should not be used to claim receiver robustness.

**Receiver comparison:** run baseline and candidate receiver on the same immutable raw stream and metadata, separately reset state, blind the truth sidecar until outputs are committed, and randomize execution order if hardware temperature/adaptation can drift. Make primary comparisons paired by episode. Report deltas and intervals for complete-payload success, false admission, completion/reacquisition, erasure/helper rate, wrong address and full complete-receiver cost. Preserve counterexamples and outputs for every disagreement. A confidence interval crossing zero means “benefit not established”; it does not establish equivalence. If asserting practical equivalence, prespecify an engineering margin and demonstrate the interval for the paired delta lies inside it using an equivalence design (two one-sided tests is one recognized method). The margin must be linked to downstream service or correctness, not selected after seeing outcomes.

### 4.2 Use matched, controlled discriminators

1. **Known calibration levels and independent check levels:** sweep source level and offset across calibrated steps, but hold back a subset of frequencies/levels as a check set. This detects calibration curvature and scale errors. Repeat after gain/filter/temperature changes.
2. **Noise/interference type:** compare white Gaussian baseline, colored external noise, man-made noise-like traces, impulsive/APD-controlled noise and narrowband/blocker cases. Hold total integrated power fixed in one contrast to reveal waveform-statistic sensitivity, then separately hold source PSD/physical level fixed. Record any level-, AGC- or clipping-dependent behavior.
3. **Co-occurring channel dynamics:** test one-factor references (level only, static delay, drift, Doppler, polarization) then paired joint episodes where measured data support co-occurrence. Keep each regime's range, time scale and provenance visible. “Worst of each margin” is a stress construction unless measured jointly.
4. **Quiet, missing input and physical outage:** compare complete-valid noise-only tails, masked/erased chunks, full fade/recovery trajectories, and delayed valid DATA resumption. These falsify early quiet completion or missing-evidence classification errors.
5. **Architecture/model ablations:** compare existing fixed/coherent lane, varying/AR/drift alternatives, and any proposal stage with the same output decision path and the same holdout. Retain exact public hypotheses/calendar behavior and all candidate traces. Attribute gains to new model evidence, candidate proposal, arithmetic, or resource routing separately.
6. **Full capacity and correctness:** use the demanding complete receiver path and actual maximum sample/workspace conditions, with DATA, helper/overlap, quiet and recovery. Report full elapsed CPU wall time per input second, preparation/cleanup, peak live memory/workspaces and counters for rejected/backpressured/failed work; pair with bit-exact/service correctness and bound checks. The owner states <1 s/input-s is the project hard point. Fast GPU/coarse or clean/easy-only gains cannot establish this complete-path criterion.

For every discriminator, state what observation would change the decision. Examples: a calibrated level shifts the supported boundary outside uncertainty; channel-holdout residuals show a systematically missing delay/frequency dependence; a new receiver lowers paired false admissions without trading correct recovery, while staying below the complete-path cost limit; or a result disappears after group-level holdout, demonstrating leakage. Keep screening outcomes separate from final holdout outcomes.

## 5. Provenance and reproducibility contract

Every captured/generated episode should carry source-revision identity; binary/build/flags and receiver/profile names; channel and frontend config; data-generator version; independent root seed(s); waveform version and keyed input identity (in the protected sidecar); sample rate/format/channel count; all timing and validity masks; hardware/serial/firmware/clock and calibration IDs; location/antenna orientation/polarization and dates for field data; temperature and setup photos/notes or fixture protocol; raw file hash; transformations and filter coefficients; trial order/state reset; outcome and reason; censoring marker/deadline; and exact command/runner plus stdout/stderr/log hash. Preserve machine-readable raw observations, not only aggregate plots. Store policy labels/parameters separately from measured outcome labels.

Pre-register endpoint definitions, failure categories, practical margin, trial unit, inclusion/exclusion and stop rules before held-out validation. Keep a permanent holdout inaccessible during tuning, and version it rather than silently reusing it after changes. For public model/reference products record exact edition, retrieval date, page/section/table/figure and assumption match. Label any synthetic worst-case profile as stress. Publish negative cases and incomplete/censored runs so the next analysis cannot silently convert them to successes.

## 6. Reproducible desk calculations

The checked-in `desk_checks.py` and `desk_checks.txt` are independent statistical/metrology illustrations, not product execution. The script uses only Python's standard library and no pump data. It computes: exact one-sided 95% zero-failure bounds; sample size needed for selected illustrative bounds; finite-(n) AR(1) effective sample size (n/[1+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(1-k/n)\rho^k]); (kT_0) bandwidth conversion; and cancellation of a shared additive level calibration error in a paired delta. Commands and observed outputs are retained in the output files. No assertions, pump tests, build, receiver run or qualification was performed in this branch.

The binomial results assume independent identically distributed Bernoulli episodes drawn from a stated target population. The AR(1) example is only an illustration for a mean, not a general correction for dependent failure events. The noise calculation assumes white thermal noise at 290 K and equivalent noise bandwidth as stated. The shared-error calculation assumes the same additive dB correction applies to both systems. These assumptions are not claims about actual modem data.
