I also examined an accessible university-repository copy of the 2025 spectral-riometer absorption paper, specifically its discussion of deviations from the `f^-2` power law and low-frequency terrestrial-noise/geometry confounds.

The published F.1487 7 ms/30 Hz test row and separate 5 ms/25 Hz high-latitude occurrence indications are indeed distinct. The original norm says the profiles are comparative test conditions; §5 states the two separate approximately 5% marginal thresholds. It does not give the joint exceedance. Independently recalculated `B=2σ=30 Hz`: FWHM `35.322301 Hz`, central 80% Gaussian width `38.446547 Hz`, and ideal Gaussian gain e-fold `15.005272 ms`. The chapter's analytic formulas and values are correct. `cΔτ` gives `149.896 km` for 0.5 ms and `2098.547 km` for 7 ms; the text correctly labels these path-length differences, not reflection heights. These checks validate transformations only; they do not substitute for a path-loss calculation or field-data check.

Web access followed the assigned `review-hf` meter recipe: 24/24 allowed operations, including failed opens, were metered. Opened primary pages were F.1487, Stocker, Bland, Ghaly, Marew, P.1147-4 and Dear. Search also surfaced the 2024 spectral-riometer absorption analysis and QAN–ALE public dataset. The QAN search result confirms the DOI and repository presence, but I did not download or analyze its archive. Do not represent that as an executed dataset discriminator. Bounded source/calculation review used 11 of 20 shell calls; no project build, pump execution, or source write was made.

## Six domain-depth checks

1. **Mechanisms, regimes, observables and conditions — Partial.** Strong high-level separation of circuit level, waveform channels, absorption, outage, and modal geometry. Concrete coverage is uneven: high-latitude Stocker and storm references are better specified than midlatitude, NVIS, low-HF and equatorial communication channels. The 1 MHz edge is described as unsupported even though P.1147 has a valid in-domain planning method below 1.7 MHz; 1.7–2 MHz is the actual standards gap.
2. **Foundational and recent source discovery — Partial.** Live source ledger has authoritative F.1487/P.533 and useful 2013/2018/2024/2025 primary articles. It records some full-text/abstract access status, but no preserved search query/backward-forward discovery trail and omits decision-relevant accessible primary cohorts in the frozen corpus (Watterson 1969/NTIA tables, DAMSON/NRL, NVIS Java and 2021 O/X, and direct equatorial HF). The new Marew 2025 paper is a useful recent geophysical discriminator but not a communications-channel result. I found an additional 2025 absorption-frequency study in the bounded review search; its discussion flags possible departures from `f^-2` and terrestrial/geometry effects below about 20 MHz. It should be checked before using riometer absorption to calibrate low-HF link loss. No blanket statement that further science is absent is warranted.
3. **Competing methods, alternatives and counterevidence — Partial.** Strongly distinguishes F.1487 stationary Gaussian taps from the code's deterministic ray spacing, separates outage from fading, recognizes path-conditioned high-latitude behavior and avoids interpreting marginal percentile thresholds as joint. But there is no comparison of P.533 printed/text vs official implementation loss, no P.1147 alternative at MF, and little competing empirical channel law (e.g. direct HF spread-F and high-latitude scatter). The model-identity distinction is strong; the alternative appraisal remains incomplete.
4. **Quantitative interpretation and uncertainty — Partial.** Definitions, the Gaussian-width math and path-length conversion are sound. Source conditions for Stocker and Bland are described, and `hf_calcs.py` is reproducible. Missing are a path loss/calibrated receiver-plane result, uncertainty/error range for a specific link prediction, quantitative low-HF day/night or absorption curve, quantitative midlatitude channel cases, and source-matched fade/coherence statistics. The calculation checks algebra, not propagation truth.
5. **Executed independent discriminators — Partial.** The independent conversion recalc passed. The source ledger identifies several public sources, but live work includes no archive parser, raw I/Q/QAN check, independent paper-table reconstruction, or matched denominator sensitivity. In bounded review, Dear et al. identify Zenodo data and measured scalar range; direct DOI data landing page access failed through the web tool. The QAN–ALE source paper states its scalar archive is public, but I did not download/reprocess it under this review. Ghaly et al. full-text methods demonstrate direct signal-loss/CNA coincidence with explicit schedule ambiguity; live branch did not execute an event/data check. Report those limitations rather than imply the web error establishes unavailability.
6. **Decision relevance and next discriminator — Partial.** The recommendation to keep present stresses as controlled tests, expose a small F.1487 mode table, separate link loss/availability, delay full-wave modeling, and use paired falsifiers is connected to the project abstraction and appropriately conditional. It is not yet fully decision-ready because measured source-bound inputs are not carried through a path reference or tested against a representative channel/outage trace. A corrected research result should state which conditional model cases have adequate evidence and which need a fresh local capture/calibration.
