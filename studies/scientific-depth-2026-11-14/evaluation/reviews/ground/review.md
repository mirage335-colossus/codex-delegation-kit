# Independent groundwave depth review

## Review identity and scope

This review applies the frozen `GROUND-01`–`GROUND-11` rows in `evaluation/coverage-matrix.json` and the six depth checks in `evaluation/acceptance.md` to the frozen first-pass groundwave artifacts and live `research/groundwave/`. It does not revise criteria. The source corpus is pumpModem revision `13db435fc85114be2e2a0298580d5c3e2bcba996`; the study/kit base is `08fc5207af39b9dd728d83ffeaadd4d1b4815653`. The complete candidate chapter is 143 lines; comparison used the frozen `evaluation/first-pass/groundwave/` and its source ledger. Their SHA-256 hashes match the live chapter and ledger (`ea5ff482…bccf925` and `19a9a3e1…db9f7` respectively). The only observed text delta is the check-count phrase at chapter line 136: frozen “three” versus live “four”; it is clerical and does not change the evidence. The live script/output are separate supporting artifacts.

The review confirms the official P.368 reference convention, independently recomputes its 1-km normalization and the geometry screens, inspects the full official NTIA TR-84-151 PDF at the decisive tables/discussion, inspects Aono–Muramatsu's original 1955 mixed-path article, and checks official P.832 atlas wording. P.527 direct-PDF access failed; it is preserved below. The review also uses the original corpus map and its source locators to compare breadth, not to treat bibliography or headings as evidence. No pump build, production source access, propagation binary, modem, receiver, measurement, or physical validation was run.

## Six depth checks

1. **Mechanisms/regimes/observables — Partial overall.** Basic 1-vs-30 MHz mode distinctions are sound. Mixed coast, MF night arrival and rough-sea temporal/scattered observables remain incomplete; several 30-MHz competing mode families are absent.
2. **Foundational and recent primary discovery — Partial.** P.368/P.527/Millington/Aono, NTIA 1984, Fockens 2022 abstract, Hehenkamp 2024, and Valčić 2026 make a reasonable foundation-to-recent spread. Independent source checks confirm the decisive P.368, NTIA and Aono claims. But primary 1.242-MHz observations, 30-MHz coast-height work, rough-sea sources, NTIA Phase 1 archive, and some coexisting-mode observations in the corpus are not examined. Fockens is explicitly abstract-only, appropriately bounded.
3. **Alternatives and counterevidence — Partial.** Candidate compares P.368/LFMF, Millington, terrain standards, full-wave families and site models, and preserves NTIA/Aono/Fockens disagreement. The alternatives are not generally exercised under matched inputs, and useful empirical competitors are omitted.
4. **Quantitative interpretation/uncertainty — Partial.** The normalization, amplitude/power convention, σ/(ωε) and geometry arithmetic are auditable, and source transfer is qualified. There is no material-to-field/loss sensitivity or antenna-plane/noise link calculation; the P.832 atlas frequency statement is incorrect. Site/season and model uncertainty are not propagated through an illustrative path result.
5. **Executed discriminators — Partial.** The four script checks were rerun/recomputed in scope and arithmetic is valid. They check conventions/constitutive and geometry scales, not the central path attenuation or mixed-route questions. NTIA and Aono were inspected in source form in this review, but not independently processed into a new candidate discriminator; future survey/calculation proposals remain plans.
6. **Decision relevance and next discriminator — Partial.** Native C++ integration and staged physical/receiver validation are concrete and appropriately distinguished. A source-backed, matched-input desk comparator should precede the proposed pump integration, and the corrected 1-MHz atlas statement should alter soil-input guidance.

2. Compute and report a small P.368 homogeneous-earth field/loss grid at 1 and 30 MHz with declared frequency, range, σ, ε, height and method/revision; compare sea/ordinary/dry material or equivalent evidence. This distinguishes the correct 1-km normalization from substantive attenuation and material sensitivity.
3. Execute one bounded mixed-path comparison against the accessible Aono–Muramatsu figures/table or near-band Nishioka profile, preserving unknown gain and relative-only observables; compute forward/reverse Millington using stated segment inputs and explain fit/disagreement.
4. Add one conditional antenna-plane link arithmetic example and one 1-MHz night-arrival/crossover reference with exact observable, path, antenna and time caveats. Do not claim a universal complex channel.
5. For the applied marine/30-MHz branches, compare rough-sea coherent mean versus fluctuating scatter, then use an available adjacent-band coast/terrain or competing-mode observation as a clearly bounded discriminator. Keep long calibrated 30-MHz marine complex-path qualification as an external boundary.

**Domain judgment: Partial.** Basic mechanism framing, standards scope, adverse 28-MHz field evidence, careful limitations, and implementation planning are supported. The wrong 1-MHz atlas assertion plus feasible missing material-path, mixed-path and band-specific empirical calculations prevent declaring the essential set or all six depth dimensions satisfied.
