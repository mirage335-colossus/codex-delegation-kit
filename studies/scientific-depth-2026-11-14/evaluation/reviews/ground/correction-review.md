# Independent review of the groundwave correction

## Scope and preservation

This is a supplemental review of the participant's post-freeze correction in `research/groundwave/chapter.md:143-188`, its updated source ledger/acquisition log, and `tmp/LFMF/model_sweep.cpp/.txt`. The initial `review.md` and `dispositions.json` remain unchanged and record the original **Partial** result. Frozen criteria remain `GROUND-01`–`GROUND-11`; no criterion was added or relaxed. I examined the official NTIA LFMF source package acquisition log and source files, then independently compiled the caller in this review directory and replayed the output. I also independently wrote and ran a second small caller for the mixed-path equations. No pump code/build, modem, device, route calibration or measurement was used.

The correction directly closes two initial omissions: it reports actual LFMF field curves at 1 and 30 MHz over explicit soil/sea and distance inputs; and it computes forward, reverse, reciprocal and shifted-boundary Millington outputs. The corrected P.832-4 statement is consistent with the official MF map standardization to 1 MHz. The LFMF outputs are model results for declared hypothetical homogeneous segments, not field observations. The numerical results show that input choice matters strongly; they do not identify the ground on an unspecified deployment route.

## Independent numerical/source replay

The acquired repository is NTIA/LFMF commit `57886e9d0a29fd8f04a6416dc3f782322f5a3589`; its public source reports project version 1.1. The acquisition log records the selected upstream blob IDs. I checked all nine copied header/source files against those Git blob IDs: each local file has exactly one additional final LF from text serialization, while removing that byte gives the recorded upstream blob ID. No source-body difference was found. The API implementation confirms inputs are metres, MHz, W, N-units, km, relative permittivity, S/m and polarization; it returns `E_dBuVm`, basic transmission loss, a receive-power estimate and method. It applies a 4.77 dBi reference transmit and receive gain when forming the respective reported field and receive power. Its code validator accepts 0–50 m heights, 0.01–30 MHz, 0.001–10,000 km, `Ns=250–400`, `εr≥1`, `σ>0`; these numerical limits do not override the P.368 physical conditions.

I compiled the locally saved source files plus the candidate's exact `model_sweep.cpp` using g++ 14.2, C++11 and the two CMake-derived string definitions, into `evaluation/reviews/ground/replay/LFMF/model_sweep`. The output was byte-for-byte identical to the participant's saved `model_sweep.txt`. The source uses P.368/LFMF's flat-Earth-curve correction below `80/f_MHz^(1/3)` km and residue series above that switch (80 km at 1 MHz, about 25.8 km at 30 MHz); this is a numerical branch switch inside the smooth-Earth model, not a physical path-mode transition.

A separate reviewer-written C++ caller recomputed the mixed route at the same 1/30-MHz frequencies, 100-km length, `Ns=301`, vertical polarization, 0-m endpoints, 1-kW input, and land/sea pairs. It evaluates the homogeneous section fields and the documented Annex-2 forward/reverse log-field interpolation, then averages those dB field estimates. All six split/order outputs matched the candidate to 0.001 dB. At the 50/50 split, reciprocal estimates are 48.917 dBµV/m (1 MHz) and 12.972 dBµV/m (30 MHz); reversing route order swaps the one-way estimates but preserves that reciprocal result by construction. The 10/90, 50/50 and 90/10 splits produce markedly different fields. This is a valid same-input desk discriminator for route order and boundary placement within Millington's formula; it is not an empirical shoreline fit or a test of sharp-boundary physics.

The homogeneous results are internally consistent and their implications are large: for 100 km, the dry-to-sea spread is 39.145 dB at 1 MHz and 52.354 dB at 30 MHz; at 1 km it is 4.645 and 31.431 dB, respectively. Sea equal endpoint heights moved from 0 to 30 m alter the model field by only 0.026 dB at 1 MHz/10 km and 3.914 dB at 30 MHz/10 km (3.760 dB at 30 MHz/100 km). These are conditional model sensitivities. The unusually long ideal 1-MHz dry/sea cases and 30-MHz/100-km case cannot be converted to usable radio range without the P.368 conditions, terrain/mode competition, antenna/feed calibration, noise and local ground inputs.
