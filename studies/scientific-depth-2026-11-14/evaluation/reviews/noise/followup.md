- **Historical tail convention and physical model.** The NIST-hosted full Beckmann article is now directly accessible at `https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/68D/jresv68Dn6p723_A1b.pdf`. It defines its measured APD as an instantaneous *envelope* exceedance (PDF lines 25–30), describes spikes lasting milliseconds (47–50), and derives a Poisson-arrival / spike / lognormal-plus-Rayleigh-vector model (110–128, 189–217, 313–378). That supports using the old 113 kHz / Boulder / 1958 APD as a historical tail illustration and model alternative only. It does not make it transferable to 6 MHz or the modem's 225 Hz width. The continuation correctly labels signed-real Gaussian and complex-Gaussian Rayleigh calculations separately; these numbers use different RMS conventions and must not be read as two matched realizations of one normalized Gaussian. The exact plotted +22/+40 dB values are ledgered as approximate readings of Fig. 5; I did not independently digitize that figure in this follow-up.
- **Dependence and combination.** The script's two-equal-component identity was independently recomputed: `Psum/P=2(1+rho)` gives +3.010, +4.771, and +6.021 dB for `rho=0, .5, 1`, and −6.990 dB for `rho=−.9`. This closes the previously noted missing *dependence sensitivity* calculation. It still does not settle the corpus-specific distinction between P.372's reference/quantile combination approximation and a literal positive-power component sum, including the reference example where their ordering differs.
- **Measured occupancy against the modem scale.** I reopened the Cyprus primary text: §3 (web-readable lines 151–198) defines allocation-conditioned Q as the percentage of 1 kHz channel RMS values above threshold, sampled for 100 ms, with the full HF sweep under an hour; §4 (207–228) describes day/night and usable-window dependence. The continuation correctly compares 1 kHz with 225 Hz (4.444×; 6.478 dB integrated-power difference only under flat PSD), and does not infer 16 s modem slot loss. ITU-R SM.2256-1 directly supports the measurement warning: §3.1 (PDF lines 432–457) discusses filter selectivity/RBW and adjacent-channel contamination; §§3.2–3.4 (458–540) discuss overload and threshold/noise estimation. The 22.5 Hz RBW is a conditional measurement-design calculation from the report's one-tenth spacing guidance, not a modem-detector requirement.
- **Filter, clock and chain implications.** The continuation's 960 sample/s, 225 Hz, 16 s scale checks independently reproduce: 112.5-to-480 Hz is 2.093 octaves and the idealized 60 dB / 6 dB-per-pole estimate is 4.78 poles; 1 ppm is 16 µs or 0.01536 samples, while 100 ppm is 1.6 ms or 1.536 samples. These are labeled planning scales, not hardware specifications. The owner report supplies a separate deterministic clipping/order desk example: at 12 kS/s, a 1 kHz blocker ahead of hard clipping reduced a 37 Hz wanted peak from 0.08 to 0.04/0.01333 for blocker peaks 1.5/3.0, while pre-clipping rejection preserves it (`results/owner.md:41`). It is explicitly synthetic, not measured-radio behavior. Together with the noise chapter's reciprocal-mixing source, the receiver/native sections' nuisance and front-end limits, and the lead's calibrated-blocker acquisition discriminator, the generic blocker/order gap is materially improved; target-radio behavior remains uncalibrated.
