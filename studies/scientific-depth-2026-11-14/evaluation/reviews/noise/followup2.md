## Independent source and calculation checks

### P.372 combination rule and the sign ambiguity

The metered official-text search for P.372-17 §7.1 returned the definitions of the half-normal dB deviations and the moment-matched lognormal-sum equations: the median and spread equations (18–20), `alpha_i` in equation (21), `beta_T` in (22), and decile deviations in (23–24). The parsed equation (21) has a **minus** sign in `exp(F_ami/c − sigma_i²/(2c²))`. P.372-15's reported equation (19) has a **plus** sign. The follow-up calculator correctly uses the P.372-15 expression only as an explicitly labeled older-method comparison; it does not quietly substitute it for P.372-17 or P.372-18.

I independently applied the displayed equations to one component with median 0 dB and sigma 9 dB. With the P.372-15 plus sign, equations (18–22) preserve the input median at 0 dB. Literal use of the P.372-17 parsed minus sign returns −18.651 dB while retaining sigma 9 dB. This is an internal self-consistency warning for the parsed equation, **not a finding that the normative PDF contains an error**: the official P.372-17 screenshot attempts at the relevant pages return HTTP 404, and the P.372-15 PDF fetch is over the reader's size limit. The response appropriately preserves that failure and recommends no implementation until visually verified from an authorized official copy or correction.

For two independent equal-median lognormal power sources with 9 dB Gaussian spread in dB, I reproduced the saved seeded Monte Carlo and P.372-15 moment calculation: simulated sum median/Q90 = 5.797/15.091 dB; moment approximation = 4.486/15.068 dB; sum of the separate component Q90 powers = 14.544 dB. With perfectly common dB variation and the same marginals, total Q90 is 14.544 dB, 0.547 dB below this independent-sample simulation. Thus separate quantiles are not generally the quantile of the sum. The conditional calculations use an equivalent-power model and do not model instantaneous coherent voltages; the chapter says so and retains cross-spectral terms as the physical alternative. These are illustrative distributions, not measured environmental component values.

The result materially addresses the frozen comparison question: it distinguishes a documented approximate component-sum quantile method from summing marginal percentiles and shows a conditional dependence alternative. The unresolved official sign/current-v18 text is handled as a source boundary, not a standard-bug claim. The corpus-specific low-output ordering is investigated with a one-component identity counterexample but should not be treated as an implemented standard behavior.

### Fockens campaign and 2024 ITU comparison conditions

I directly reopened the full 2019 Fockens et al. paper from the University of Twente repository. Its scope is 54 amateur premises plus five other sites across Dutch environments, 470 kHz–50.5 MHz, WGN field-strength measurements restricted to amateur bands, with PLC notches limiting their contribution (§II, lines 149–174; §III, lines 198–223). It uses 500 Hz measurements converted to 2,700 Hz under an explicitly Gaussian assumption; measurement periods are April–October during long daylight and times chosen to minimize atmospheric noise; the MF/HF antenna is vertically polarized, measurements are outdoors about 10 m from premises walls where applicable, and a self-calibrating R&S ESH2 is used (lines 224–248). The report compares against a short monopole above perfect ground, equation (2), with frequency/category curves and means/standard deviations; its stated n is city 8, residential 36, rural 10 and quiet-rural 3 (lines 252–294). It concludes that changes are frequency/category dependent (lines 303–323). These details materially qualify any graph-read residual.

The continuation's graph values and differences are labelled approximate. Its conversion equation and arithmetic reproduce for P.372-17's short-lossless-monopole reference at 2,700 Hz; they are not matched-antenna paired residuals or significance tests. They avoid turning Dutch residential values into one universal offset. Fockens itself compares to P.372-13, whereas this continuation explicitly recomputes the predecessor P.372-17 reference. This is a useful conditional check, but does not verify continuity to current P.372-18.

The opened SM.2449-1 report §10.1.1.1.3.3 and Tables 30–31 independently establish that its noise environment analysis compares P.372 with Netherlands levels, converts them to magnetic field with a 51.5 dB factor, and cites Fockens et al. 2019 as reference [1] plus another source [3]. This is adequate evidence that the 2024 table uses the same Fockens campaign at least in part; it is not enough to show reference [3] is independent. Its tables also characterize *spatial* variation; the report separately notes temporal variation may be larger (§10.2, lines 2564+). Do not count the reused table values twice as separate empirical replications.

Exact NTIA pulse record/date and current standard equation image are unresolved; no target conversion uncertainty exists without hardware calibration.

Available report-index source details were checked, but the large NTIA PDF prevented re-reading the exact events and raw measured data are not supplied. No receiver/pump experiment was in scope.
6. **Decision relevance and next discriminator — supported boundary.** Findings change how P.372 medians, same-number deciles, AWGN, occupancy and front-end stresses should be used. Concrete next checks are identified, and the research does not pretend to qualify an unknown site or receiver.
