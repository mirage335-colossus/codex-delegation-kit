## Deduplicated consequential criteria

1. **Physical link budget, reference plane, and model provenance.** Evaluate candidate skywave field/loss calculations with explicit path, antennas, frequency, power, noise/reference plane, mode summation, and model build/version. Preserve the P.533 term definitions and important implementation/text discrepancy rather than quoting a generic loss number. Corpus evidence: Fable-30 §§“Loss-budget anatomy”, “Representative loss magnitudes”, “Reproduction and calculation provenance”; official P.533-14 text compared with executed ITU-R-HF v14.3/P533 14.2 implementation. Equivalent coverage must identify the implementation and quantity calculated and distinguish model prediction from measurement. A model median is not a physical bound.

2. **Frequency- and geometry-dependent absorption and other path terms.** Cover D/E-region and F-region absorption, solar zenith/season/sunspot/gyrofrequency/elevation effects, winter anomaly, polar precipitation/PCA, ionospheric/ground reflection, focusing, polarization, Es, and scatter/duct/chordal alternatives where applicable. Fable-30 §§“Non-deviative D/E-region absorption”, “Other loss and gain terms”, “Marginal and exotic modes”; it gives equations, stated domains and examples. Equivalent evidence keeps frequency/path geometry, one-way/doubled traversal, measured versus modeled loss, and unsupported event-law parameters explicit. The chapter itself leaves some spectra/oblique event laws and rare-mode prevalence undetermined.

3. **Propagation support and service availability are not channel spread.** Distinguish basic/operational MUF, OWF/HPF, system-dependent LUF, mode appearance/loss, operational service probability, and instantaneous waveform response. Fable-30 §§“MUF, LUF and what controls availability”, “Accuracy of prediction programs”; Fable-32 §J (standards vs observations); Astra-6 “What the standard channel does and does not say”. Accept evidence that preserves prediction time scale and system criterion; do not treat monthly median P.533 or an ionogram as an instantaneous mode set or modem success probability. It is unresolved how to derive a global link-level success distribution from these heterogeneous data.

4. **Signal-level variability and prediction uncertainty across time scales.** Cover monthly median model error, between-day/within-hour deciles, foF2/MUF variation, frequency and path dependence, and whether uncertainty terms are correlated or additive. Fable-30 §§“Variability statistics”, “Accuracy of prediction programs”, “What a high-fidelity simulation would need”; the summary explicitly separates deciles. Equivalent coverage requires reported sample, conditioning and interval definition; do not double-count dependent deciles or invent a universal shared-error law. Fable-30 says model-error residuals and temporal correlation do not identify a universal probability law.

5. **Outage/event families with direct communication evidence kept distinct from proxies.** Include flare SWF, proton/PCA, auroral/post-storm absorption, geomagnetic-storm MUF/mode changes, and non-absorption propagation failures. Fable-30 §§“Outage mechanisms and magnitudes”, “Occurrence over a solar cycle”, with recent 2024–26 ionosonde, oblique-sounding, passive-receiver and link studies; Fable-32 §“Storm-time route availability…” and Ottawa–Alert/QAN sections. Equivalent coverage identifies the observable (received link pulses, ionosonde echoes, MRF, riometer, radar), denominator/missingness, route/epoch, and causal limits. The corpus documents conditional events but leaves operation masks, calibrated local link loss, and general joint outage probabilities unresolved; echo loss or remote riometer absorption is not interchangeable with modem blackout.

6. **MF / low-HF and transition boundary.** Assess night skywave, sunset/sunrise transition, MF frequency/solar/geomagnetic dependence, night-fading zone, and the explicit 1.7–2 MHz model gap between P.1147 and P.533. Fable-30 §“MF night skywave” plus measured values §§“MF and low-HF night skywave” and §“Marginal and exotic modes”; Fable-31 §I. Equivalent coverage retains P.1147’s annual-median/six-hours-after-sunset scope, transition accuracy caveat, and separates 160-m practice reports from calibrated distributions. The 1.8–2 MHz loss/delay/fade laws and long-path prevalence remain weakly evidenced, not filled by algebraic extrapolation.

7. **Discrete modal physics and geometry-derived delay.** Cover E/F1/F2, hop count, low/high rays, O/X, multipath and ground wave; distinguish total time of flight from relative mode delay and RMS/composite delay spread. Fable-31 “Physical mechanisms” and §B (including Watterson, Balser–Smith, WWV, Texas path examples and spherical mirror calculation); Fable-32 §§“High-latitude mechanisms”, “Equatorial spread F on HF links”; Fable-83 §3. Equivalent evidence binds mode geometry, carrier, path and measurement resolution; geometry calculations may check plausibility but are not measurements. Corpus has substantial examples but not complete frequency × path × season joint mode statistics.

8. **Doppler centroid/shift versus stochastic spread and apparent frequency error.** Require separate per-mode/common shift, spread estimator, sign, phase-path or reflector motion, TID/terminator/flaring behavior, and instrument oscillator error. Fable-31 §§D–E and H; Fable-32 §§“High-latitude mechanisms”, “Equatorial and low-latitude mechanisms”, DAMSON/NRL and §H; Fable-83 §1. Counterexamples include a 25-Hz NRL event and low-Kp high spreads, as well as Male et al.’s clock-dominated offset. Equivalent coverage must not count a changing centroid as a wider stationary spectrum or confuse oscillator drift with ionospheric Doppler.

9. **Estimator, window, censoring, and cross-study conversion discipline.** Preserve per-tap 2σ, RMS, 68/80/95%-power width, threshold width, FWHM, composite span, first-to-last mode spread, delay RMS/window/threshold, correlation bandwidth/time, and exact analysis limits. Fable-31 §A; Fable-32 §1 and DAMSON/NRL sections; Fable-83 §§“Source envelopes…” and “S5-D”. Native examples include the Angling 80%-width / 2σ conversion only for its Gaussian construction, Lorentzian infinite variance, DAMSON ±40-Hz/12.5-ms limits, and censored LA-UR windows. Equivalent coverage makes conversions conditional and carries noise threshold, SNR, mode inclusion, duration, and instrumentation. A named “spread” with no method is insufficient.

10. **Mid-latitude quiet/moderate/disturbed and long-haul cohorts.** Include primary Watterson sample values and its short selected stationarity windows; path-specific UK trough-path percentiles across season and solar cycle; long-haul observed profiles; and contrasts between quiet, moderate, and storm cases. Fable-31 §§B, E, H, “Recent measured-profile evidence and time-scale limits”, “Contradictions…”; Fable-83 S1. Do not promote the often-cited 30-Hz/7-ms preset as a midlatitude observation: Fable-31 summary says no read source supports it for midlatitude, NVIS, or long-haul paths. Corpus has actual route-conditioned observations but sparse campaigns, selected/detected samples and unmatched estimators; no global midlatitude law is supported.

11. **NVIS and near-vertical/equatorial quiet cases are a separate geometry.** Include E/F/O-X mode combinations, shallow/near-vertical paths and ground-wave interaction; measured quiet Java and 97-km Male et al. O/X behavior, while preserving available-frame conditioning and clock-quality limits. Fable-31 §§B, G, I, J; Fable-32 Java NVIS §355 onward and §G; Astra-6 “Path physics beyond a tap table”; Fable-83 S2. Equivalent evidence distinguishes a one-week quiet campaign, detected frames, missing frames, conditional delay estimates and an unsupported disturbed-NVIS distribution. Midlatitude long-haul or VHF data are not substitutes.

12. **High-latitude auroral oval, trough, and polar-cap regimes.** Include DAMSON path/season/frequency/SNR-dependent spreads, NRL measured 2σ single-mode and scatter examples, trough-wall/off-great-circle and polar patches/arcs, Leicester trough and polar-cap path contrasts, and geomagnetic context. Fable-32 §§A–F and §J; Fable-83 S4. Keep 194-km DAMSON extrema and NRL window-limited events distinct from more typical longer routes; 73-Hz vs 55-Hz published/thesis discrepancy remains unresolved, not a chosen bound. Severe channels at low Kp contradict a simplistic Kp severity ranking. Radar spectral width is a proxy with known estimator bias, not a one-way HF link width without a model.

13. **Equatorial spread-F and trans-equatorial propagation.** Cover bottomside/plume evolution, local-time/equinox dependence, motion and onset, F-layer scatter, separate afternoon/evening TEP, non-great-circle returns, delay and Doppler evidence, and quiet/daytime counterexamples. Fable-32 §§3, G–H and §J; Fable-83 S6/S5-D; Astra-6 “Why stationarity sometimes works and sometimes fails”. Evidence includes Kwajalein threshold widths, Texas/Thailand multipath, Peru’s coupled transition and alias/window caveats, Hawaii–Samoa slide extrema, and the low-spread Antarctica–Spain campaign that truncates large events. Preserve their different frequency/path/estimator and access depth; none alone yields a transferable full joint law or incidence rate.

14. **Fading statistics, slow power structure, and fade-duration evidence.** Assess Rayleigh/Rician/log-normal/other alternatives; specular-to-diffuse fraction, mode beating, polarization fading, depth, correlation/fade duration and stationarity. Fable-31 §F and §H, MF §I; Fable-32 §I; Fable-83 §§2–3. The midlatitude selected records support heterogeneous per-mode and composite behavior, while a fixed Rician K is challenged by evidence that a “specular” term itself wanders. Fable-31 explicitly found no measured midlatitude HF fade-duration distribution; its Rayleigh/Gaussian fade-rate table is derived, not observed. MF duration measurements are below 1 MHz and cannot be silently extrapolated.

15. **Frequency selectivity, delay–Doppler structure, and coherence.** Assess occupied-bandwidth effects, inter-mode nulls, coherence time/bandwidth, intra-mode dispersion, O/X and frequency covariance. Fable-31 §§C, G and Watterson tables; Fable-32 §§I and Nissen–Bello; Fable-83 §§1–2. Include the source limits: no universal profile is implied by a two-ray cosine model; selected Nissen–Bello few-microsecond within-mode correlation does not establish inter-mode or cross-carrier covariance; QAN/Ottawa scalar records are not complex channel captures. These cross-carrier/coherent quantities remain truly unresolved in this corpus.

16. **Nonstationarity and coupled parameter trajectories.** Cover physical timing and correlation of mode birth/death, delays, centroid/spread, power, absorption, outage/recovery, terminator/TID/flaring/storm transitions; distinguish within-dwell, minute-scale, hour-scale and multi-day evidence. Fable-31 §H and recent profiles; Fable-32 §§F, G–H and event archives; Fable-30 outage chronology; Fable-83 §§4–6. Do not collapse a time series or selected-event trajectory into an iid stationary draw or independently combine marginal extremes. The corpus offers several event-specific trajectories, but natural transition rates and representative joint complex trajectories are not established.

17. **Standards/test envelope versus natural occurrence, measured profile, and decoder performance.** Fable-31/32 comparisons with F.1487/F.520, MIL-STD/DRM, and Fable-83 §3. A complete comparison states whether values are standards stress points, one measured population, source-summary-matched realizations, explicit design hypotheses, or actual modem outcomes. F.1487’s test grid and selected percentile notes are not worldwide climatology; apparent standards/modem margin is not evidence the physical channel occurred with that joint profile. Equivalent coverage preserves exact edition, scenario row, SNR/bandwidth, and provenance.

18. **Primary-source depth, contrary evidence, and honest unresolved inventory.** Source-depth parity cannot be judged by bibliography length. Fable-30’s source tags, resolved-discrepancy section, 2024–26 source bodies and access statements; Fable-31 §J and references; Fable-32 §“Contradictions, open questions, and things not found”, later QAN/Ottawa analyses and dispositions; Fable-83 §8; Astra-6 annotated references. Equivalent work identifies the first-party source and exact section/table/figure, says full text/scan/abstract/index/secondary, reproduces method and selection, and retains disagreement/access blocks. The reviewed corpus itself names continuing limits (e.g. operation/quality masks, sparse routes/seasons, unavailable raw complex responses, missing joint/fade laws, uninspected papers) rather than asserting exhaustiveness. A named unread source is an evidence gap only when its missing content could materially alter a criterion; it is not proof that no science exists. Conversely, if reviewed source sections already cover the claim, an evaluator's failure to find them is a search omission, not a corpus gap.

## Scope limits and current gap assessment

The headings and selected substantive passages are enough to establish broad topical coverage across HF/MF loss and outages, midlatitude/NVIS/long-haul dispersion, high-latitude extremes, equatorial/TEP behavior, estimators, event records, and scenario formulation. This is not a paragraph-by-paragraph verification of every numeric claim or full independent source audit. Exact references and native access grades in the corpus should be checked before quoting a result as independently reproduced.

The strongest genuinely unresolved areas visible in the reviewed documents are: calibrated end-to-end terminal loss/availability and active-operation denominators; source-matched event-conditioned outage distributions; joint time-varying complex amplitude/delay/Doppler/O-X and cross-carrier covariance; complete independent event/season/path holdouts; midlatitude HF fade-duration statistics; disturbed NVIS/equatorial-wideband populations; rare-mode incidence laws; and global geography/season/frequency tail coverage. These are not fixed by an uninspected single paper, because the source limitations repeatedly concern missing joint observables, local calibration, or coverage. Other apparent “missing” topics that are already represented substantively in these five documents should be treated as evaluator search misses unless a criterion-specific contrary source shows a real limitation or correction.
