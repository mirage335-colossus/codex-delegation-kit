### VLF

The `S0` end-to-end complex data are identical in the two cases (order of the two linear transfer factors is exchanged). At +112.5 Hz both show −16.258 dB. Use the midpoint injection to divide the cascade and isolate the effective upstream factor, or use a model and independent recalibration with the same implication. In V01 the downstream factor is −15.079 dB there under S0. Selecting S1 leaves the upstream factor untouched and the full response becomes −2.359 dB at +112.5 Hz (−2.418 dB at −112.5 Hz). In V02 that downstream factor is already −1.179 dB and unchanged. The upstream factor is −15.079 dB and the same change leaves −16.258 dB. The sampled response reaches the provisional criterion in V01/S1 only. Interpolated or full-band performance is an additional question.

This can overturn a shared downstream-wide-match priority or an inference that matching end-to-end spectra validate a common stage or fading model. V01 supports deferring a transmit-path or receiver change while testing the available downstream route. V02 supports deferring the same downstream work, measuring the upstream launch/path or narrowing the waveform if it actually helps under the physical constraint. The midpoint alone does not identify whether the upstream factor is tunable antenna impedance, a transducer or propagation. Deconvolution of a deep response does not restore missing SNR. Even the V01 result says nothing about absolute gain, SNR, calibration, timed settling or complete-waveform admission. A blanket adoption, or residual classification entirely as multipath, skips the decision posed by the new evidence.

### EME

The signed observed post-mix residual centers are +62.5, −144, and +283 Hz. All lie within the declared public center prior. Only L01 lies in the proposed ±100 Hz detector interval; its listed occupied probes also survive the candidate rate/filter nearly unchanged (maximum <0.03 dB across probes). L02 keeps its center in the lowpass but places its −112.5 Hz relative edge at −256.5 Hz, attenuated about 19.62 dB. L03 places its center at +283 Hz (attenuated about 52.82 dB), and three listed probes at +320.5, +358 and +395.5 Hz exceed the output Nyquist boundary. The residual filter leakage maps to −279.5, −242 and −204.5 Hz. These are probe attenuations and folded frequencies. They do not quantify what energy a defined modem signal, Helper or receiver would retain.

Keep the useful geometry correction while challenging the proposed order or residual-support choice. A full-prior detector fallback applied only to the already filtered, decimated stream cannot recover removed bands or uniquely invert folded components. For a single hard-centered branch intended to preserve every nominal DATA offset across the complete stated public interval, the spectral extent alone is ±412.5 Hz, implying a complex output rate strictly above 825 samples/s with additional room for filter transition and actual waveform support. This bound is conditional on the declared full prior and nominal width. Feasible alternatives include widened capture and filter before fallback, high-rate dual-path buffering, or multiple pre-decimation regions with justified coverage and accounted work/resources. Independent calibrated correction may justify a narrower supported branch, with a comparison that quantifies when it fails. The field distribution of residual errors and need for a wide route are unresolved.

Apply the research handoff and inspect the current public-frequency helper limit, capture/decimation configuration, slot admission, and complete-window state before recommending a native seam. Preserve the original public hypotheses in the actual candidate comparison. Show how same input, calendar, original prior, work leases, aperture, clocks and receiver endpoint will distinguish pre-stage support loss from a bad detector hypothesis or model. The wide witness is evaluator evidence, not a truth input to receiver scoring.
