# HF primary-source verification record

Read-only verification for a future Gaussian-vs-nonstationary HF exercise. This is not a claim that the reports are correct in full. The pumpModem reports remain untouched. Access status below refers to what I inspected during this verification, independently of their own status labels.

## Verified baseline: Watterson / F.1487

**Primary sources:** [ITU-R F.1487-0 official recommendation](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I!!PDF-E.pdf) and [NTIA Report 87-221 official full text](https://its.ntia.gov/umbraco/surface/download/publication?reportNumber=87-221_ocr.pdf). Both were opened and inspected as full PDF text through the web reader. The first-party F.1487 record confirms approved May 2000 and in-force status: [ITU record](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I/en).

F.1487 defines a stationary Gaussian-scatter tapped-delay model with independent complex Gaussian tap gains/Rayleigh fading, optional two magneto-ionic components, and Gaussian Doppler spectra. Its stated validation domain is 3 kHz; it says the model may apply up to 12 kHz. The common 2-sigma width convention is explicit. It notes that separate taps represent paths and that O/X components can instead be separate taps when delay separation is large. (Official PDF lines 134–147, 172–191, 208–215.) F.1487 states that the model represents the majority of typical links, but elsewhere stresses simulator benefits are limited if the underlying channel model is invalid (lines 134, 208).

NTIA 87-221 independently recaps Watterson's 1967 Illinois–Colorado 1294-km near-east-west midlatitude data at 5.864 MHz night / 9.259 MHz day. It says ~17 hours were recorded, divided into 10–13 minute intervals, with ~2 hours (12%) considered approximately stationary by fading rate, modal delay, and average modal power; only three samples received detailed analysis. Its summary says the narrowband model is a reasonable facsimile, with sample-specific validity bandwidths and acknowledged time-varying channel complexity (official PDF lines 864–875). This is the basis for the limited evidence warning; the 12% interpretation is NTIA 87-221's, not the original paper's wording.

The original Watterson/Juroshek/Bensema report is cited by the local research as [ESSA ERL 112-ITS 80](https://archive.org/details/DTIC_AD0699486), but that Archive.org item could not be opened by this web reader. Therefore I did not directly inspect the underlying 1969 source in this task; the bounds above rest on the official ITU standard and official NTIA 1987 review of it.

## Contrary stationary spectrum evidence: Lorentzian is conditional

**Primary source:** [Vogler & Hoffmeyer, NTIA Report 92-284, official full text](https://its.ntia.gov/umbraco/surface/download/publication?reportNumber=92-284.pdf), opened and inspected. The abstract says comparisons of model and measured data indicate an exponential autocorrelation random process, hence Lorentzian scattering spectrum; it shows comparison against measured scattering functions on a near-vertical auroral path, with broad spread-F conditions (PDF lines 145–162). In the comparison section it identifies the observations as May 1988 measurements over an approximately 80 km east-west near-vertical Fairbanks path, from quiet/stable to intense spread-F (lines 564–573).

This is real contrary evidence to treating Gaussian shape as universal, but not a universal replacement: its empirical fit is on a short, near-vertical auroral path and the listed conditions. The 1992 report's abstraction does not supply general midlatitude validation or a matched-estimator cross-population distribution. When comparing with F.1487, match correlation time or a named spectral-width convention; do not call Lorentzian width “2 sigma” because a Lorentzian has no finite variance. The local resilience report makes the same limitation explicit (Fable report lines 248–256, 486–491).

## Nonstationarity / recent empirical evidence

- **2025 full-text, direct evidence for dynamic path behavior but not a channel law:** [Ji & Wu, Sensors 25(14), 4246 (2025)](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/14/4246), full open article inspected. Its simulation section is internally inconsistent: it first gives a two-path delay difference of 10 ms, then sets second-path delay randomly from 0.1 to 2 ms; 140 simulated 8PSK records use 12.12 MHz, 2.4 kHz, 0 dB SNR, and max Doppler 0–20 Hz (PDF lines 299–304). Do not take either delay as an authoritative channel profile without resolving this conflict. Its real Xi’an–Urumqi measurement is 12.36 MHz, 9.6 kHz bandwidth, 86 cycles, 120 MHz sampling; reported delay-estimate variation is compared between MATE and GCC, but the authors say the “real delay” is agnostic to the actual test environment (lines 371–378). This is algorithm-performance evidence on a slowly varying measured link, not calibrated channel truth, a Watterson falsification, or a complex-channel replay dataset. It does contradict an overly broad statement that no recent HF measurement/algorithm study exists.
- **2026 records: publication is distinguishable from inspected methods.** The local Fable report identifies three contemporaneous model/measurement papers: [high-latitude antenna-directivity model, DOI 10.1109/TAP.2026.3651377](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAP.2026.3651377), [three-link distance/channel evolution, DOI 10.1109/LAWP.2026.3696384](https://doi.org/10.1109/LAWP.2026.3696384), and [midlatitude channel measurement/statistical modeling, DOI 10.1109/TAP.2026.3705045](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAP.2026.3705045). Official IEEE pages for the latter two returned only a JavaScript robot-verification gate; the first IEEE document ID was inaccessible. I did not inspect their full papers or treat abstract summaries indexed by secondary services as primary evidence. The local report likewise says its available text was abstract-only and lists unresolved RF/bandwidth, calibration, censoring, sample, and held-out validation details (highFidelity Fable 33, lines 257–263). These are valuable discovery leads; their title/date alone cannot qualify their results.

## Reconciliation and decision implications

The supported exercise is not “Gaussian is wrong” or “recent work proves a nonstationary law.” It is: compare a named stationary Gaussian baseline with a named Lorentzian alternative on matched statistics; separately test a slow common-frequency trajectory or measured time-varying episode. Keep common shift, drift rate, per-path Doppler spread, inter-ray offset, and delay spread as distinct quantities. Use the 2025 paper only as a scoped real-link/algorithm lead and leave the 2026 papers as unresolved primary-source leads until body/methods are accessible. The reports' deterministic corner cases are sensible discriminators, but their outputs remain unexecuted proposals in this review.
