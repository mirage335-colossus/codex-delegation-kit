# Channel appraisal for the three-carrier modem

## Decision for development

Use shared motion and separate DATA gain as the inexpensive receiver hypothesis. Use shared propagation support with carrier-dependent complex response for the next simulator comparison. These operate at different levels. The simulated channel can share a physical path while the receiver treats the DATA gain as unknown. Compare both hypotheses against weak coupling and model mismatch before allowing Helper to remove DATA search coverage. Extend the native renderer and appraisal by reusing its fractional-delay and evolving-path code. The existing composite two-path renderer already represents a mathematical shared response. A duplicate renderer or a new label for that case would add no discriminating power.

- **HF skywave.** Start with an uncertain common group-arrival trajectory and an independently parameterized common frequency residual. Retain path-specific delay, Doppler and complex evolution for the sparse alternative and support carrier-specific strength and mode dropout. Place useful limits on propagation by testing ground/sky or resolvable sky modes separately. Group-delay rate and carrier Doppler can be related on the same mode. The relationship depends on the ionospheric mechanism and can change at mode transitions. The receiver should transfer low-order timing or frequency information only when supported by the observations and the carrier layout. [Nickisch et al., 2006, sections 2–5](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2005RS003358)
- **Passive 5.8 GHz EME.** If both station locations, observation epoch, RF frequencies and the transmitting schedule are known, compute a common two-leg geometric delay and its corresponding RF-dependent Doppler outside the sample loop. Carry a separate clock/frequency residual into acquisition. Model the echo remaining after center compensation as a delay–Doppler distribution with geometry- and polarization-dependent power. A few response clusters are a useful first stress model. Include an extended diffuse counterexample and test the shortest coherence the receiver is intended to handle. Actual passive weak-signal link performance remains to be measured. [Tasmania bistatic radar experiments, 2025, section 3.1](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/17/3/352)
- **Receiver.** Stage 1 should remain frequency proposal plus bounded DATA-only verification. Measure with the same IQ both the benefit of accepted Helper proposals and their ability to suppress correct DATA candidates. If the current accepted-Helper policy drops an otherwise recoverable DATA slot, a small second search budget or prioritized traversal of the original prior is a candidate remedy. Merely retriggering the full search after an empty proposal path misses cases where a wrong DATA hypothesis was accepted on the restricted path. Prefer a bounded uncertainty-aware proposal propagated over DATA block times before adding a joint channel estimator. Existing coherent, varying and drift receivers provide the comparison.
- **Defer richer methods.** Fit a higher-order time–frequency basis, a larger clustered delay–Doppler model, or a delay-domain tracker only if a held-out case demonstrates a material DATA failure mode that a low-order trajectory and a sparse nuisance response miss. Generate expensive environmental predictions or recorded scenarios offline when useful, then use native propagation and receive code to measure decode and capacity. A delay–Doppler representation alone does not justify a waveform change, a full environmental solver in the hot path, or cross-carrier coherent equalization.

This research and static source assessment defines comparisons for the native owner. Runtime outcome and RF qualification remain open.

## What the alternatives mean

For carrier index `c` with RF center `f_c`, let `x_c` and `y_c` be transmit and receive complex envelopes. A local shared-support model is

```text
y_c(t) = exp(j φ_station,c(t)) Σ_p b_c,p(t) x_c(t − τ_p,c(t)) + n_c(t).
```

Common trajectory plus separate gains sets one group delay `τ_p,c = τ_0` and includes one shared frequency trajectory with uncertainty, with a carrier-specific complex gain accounting for local amplitude and phase. This is a flat-fading envelope approximation for each channel. With more than one resolvable delay, it loses intra-DATA dispersion. If `b_c,p(t)` is allowed arbitrary phase evolution, the decomposition into a common phase and carrier-specific gain is nonidentifiable. Make a common-phase estimate conditional on a constrained residual bandwidth or on an independent physical prior, and evaluate an aggregate observable such as predictive frequency coverage rather than an arbitrary phase factorization.

Sparse shared support permits several locally common delays, optional small carrier offsets, and different complex weights across carriers. If transmission traverses the same nondispersive propagation, a physical delay already induces `exp(−j 2π f_c τ_p)` for each RF center. In that model `b_c,p` can be written as this propagation phase times a local scattering coefficient. A moved delay already produces geometric Doppler and should not be charged again as a separate geometric frequency offset. Relative speed uses the RF centers. Evaluating a chosen baseband/IF delay path adds frequency dependence across the represented spectrum. The RF-to-IF phase reference matters when a simulation is intended to reproduce a particular RF geometry. HF may instead require different phase and group paths, per-mode Doppler and weakly related or missing modes across carriers.

An exact shared response uses a single time-varying filter on the composite IQ. It naturally generates different complex gains at different frequencies. For two locally stationary paths with `Δτ = τ1−τ0`, the relative phase winds by `2π Δf Δτ` between carriers separated by `Δf`. Two equal-amplitude paths on the same shared support can produce a null at one carrier and reinforcement at another. Use widely spaced carriers and unequal relative powers as ordinary stress cases. Calling these aggregate gains independent is a useful conservative receiver approximation in some layouts. It is an additional statistical assumption in the renderer. The general wideband response or local delay–Doppler distribution covers common-motion, sparse, and diffuse cases with the appropriate parameter choices. [Bello, 1963, original abstract](https://forohistorico.coit.es/index.php/biblioteca/articulos-seminales/item/characterization-of-randomly-time-variant-linear-channels)

Two dimensionless quantities expose different failures. `B_data × τ_eff`, where `B_data` is occupied DATA bandwidth in Hz, governs intra-DATA selectivity. `|Δf| × τ_eff` across DATA and Helper governs gain-transfer plausibility in a shared-support channel. `τ_eff` must name its measure, for example RMS spread or a stated energy-percentile width in seconds. A tail width is a separate guard measure. In a locally stationary uncorrelated-path model, normalized complex frequency covariance is the Fourier transform of the normalized local power-delay profile. With a stable aperture and fixed power weights this gives a quantitative comparison between a modeled covariance and observations. Coherence bandwidth needs a threshold and a delay-profile shape. At two equal-power delays 1 ms apart, covariance magnitude first reaches zero at 500 Hz, then revives. A universal `1/τ` threshold would miss both the null and revival, and a time-evolving correlated channel needs measurements or a local estimate.

Pilot identifiability matters before the receiver design. A continuous tone principally exposes a complex scalar `H(t,f_c)` and temporal spectral structure. Two narrow pilots give a limited view of multiple paths, with delay phase wrapping and ambiguity under unknown independent gains. Any isolated `τ` recovered from the phase difference is periodic in `1/|Δf|` even when a single common mode and calibrated gains make that measurement meaningful. Dedicated known modulation with sufficient bandwidth and a calibrated timing reference, identifiable mode structure, or a sufficiently informative DATA observation is required to claim detailed delay recovery. The onset from a known schedule gives an aperture/timing proposal even when phase transfer is unusable. Count waveform, power and occupancy costs if considering wider sounding.

For one locally identifiable and differentiable path with phase `φ(t,f)`, the envelope group delay is `−∂φ/∂f / (2π)`, and the carrier frequency shift is `∂φ/∂t / (2π)` under the corresponding Fourier sign convention. Their compatibility is `∂τ_group/∂t = −∂ν/∂f`. This constrains a physical joint delay–phase model but does not force `ν = −f τ̇_group` in a dispersive channel. A small simulator can approximate group delay and frequency shift independently around the actual discrete carriers. For a calibrated contiguous RF span or resolved path, fit their frequency dependence with a consistent local phase surface. Always use a local reference epoch for waveform-phase evaluation, and test signs and reference conventions against timed pulses and a known chirp before interpreting a receiver drop.

### Lunar echo near 5.8 GHz

The geometric variable is `L_p(t)=R_tx,p(t)+R_p,rx(t)`, where point `p` is on the lunar surface, with suitable transmit/reflection/receive times. The point delay is `L_p/c` and the term from range rate alone is `−f_c × d(L_p/c)/dt` in Hz. The same formula applied to the selected geometric center/phase reference separates bulk compensation from differential delay and Doppler. In monostatic near-spherical geometry, the center-facing surface begins about one radius nearer than the center. After aligning to leading echo, the limb is up to `2R_moon/c`, about 11.6 ms, farther in delay. This derived support is a guard, distinct from the time containing a stated fraction of power. A bistatic link uses both endpoints and its illuminated/visible overlap. The effective width and polarization weighting then vary with geometry. Phase from station clocks/LOs/timebase, optional path propagation effects and scattering evolution should be accounted for separately. At 5.8 GHz, 1 ppb of effective relative oscillator error is 5.8 Hz, so it can compete with fine Doppler estimation even after bulk compensation.

## Receiver alternatives and their costs

1. **Robust coarse proposal and bounded native refinement.** Keep a multimodal union of frequency regions rather than a single fitted peak. Give each its observation interval, validity status and forecast expiry or uncertainty. Propagate uncertainty to DATA block times before trying smaller candidate neighborhoods. First try a constant residual, then a local linear drift or short block state only when the time-bandwidth warrants it. On a rectangular coherent block of duration `T`, an uncompensated constant residual `δν` gives power ratio `sinc²(δν T)`. The familiar quarter-cycle residual target is about 0.91 dB ideal coherent loss. This derived number does not account for within-block acceleration, scattering, bin exclusion or pilot noise. Operational tuning needs measured block-wise residual and the native decoder's admission and gain evidence.
2. **Shared-support candidate tracking.** A small set of fractional DATA delays can be scored in existing blocks with independent carrier/path gains, and their evidence combined where support is identifiable. The strong version shares delay candidates and local delay trajectories. The weaker version shares a population of delays and a drift prior, allowing dropout and bounded offsets. Use shrinkage or evidence for adding a path, retain a single-path alternative, and let the decoder keep residual energy unexplained. Exhaustively crossing delay order, per-path Doppler, drift and timing increases hypotheses and scratch, especially after lost coherence. Prefer template reuse and nested evaluation of one- and two-path candidates over an exhaustive joint grid. Barbotin et al. demonstrate shared-support estimation for continuous-time delays with uniformly spaced frequency pilots or coded broadband observations. A later experiment uses 251 high-SNR frames, eight antennas, a 120 MHz channel at 2 GHz and synthetic added AWGN. Shared support lowered symbol error below about 0 dB in that setting. The authors observe high-SNR model bias, and use rank-based fallback for nonsparse portions. The particular estimator and improvements require richer measurements than a pair of Helper tones. [Barbotin et al., 2011 preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.1339) [Barbotin and Vetterli, 2012 preprint, section V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.4626)
3. **State-space gain/delay/Doppler or a local basis.** A small linearized filter or fixed-state search can track a common residual and maintain uncertain per-path complex gains between blocks. A small basis expansion can represent faster within-block variation, conditional on pilot/DATA information and a guard against spectral/model leakage. Kalman-style prediction shrinks work if uncertainty is reliable. Deep fades, incorrect initialization and path birth can make a Gaussian single-mode filter lock to the wrong state. Maintain a reset or alternate mode if field or seeded evidence warrants it. A generic joint delay–Doppler equalizer, particle filter or larger time-varying basis needs more state and wider/practically repeated known probes. A strong benchmark would have to show that existing drift and coherent-gain states fail for a real intra-block phenomenon and that the new state materially improves complete-receiver output at useful capacity. [Giannakis and Tepedelenlioğlu, 1998, university-hosted abstract](https://experts.azregents.edu/en/publications/basis-expansion-models-and-diversity-techniques-for-blind-identif)

Publish the ordinary no-assistance result separately from the accepted, rejected, and incorrect-assistance strata. Measure candidate counts, peak scratch/leases, stages run or skipped, CPU time, wall time and successful complete decodes per time or resource allocation. Include wrong DATA and false DATA on quiet/noise, message-level completion and repeatability over paired seeds. Report hard-case throughput together with the achieved error/coverage tradeoff, in addition to the clean fast path. Keep state, waveform kernels and eligible low-precision template/FFT work native. Accumulate likelihoods and form phase/time arguments robustly where needed, promoting only the operations that empirically lose meaningful decisions or coverage. Model-first versus input-first equivalence must include the actual projected observations, known helper carriers, receiver resource settings and admission prior. Equal final outcomes alone do not demonstrate a matched test.

## Remaining discriminators

- The intended RF carrier centers and spacings, occupied bandwidth per carrier, station clocks and transmitter/receiver phase relationship determine whether a shared delay and Doppler proposal is observable. Specify them before choosing numerical coherence, range-rate or capture bounds. Use the measured native search and projection costs for the selected layout.
- Require synchronized, calibrated three-band observations or equivalent complex baseband sounding to assign cross-carrier transfer. Record carrier-offset convention, Tx reference/time, channel quality and usable band during fades. For HF include repeat mode geometry, changing ionosphere and link placement relative to MUF. For EME require known Tx schedule, both endpoint coordinates, dates, frequencies, polarization, receiver sample clock, and valid echo intervals. Without a suitable known transmission, narrowband passive IQ can bound apparent line drift or width under assumptions, with delay profile and separation of transmitted modulation from channel spread unresolved.
- Per case, measure whether a short Helper aperture ensures DATA prediction coverage through the full horizon. Compare external EME trajectory errors, oscillator residual and in-echo libration separately. In HF, resolve whether a miss comes from plasma dispersion, a newly dominant mode, onset ambiguity or pilot SNR. A common trajectory model can improve candidates while leaving gain forecasts poor. Measure both along with independently resolved mode coincidences if the observations permit them.
- Higher-order basis, stronger support sharing and further propagation realism remain conditional on a reproducible receiver gap. The near-band EME measurements and the HF sounder sources support useful stress models, with simultaneous target-layout complex-channel histories and qualified complete-receiver throughput still unavailable.
