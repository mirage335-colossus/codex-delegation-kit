# EME continuation: measurements that can constrain receiver decisions

The first report recommends shared scattering across the carriers, with a geometry-conditioned dispersive model as the initial reference. The continuation found a published nearby-frequency lunar echo that can check bulk compensation, plus band-matched operational records and noise measurements. The additional sources provide distinct checks for geometry, front-end noise and plausible weak-signal operation. A simultaneous channel measurement at the modem’s actual spacings remains the decisive missing evidence for Helper-to-DATA fade transfer.

## Evidence that changes the appraisal

**Replay a published two-leg geometry case before interpreting lunar spectral width.** In the 2025 Tasmania paper, DSS-43 transmitted a constant 7159.45 MHz tone at 20 kW on 13 June 2024, with lunar returns detected at Ceduna, Katherine and Hobart. The 45-minute Hobart waterfall showed about 2 kHz of uncompensated bulk drift. A polynomial obtained from predictive radial velocity flattened the drift and centered the return near the expected zero. The transmitter switched circular sense partway through and the plotted Hobart channel was one linear polarization. The figures support checking the frequency track and the effect of receiver integration before and after correction. Their single-tone display cannot recover a delay power profile, joint carrier fading or polarization transfer. The quoted drift is session-specific, so neither the 2 kHz total nor its rescaling by frequency is a generic 5.8 GHz allowance. Recreate date, station pair and frequency as a geometry-only benchmark and derive candidate pass offsets and rates separately. This advances the initial report’s indexed observation to full-text verification. [Tasmania radar team, 2025, lunar observations and Figs. 4–5](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/17/3/352)

**Add 5.760 GHz station observations as independent sanity checks.** The OK1KIR team’s digital log includes Q65-60D contacts on 5.760 GHz, for example the K5DOG entry on 20 July 2025 with a 4.4 m dish and 10 W annotated. This establishes operation using those logged circumstances. A contact-only log has no all-attempt denominator or channel record, and the shown signal reports do not establish the new modem’s acquisition or decoding threshold. The band-matched CW log records 1.4 dB of Moon noise on 12 July 2012 with SDR-14. SM6FHZ independently reports more than 2.0 dB on 9 April 2016 near new Moon. These are station observations in indexed text, with beam, pointing, reference sky and receiver noise affecting their magnitude. If the reported dB values are on/off power rises, 1.4 dB would imply on-source \(T_{\rm sys}\) of about \(1.38T_{\rm off}\) and 2.0 dB about \(1.58T_{\rm off}\). Those factors are conditional interpretations, not transferable system temperatures. A useful first local check is the on/off-Moon noise rise at each carrier reference plane, paired with a long echo power measurement and separately recorded filter ENBW. Anecdotal off-band contacts or larger-dish data can then be kept out of the 5.8 GHz decision thresholds. [OK1KIR digital log, entry 125](https://www.ok1kir.cz/index.php?page=logs&section=6cm&subsect=jlogb) · [OK1KIR CW log, entry 252](https://www.ok1kir.cz/index.php?page=logs&section=6cm&subsect=logb) · [SM6FHZ station report, April 2016 text](https://2ingandlin.se/EME_6.html)

**Preserve where echo energy is discarded.** Evans’s distributed-target treatment shows why CW radar cross section cannot simply be assigned to a short time gate or a narrow post-tracking frequency gate. A short pulse illuminates different delay annuli at different times, and a narrow Doppler gate rejects part of the echo. Its example reduction curve in Fig. 1 is for 68 cm and the author explicitly limits transfer toward shorter wavelengths. Gekat, Gölz and Hannesen’s lunar radar calibration is a tempting C-band lead from the initial results of this continuation, but their own §2 specifies an S-band radar. Its short-pulse observations illustrate measurement design, with no 5.8 GHz delay-gate calibration. The appraisal should therefore normalize total modeled echo power at the antenna, apply actual filters and acquisition windows, and compare in-window energy or post-filter statistics independently. [Evans, 1965, §2.1 and Fig. 1, indexed excerpt and previously accessed pagetext](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/69D/jresv69Dn12p1637_A1b.pdf) · [Gekat, Gölz and Hannesen, 2011 AMS conference paper, indexed PDF §2 and Table 1](https://ams.confex.com/ams/35Radar/webprogram/Manuscript/Paper191872/WEATHER%20RADAR%20CALIBRATION%20AND%20TESTING%20USING%20THE%20MOON%20AS%20REFERENCE%20TARGET.pdf)

## Narrow the model choice with two calculated tests

1. **Check frequency transfer at two separate scales.** Use the first report’s shared scattering model. Under uncorrelated diffuse taps with normalized power-delay profile and slowly varying amplitudes over the separation, de-rotate transfer functions to the same mean delay and remove any coherent mean. The expected squared complex gain difference at separation \(\Delta f\), divided by one gain’s expected power, is \(2[1-\Re\rho(\Delta f)]\le (2\pi\Delta f\,\sigma_\tau)^2\), where \(\sigma_\tau\) is RMS excess delay about that mean. This follows by averaging \(2[1-\cos(2\pi\Delta f(\tau-\bar\tau))]\). Use actual filter support to screen within-carrier flatness and use the actual DATA–Helper separation for transfer. If the bound is large, it is inconclusive. Fit the measured frequency correlation itself, including phase slope and uncertainty, and compare conditional outage. A shared timing or oscillator estimate may help even if a Helper’s complex gain transfers poorly. Distinguish those uses with actual, absent and oracle common tracking and with matched calibrated gain information. [Hagfors, 1961, publisher-indexed abstract on power pulse response and frequency correlation](https://doi.org/10.1029/JZ066i003p00777)
2. **Check how tracking and coherent integration interact.** For a unit-area coherent time window of length \(T\) applied to a constant probe after bulk compensation, integrate the local Doppler spectrum against its squared response. For a rectangular window and an uncorrelated diffuse spectrum, the expected power relative to instantaneous diffuse power is \(\int S_\nu(\nu)\mathrm{sinc}^2[(\nu+\epsilon)T]\,d\nu / \int S_\nu(\nu)\,d\nu\), where \(\mathrm{sinc}(x)=\sin(\pi x)/(\pi x)\) and \(\epsilon\) is constant residual offset over the window. Treat a coherent mean separately. The relation follows by filtering stationary fluctuations and is a probe check under local stationarity. It quantifies why excellent bulk correction can coexist with severe coherent averaging loss. Estimate capture in the actual receiver over multiple integration lengths and two sweep axes, Doppler spread and common residual drift or offset. If a shared flat model matches the decision under the measured frequency response, retain it. If dispersive structure or power at the acquisition-window edges changes decisions, retain shared taps. Use the disk reference when changes in beam or geometry invalidate the tap profile.

No measurements here determine the full-band 5.8 GHz delay profile, provide shared complex IQ at the intended spacings, or estimate absolute availability for this modem. The corrective follow-up is a phase-calibrated three-carrier capture or swept sounding through representative narrow and broad libration episodes, with simultaneous carrier powers, failed acquisition attempts and on/off noise logging.
