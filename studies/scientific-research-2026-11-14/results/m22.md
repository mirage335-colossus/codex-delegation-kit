# Passive 5.8 GHz Earth–Moon–Earth channel appraisal

Use a topocentric two-leg link budget and a geometry-conditioned **shared delay–Doppler channel** across the three carriers. Begin with a small stochastic tapped model and compare it against a weighted lunar-disk model and, when obtainable, a measured channel. Keep the point reflector as a deterministic control for delay, bulk Doppler, link budget and receiver acquisition. Independent per-carrier fading is a sensitivity endpoint whose suitability depends on the actual frequency separations, processing intervals and joint fade observations. The two scales that inform this choice are occupied bandwidth *within* each waveform and separation *between* waveform centers. Three individually flat channels can have partially correlated gains. [Hagfors, 1961, publisher-indexed abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/JZ066i003p00777) · [Taylor, 2010, §§1–4](https://wsjt.sourceforge.io/EME2010_K1JT.pdf)

## Separate the geometry, mean power and fluctuations

- **Visibility and timing.** Use each station’s latitude, longitude, altitude, mount limits and elevation mask, with the Moon in view of both stations at their corresponding transmit and receive epochs. Propagate each leg at its appropriate transmit, scatter and receive time. The lunar center range alone gives a scale of 2.56 s round trip at 384,400 km. The first return is displaced from the center range by the illuminated surface geometry. An approximately spherical full disk in the monostatic limit gives up to \(2R_{\rm Moon}/c \simeq 11.6\) ms *excess* delay from the near surface to the limb. The power-weighted width can be much shorter and varies with scattering law, beam overlap and polarization. A full-disk support is useful for testing tail sensitivity. Use a documented terrestrial reference frame and Earth orientation, lunar ephemeris and, for disk work, a matching lunar body-fixed orientation. Check topocentric azimuth, elevation and range rate against independent ephemeris output. [Taylor, 2010, Figs. 1–3](https://wsjt.sourceforge.io/EME2010_K1JT.pdf) · [Taylor, 2016, §3.1](https://wsjt.sourceforge.io/LunarEchoes_QEX.pdf) · [JPL Horizons manual, indexed topocentric coordinates and reference-frame sections](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/manual.html) · [JPL NAIF, indexed CLPS SPICE data archive, PCK discussion](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/pds/pds4/clps/clps_spice/document/spiceds_v002.html)

- **Bulk Doppler and libration.** For a scatterer with total retarded path length \(L=R_{\rm t}+R_{\rm r}\), the first-order shift for carrier \(f_j\) is \(-f_j\,\dot L/c\) under the usual \(e^{+i2\pi ft}\) convention. The two station velocities both matter. Compute delay, shift and drift from the same geometry. Reuse the fractional bulk shift across carriers with the appropriate frequency scaling. Distinct patches contribute small differential shifts. Their interference produces complex time variation and a broadened echo spectrum even after correct bulk tracking. Taylor’s 2010 table for stations at ±40° lists 164 Hz maximum and 86 Hz median *full limb-to-limb* bounds at 5.760 GHz. The corresponding median-geometry estimates \(w_{10}=60\) Hz and \(w_{50}=25\) Hz are widths at fractions of peak power. The table gives an engineering scale derived from observations and geometry. Its §2 and Figs. 4–5 include significant changes between times and station pairs. Order-of-magnitude reciprocals of the power-carrying widths are tens of milliseconds, with actual phase and fade persistence governed by the shape of the local spectrum. Taylor’s separate QEX experiment measured spectral wings and verified bulk predictions at 144 and 432 MHz. [Taylor, 2010, §§2–4 and Figs. 4–8](https://wsjt.sourceforge.io/EME2010_K1JT.pdf) · [Taylor, 2016, §3.1 and Figs. 1–5](https://wsjt.sourceforge.io/LunarEchoes_QEX.pdf)

- **Absolute power and terrestrial losses.** Use a bistatic radar equation at a stated receiver reference plane. With boresight gains and a beam- and polarization-weighted effective cross section, \(\bar P_{{\rm r},j}=P_{{\rm t},j}G_{{\rm t},j}G_{{\rm r},j}\lambda_j^2\sigma_{{\rm eff},j}/[(4\pi)^3 R_{\rm t}^2R_{\rm r}^2 \mathcal L_j]\), with linear \(\mathcal L_j\) for losses excluded from the gains and cross section. Define \(\sigma_{\rm eff}\) using the two beam patterns over the jointly illuminated and visible disk and a polarization-specific scattering law. Wide, accurately pointed beams may justify a disk-integrated approximation. Beamwidths comparable to or narrower than the disk, pointing errors and different Tx/Rx beam centers warrant angular integration. Use range, elevation-dependent atmospheric and pointing losses, feeder losses and obstruction or weather scenarios where they affect availability. Normalize stochastic tap or facet power to the same mean. The coherent instantaneous sum may be higher or lower. With isotropic terminal gains and a hypothetical \(\sigma_{\rm eff}=0.06\pi R_{\rm Moon}^2\), the mean attenuation at the nominal range and frequency is about 285 dB before extra losses. This is a dimensional check with an assumed effective cross section. The 2020 X-band measurement reports \(\sigma_{\rm OC}/A_{\rm projected}\simeq0.06\) for a beam-limited Mare Imbrium region. [2020 X-band bistatic experiment, §§3–5, Eqs. 1–4 and conclusion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7180880/)

- **Polarization and noise.** Project from each antenna’s local polarization frame through the lunar scattering and into the receive frame. Smooth specular return preserves linear polarization in consistently transported bases and reverses circular sense in the usual radar convention. Feed orientation and station geometry can still cause linear mismatch. Add a diffuse, partly depolarizing return with its own delay and Doppler weighting. Use a Jones channel per path, or polarization-resolved power and covariance when the receiver model permits. Simulate co- and cross-polar combinations with explicit handedness (opposite sense, OC, and same sense, SC, relative to Tx as used in radar reporting), finite feed isolation, and ionospheric Faraday rotation on both legs if significant for the chosen linear polarization and path. The observed inverse-frequency-squared scaling makes Faraday typically less important at 5.8 GHz than at VHF. The measured X-band SC/OC ratio of 0.44–0.45 applies to the specific X-band region and beams. Use it to justify allowing cross-polar power and sensitivity bounds. Legacy 68-cm pulse data demonstrate how polarization can vary across the echo delay, with parameters specific to that band. [Taylor, 2016, introduction and §3.2](https://wsjt.sourceforge.io/LunarEchoes_QEX.pdf) · [Evans, 1965, §3.3, Figs. 12–13](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/69D/jresv69Dn12p1637_A1b.pdf) · [2020 X-band experiment, §§4–5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7180880/)

  Calculate \(N_0=kT_{\rm sys}\) at the same reference plane and noise power \(kT_{\rm sys}B_{\rm ENBW}\) after receiver filtering. Beam-weight the lunar brightness replacing the sky behind the disk. Include sky, atmosphere, ground spillover, losses ahead of the LNA and receiver equivalent temperature. Solar proximity or local interference can change usable windows. Represent measured colored or intermittent interference separately from thermal noise. Ordinary thermal noise can start as complex Gaussian with the variance implied by the modeled band and filter, independent across non-overlapping channels conditional on \(T_{\rm sys}\). Tests with overlapping filters or a common front end may need the measured output covariance. Estimate \(T_{\rm sys}\) from calibrated on-Moon and off-Moon observations, accounting for phase and beam filling. The JPL measurements at 2.3, 8.4 and 32 GHz illustrate beam placement and phase sensitivity. Interpolating their antenna system-temperature increments directly to another dish at 5.8 GHz would fold the study’s beam coupling into the wrong system. [Slobin, JPL DSN Progress Report 42-166, §I and measurements](https://tmo.jpl.nasa.gov/progress_report/42-166/166C.pdf)

## Derive the cross-carrier behavior

After factoring a reference delay \(\tau_0(t)\), use common residual delays \(\delta\tau_p(t)\) and reference-frequency coefficients \(b_p(t)\). A narrowband snapshot is

\[
H_j(t)=e^{-i2\pi f_j\tau_0(t)}
       \sum_p b_p(t)e^{-i2\pi(f_j-f_{\rm ref})\delta\tau_p(t)} .
\]

Here \(b_p\) includes differential propagation phase at \(f_{\rm ref}\). Generalize its amplitude and polarization smoothly across frequency when warranted. For an occupied band with resolvable structure, apply each path to \(x_j(t-\tau_0-\delta\tau_p)\), allowing fractional delays. Propagation phase derivatives already produce Doppler. A locally equivalent model can use integrated Doppler phase with frozen residual delays over a justified interval. Preserve phase continuity across geometry updates. Add separate station oscillator offset, drift, phase noise or sample-clock errors according to the radio architecture. A shared reference can permit Helper to track some common error across frequencies even when its instantaneous complex fade has little predictive value for DATA. A Helper at another site or with different pointing/polarization needs its own geometry and scattering projection.

For a locally stationary, uncorrelated diffuse scattering component with joint power density \(S(\tau,\nu)\) relative to the tracked reference, the covariance in the convention \(E[H(t+\Delta t,f+\Delta f)H(t,f)^*]\) is

\[
C(\Delta f,\Delta t)=\int\!\!\int S(\tau,\nu)
   e^{-i2\pi\Delta f\tau}e^{i2\pi\nu\Delta t}\,d\tau\,d\nu .
\]

This follows by forming the product of shared complex path sums and eliminating cross terms under the uncorrelated-scatterer hypothesis. Normalization by \(C(0,0)\) gives correlation for a locally homogeneous frequency range. The delay power profile determines frequency correlation and the Doppler power spectrum determines temporal correlation. Geometry links delay and Doppler, so a product of independently fitted marginals needs validation in a receiver-relevant region of \((\Delta f,\Delta t)\). Retain any coherent mean separately. A two-delay example with equal diffuse powers and delay difference \(d\) gives \(|\rho(\Delta f)|=|\cos(\pi \Delta f d)|\). It gives zero correlation at \(1/(2d)\) and a revival at \(1/d\), illustrating why the 11.6 ms edge alone provides no fixed coherence bandwidth. Zero diffuse covariance implies independence for jointly proper Gaussian coefficients. More general channels can have equal second moments but different joint outages and burst lengths. [Hagfors, 1961, publisher-indexed abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/JZ066i003p00777) · [Taylor, 2010, Figs. 2–3](https://wsjt.sourceforge.io/EME2010_K1JT.pdf)

## Model choice and evidence that could change it

| Family | Appropriate use and decision |
| --- | --- |
| Point reflector with deterministic delay and bulk Doppler | Verify delay tracking, large offset and drift, band shape, link budget and noise-limited receiver behavior. An independent-gain variant bounds one possible diversity assumption. Redrawing gains per symbol, block or carrier without measured coherence can produce incorrect joint outages and omit delay tails or Doppler structure. |
| Shared correlated flat gains | Use if the response is sufficiently flat *within* all three bands over the receiver’s useful processing window. Generate gains jointly with the observed or bracketed lagged complex covariance and an envelope model. Preserve temporal behavior across packet boundaries. |
| Locally stationary tapped delays or joint delay–Doppler samples | Initial dispersive model. Represent a center-weighted quasi-specular lobe and a weaker, broader diffuse component, with geometrically conditioned Doppler and power. A specular lobe may itself include several delays and rates. A deterministic mean plus many weak diffuse contributions can motivate a Rician scenario, while zero-mean proper Gaussian coefficients give Rayleigh magnitudes. Neither distribution nor a particular K factor follows from the word “quasi-specular.” Constrain near-center and tail weights, covariance, coherent fraction and station-to-station variation with evidence or sweep the unknowns. |
| Beam- and polarization-weighted lunar disk or facet model | Use as a reference when narrow/off-center beams, angular structure, link differences or time-varying delay–Doppler coupling matter. On a sphere, generate shared scattering cells with two-leg visibility, path lengths, local incidence/scatter angles and continuous phase. Weight each voltage by the square root of its allocated mean power and sum coherently. Aggregating cell powers into joint delay–Doppler bins provides a route back to a compact stochastic model. Compare a center-peaked empirical scattering law plus a diffuse tail against wider/narrower profiles before adding terrain, detailed roughness, shadowing or subsurface terms. Topography describes the large-scale geometry and provides no measured wavelength-scale phase realization. Verify refinement in mean power, delay/Doppler occupancy, covariance and decision metrics. |
| Coherent measured channel or replay | Prefer calibrated, simultaneous multi-frequency observations at matching beam geometry, frequency, polarization and libration, with logged radio drift and noise. Replay is a useful independent benchmark if time or frequency rescaling is limited to the documented domain. Estimate channel statistics after accounting for filters and measurement noise. |

The 2020 bistatic X-band experiment observed distinct illuminated diameters on two receiver paths, with modeled full broadenings of 35 and 90 Hz and agreement between predicted and measured echo spectra. This supports varying beam-weighted spread with the path. In recent work, the University of Tasmania team documents a June 2024 Earth-based bistatic lunar echo waterfall in its 2025 radar paper and illustrates the use of drift compensation. Campbell’s 2025 Icarus paper analyzes lunar polarimetric behavior at 12.6 cm and 70 cm and relates Stokes decompositions to multiple scattering interpretations. The indexed material supports sensitivity to scattering and terrain, with wavelength and geometry limits on parameter transfer. [2020 experiment, §§3–5 and Fig. 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7180880/) · [2025 Tasmania radar campaign, publisher-indexed lunar observation caption and text](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/17/3/352) · [Campbell, 2025, publisher-indexed preview, DOI 10.1016/j.icarus.2025.116738](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0019103525002866)

Li, Qian and Hu’s published 2026 study independently proposes a beam-dependent distributed cross-section, quasi-specular and diffuse delay profiles, and orbital Doppler. Its indexed abstract reports modeled broadband BER difficulties with conventional equalizers and a large correction in a large-aperture example. These are simulation findings tied to their bandwidth, scenarios and receiver. Their abstract also reports spread bounds of −39.88 to +39.88 Hz at 1.296 GHz, while Taylor’s 2010 table gives 37 Hz as the maximum full width at 1.296 GHz for its ±40° geometry. The values as presented would differ by roughly a factor of two if compared as the same width. The full definitions and geometry in the 2026 paper could not be checked, so preserve the discrepancy and bracket it with matched-geometry measurement. The broadband BER result does not determine whether a weak-signal modem with narrower individual carriers experiences material intersymbol interference. [Li, Qian and Hu, 2026, publisher-indexed extended abstract, 48(6), 2541–2550](https://jeit.ac.cn/en/article/doi/10.11999/JEIT251028) · [Taylor, 2010, §4](https://wsjt.sourceforge.io/EME2010_K1JT.pdf)

## Receiver-level discriminators

1. Specify station pair and dates, ephemeris/clock assumptions, antenna patterns and feeds, powers, exact centers and occupied bandwidths, symbol/frame/estimation intervals, thresholds and tolerable decision differences. Use two-tone or sounder captures with separations bracketing DATA–Helper, synchronized logging, high and low libration, and on/off-Moon spectra. Obtain spectra for bulk offset/rate and Doppler wings, wide enough probing for a power-delay profile if feasible, and noise-corrected pairwise complex and joint envelope statistics. A narrowband CW spectral width alone does not determine inter-carrier covariance. Report percentile widths, RMS delay and diffuse tail energy at the receiver’s decision or guard interval, with detection floors and uncertainty.
2. In matched scenario sweeps use identical waveforms, station geometry, mean link budget and receiver settings across model families. Check deterministic single-path delay/phase/drift and two-path cancellation at known frequencies. Check mean echo power and post-filter \(kTB\), zero and finite libration, limiting correlation as centers converge, phase continuity and the response to pointing/polarization mismatch. Check convergence of a disk/tap representation in the measured profiles and receiver outcomes. Compare joint fades and fade durations as well as marginal SNR, particularly on the receiver’s actual aggregation timescale.
3. Report mutual Moon visibility, time above acquisition/decoding thresholds and successful DATA delivery per attempted window, with uncertainty across dates, geometries and fades. Stratify bulk-frequency acquisition, timing and frequency track errors, false and missed locks, packet errors and outage length. Evaluate no Helper, actual Helper and an oracle common-state reference at comparable energy and scheduling. Measure \(P(\mathrm{DATA\ success}\mid\mathrm{Helper\ lock})\), false authorization of DATA operations, and when the Helper is useful for timing or common oscillator tracking despite deep DATA fades. A model that matches a marginal fade histogram but changes these outcomes can change the receiver decision. Retain the least detailed model that preserves measured channel structure and those decisions within the task’s tolerance.

The search found no open simultaneous three-carrier 5.8 GHz dataset at the intended spacings, and the case does not supply a waveform, station pair, power, receiver thresholds, schedule or weather distribution. Consequently no numerical availability forecast, exact coherence bandwidth, band-specific channel-PDF choice or decision-performance bound follows. The 5.760 GHz table is an engineering estimate informed by other-band observations, the JPL noise and 2020 X-band links have different frequencies and beams, and the recent 2025/2026 publications cited above were assessed using indexed text only. No simulation or product qualification was performed.
