# Appraising an HF weak-signal modem

Keep the two-ray simulator for controlled diagnostics. Add a reproducible Watterson/ITU-R reference case, then use a joint DATA–Helper sweep and a small set of nonstationary and distributed-scattering cases to decide where higher fidelity changes receiver choices. Evaluate path availability and interference separately from distortion on an available skywave link. The illustrative simulator description is the only evidence about the modem here.

## What 7 ms and 30 Hz establish

[ITU-R F.1487-0, Annex 3 §§1–4](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I!!PDF-E.pdf) specifies two independently fading paths, equal mean attenuation, equal Doppler spreads and zero frequency shifts for its representative quantitative tests. The delay is the separation between the two fixed modes. The Doppler number is the **2σ width of each path's Gaussian Doppler spectrum** in the benchmark, with σ the standard deviation. It is neither a maximum frequency excursion nor the separation of two spectral centroids. A Gaussian PSD has tails. The illustrative 7 ms support and 30 Hz differential Doppler match the numerals of the high-latitude disturbed row. Equivalence would require verifying actual path separation, tap spectra, centroids, powers and independence in the reference configuration. A 30 Hz separation of two otherwise steady rays instead produces beating with a period of about 33 ms where their received contributions interfere.

| F.1487 representative condition | Differential delay, ms | Per-path 2σ spread, Hz |
|---|---:|---:|
| Low-latitude quiet / moderate / disturbed | 0.5 / 2 / 6 | 0.5 / 1.5 / 10 |
| Mid-latitude quiet / moderate / disturbed | 0.5 / 1 / 2 | 0.1 / 0.5 / 1 |
| Mid-latitude disturbed near-vertical incidence | 7 | 1 |
| High-latitude quiet / moderate / disturbed | 1 / 3 / 7 | 0.5 / 10 / 30 |

Annex 3 §5 gives illustrative **marginal** exceedances of around 5% for 4 ms and for 3 Hz at low latitudes, and for 5 ms and for 25 Hz at high latitudes. These are separate marginal statements in a benchmark recommendation. They cannot assign event weights, simultaneous exceedance probabilities or modem availability to the illustrative presets. Annex 2 explores separate delay, Doppler and S/N axes, reaching 20 ms and 40 Hz in its illustrative sweep. Annex 3 §6 recommends the larger of 3,000 divided by Doppler spread and 100 divided by target BER times bit rate for its quantitative tests. That is context for how poorly a short run samples slow fades and rare errors. It is not a duration rule derived for this modem's packet and acquisition metrics. [F.1487-0, Annexes 2–3](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I!!PDF-E.pdf)

The corner does not subsume intermediate conditions. Equal-power rays give periodic frequency notches whose positions move with relative phase and whose spacing is the reciprocal of delay separation. Changing delay can move a notch onto DATA or Helper from an earlier position between them. Smaller spread can also sustain a bad notch for a greater part of a frame. A shifting narrow ray can fall outside a tracking loop while a centered spread of similar numeric value is still partly inside it. Measure these effects with actual carrier spacing, symbol period, coherent integration time, packet duration and tracking bandwidth. Those values were not supplied.

## Model families and what they buy

1. **Watterson fixed taps, locally stationary.** Use a sum of delayed samples multiplied by independent, zero-mean proper complex Gaussian tap gains with Rayleigh envelopes. The general Watterson form allows two Gaussian spectral components per tap with distinct powers, centroid frequencies and widths, representing magnetoionic components. A single centered Gaussian per tap suffices for Annex 3. Generate colored complex gains to the specified PSD rather than resampling independent gain at each audio sample. This reference isolates time selectivity, frequency selectivity and noise reproducibly. It accommodates multiple fixed delays and differing spectral components, with stationarity and delay-uncorrelated scattering over the test window. An instantaneous band up to about 12 kHz is the recommendation's scope, with applicable bandwidth dependent on unresolved *within-mode* dispersion. Classic validation had limited measured coverage. A waveform can already be frequency selective across a few carriers with fixed delay taps even where a narrowband Watterson representation of each mode is adequate. [Watterson's 1969 ITS simulator memo, agency abstract](https://its.ntia.gov/publications/details?pub=3106), [F.1487-0 Annex 1 Eq. (2)](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I!!PDF-E.pdf), [Vogler and Hoffmeyer, NTIA TR-90-255, §1](https://its.ntia.gov/publications/download/90-255_ocr.pdf), [DRDC D122, §3.1](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2019/rddc-drdc/D68-2-122-2018-eng.pdf)

2. **A time-varying tapped delay line with local stationary intervals.** Reuse the two-ray path as a special case. Add unequal powers, extra discrete modes, optional dominant specular components, fractional delays with smooth trajectories, and locally changing diffuse bandwidth and Doppler centers. For state transitions, vary mean power, mode availability, delay and spread on explicit time scales, with phase continuity where a path persists. Test loss and regain as well as mode replacement. Keep tap correlation optional and calibrated. A scalar correlation control alone cannot set the cross-spectral behavior of taps with different PSDs. This is a practical first extension because it can expose mode ambiguity, synchronization failures and recovery time without the input needs of ray tracing. A state schedule may be synthetic for diagnosis. Realistic dwell and transition distributions need time-contiguous measurements. The variation and window limitations reported in DAMSON and Alaska motivate this option. [DRDC D122, §§2.2, 3.2](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2019/rddc-drdc/D68-2-122-2018-eng.pdf), [2026 Alaskan HF beacon study, §§4–5 and Fig. 10](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2026.1848834/full)

3. **Distributed delay–Doppler scattering, e.g. ITS/Vogler–Hoffmeyer.** Allow a mode to occupy a cluster of delays, potentially with centroid and Doppler spectrum changing across that cluster, plus group-delay dispersion across frequency when relevant. Use a sparse cluster or localized measured delay–Doppler power distribution before adopting a full model. ITS Part II describes stochastic dispersion, diffuse scattering and delay-dependent shift, and compares examples with disturbed equatorial and trans-auroral measurements. Figure 9c shows that different assumed correlation forms change local spectral shape. The report calls for more measurements to calibrate distributions, so its examples establish useful model structure without establishing case probabilities. A discriminator is whether distributed scattering changes a receiver ranking or measured residual at matched gross delay and Doppler metrics. [NTIA TR-90-255, §§3.6, 4 and Figs. 9–10](https://its.ntia.gov/publications/download/90-255_ocr.pdf)

4. **Path prediction, physics-based synthesis and measured replay.** Use P.533 for route/frequency/time conditioning, expected propagation support and link budget, including MUF/LUF and median S/N. Use a ray-tracing-plus-scattering model such as HiCIRF when specific geometry, antenna pattern, so-called off-great-circle arrivals, multihop paths or operating frequency need to explain the results. Its one-way example exhibits mode-dependent spreading, and the modeled antenna changes which parts of the scattered energy enter the receiver. A climatological mode prediction needs stochastic/observational inputs to generate fast fades. A measured complex CIR trace or replayable synchronized I/Q yields realistic transitions within the observed coverage. Channel-only replay from estimated CIRs can compare new waveforms if its sounding bandwidth and cadence suffice. Direct raw waveform replay tests receiver changes on those recorded transmissions. Hold out paths, dates or events for a transfer check. [ITU-R P.533-14, Parts 2–3 and Attachment 1](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.533-14-201908-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf), [HiCIRF, §§3–4](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2011RS004928), [DRDC D122, §3](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2019/rddc-drdc/D68-2-122-2018-eng.pdf)

## Adverse scenarios with different receiver consequences

- **Auroral, subauroral and transauroral links.** Include elevated diffuse width, offset modes, several resolvable echoes, mode changes and long loss/re-entry. Angling et al.'s original 1998 abstract reports values exceeded 5% of the time across four sampled paths ranging from 2 to 55 Hz Doppler spread and 1 to 11 ms multipath spread. DRDC summarizes DAMSON experience as spreads up to at least 12.5 ms, spreads above 50 Hz and shifts up to 30 Hz. Those are path-specific findings with sounding and metric conventions. They justify testing outside the illustrative bounds without implying a joint 12.5 ms/55 Hz percentile case. Test wide/short-delay, long/narrow-Doppler and joint stress with clearly documented provenance. [Angling et al., publisher abstract](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1029/97RS02206), [DRDC D122, §2.2](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2019/rddc-drdc/D68-2-122-2018-eng.pdf)
- **Equatorial spread-F/scattering and NVIS.** For disturbed low-latitude paths, add a diffuse mode with delay and Doppler extent, possibly alongside one or more relatively narrow arrivals. P.533 provides an equatorial-scattering planning attachment and ITS compares against a disturbed equatorial example. Separately include long-delay, slow-fading NVIS and, if relevant to the route, a relatively coherent ground-wave component. Java NVIS sounding published in 2024 recorded roughly 0–1.3 ms RMS delay spread in its conclusion and about 0.7–1.1 Hz Doppler **shift** during one quiet campaign. The abstract gives 0.2 ms as the low delay figure. The conclusion separately states the smallest observable delay was 0.2 ms. Neither reading estimates disturbed NVIS or equates RMS delay with a two-ray separation. [ITU-R P.533-14 Attachment 1](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.533-14-201908-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf), [NTIA TR-90-255](https://its.ntia.gov/publications/download/90-255_ocr.pdf), [Dear et al., Fig. 12 and conclusion](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023RS007877), [F.1487-0 Annex 3 §3.4](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I!!PDF-E.pdf)
- **Drift, changing propagation support and intermittent interference.** The 2026 Fort Collins–Newark 10 MHz study finds day/night differences in Doppler residual distributions, including a daytime Cauchy fit. That narrowband residual statistic is evidence for stratifying shift trajectories and testing transient tracking errors. It is not a tap Doppler PSD or a transferable CFO generator. The 2026 Alaska case saw intervals with frequency spreading without detectable range spreading and different spreading between paths and carriers. The authors report resolution and selection limits and acknowledge that secular changes during the one-minute analysis could mimic spreading. Estimate centroid trajectories and within-window widths at several window lengths. Include common shift with steps, ramps or drift, path-selective fading, joint propagation loss near a viability boundary and reacquisition. Retain AWGN for calibration. Add measured or defined burst noise and narrowband interferers with independent power, duty, bandwidth and placement controls. Lightning/background noise, receiver gain effects and interference are separately measurable from the wanted-signal CIR. [Fernandes et al., 2026, abstract and §1](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2026.1713968/full), [Alaskan beacon study, Fig. 10 and §§4–5](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2026.1848834/full), [ITU-R P.372-18 scope](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.372-18-202609-I%21%21SUM-HTM-E.htm)

## Receiver decisions the appraisal should resolve

Pass the composite through one time-varying channel before receiver channelization. A useful narrow-slice envelope model is a common rotation exp(jφ(t)) times the sum of delayed copies weighted by complex gains, plus receiver-side noise and interferers. A path-specific centroid belongs in the phase of its gain. Diffuse spread describes the residual spectrum around that centroid. Combine a physical delay trajectory with a consistent phase/Doppler convention so the same physical motion does not enter twice. Correct for instrument oscillator offset and drift in sounding. A common correction from Helper can remove a correlated shift within its tracking bandwidth. It cannot by itself undo differential modes, diffuse spreading, differential delay or a DATA notch. The dominant apparent centroid can differ across frequencies when the paths combine differently. Compare common-only, independent and confidence-weighted Helper assistance, including operation during Helper outages.

For fixed delays and independent stationary gains, the normalized complex frequency correlation is the Fourier transform of the normalized delay-power profile, with the sign set by conjugation convention. Two equal-power rays separated by τ give magnitude |cos(πΔfτ)|, with the first zero at Δf = 1/(2τ), about 71 Hz for 7 ms. This is a model calculation, not an assumed modem spacing or measured coherence bandwidth. The correlation can revive with larger spacing in this special two-ray case. Measure the actual joint gains, phase/CFO estimate error, conditional DATA outage given a good Helper, and joint deep fades at each real separation. Use one shared realization across the components. Independently fading whole receive bands and assigning them identical scalar fades are useful comparison bounds only when their assumptions are explicit.

1. **Check the generator before the modem results.** Use one-ray delay/tone tests and two-ray notch checks. Recover phase/centroid, tap powers, Rayleigh envelope, fading autocorrelation/PSD, delay profiles, cross-frequency correlation and intended transition timing from synthetic probes. Report delay support, RMS delay and any power threshold separately. Distinguish centroid difference, per-mode 2σ and measured FWHM or percentile width. Under finite windows, validate aliasing limits, resolution, filter/interpolation effects and false broadening from drift. The DRDC sounder gives practical relations between delay resolution, repetition interval, unambiguous Doppler range and integration time. [DRDC D122, §§3.1–3.2](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2019/rddc-drdc/D68-2-122-2018-eng.pdf)
2. **Map the operating surface.** Use AWGN and representative rows, then independently vary delay, centered spread, differential centroid, common offset, drift rate, mode power/correlation and outages. Pair spreads with several S/N values near the decode cliff. Use matched realizations between receiver variants. At actual carrier spacing, vary phase and delay to put notches on each band and in between. Follow those with acquisition starting before, inside and after loss, and dispersed-mode comparisons at matched mean S/N. Preserve both baseline and variant sensitivity to interference on DATA or Helper alone. Compare achievable acquisition and decoding, packet error and goodput, false lock, lock survival, time to reacquire, tail latency and run-to-run variation. Record S/N reference bandwidth, pre-fade power conventions, seed, time duration and uncertainty across independent seeds or sessions. Include failed or censored reacquisitions.
3. **Spend higher fidelity where it changes a decision.** Test whether common-only tracking is enough on matched composite traces. If carrier-selective errors dominate, examine separate or joint per-carrier channel estimates, equalization or combining with quality weights. If failures cluster at state changes and Helper dropout, prioritize fallback, state reset and reacquisition before adding more diffuse taps. Sound target geometries and frequencies using calibrated shared-clock or frequency-referenced I/Q over the occupied band. Record quiet-to-disturbed transitions, no-signal periods, concurrent noise and contextual metadata. Build time-local scattering and signed centroid estimates with resolution/error bounds, and reserve independent capture episodes for validation. Expand from sparse synthetic clusters to ITS or physics-based simulations only where simpler models fail to reproduce observable impairments or receiver ordering.
