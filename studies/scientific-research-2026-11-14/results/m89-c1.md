# EME continuation for m89

The joint lunar scattering model remains the starting point. Three additions affect how it should be appraised at 5.8 GHz. Atmospheric loss and receive noise can move together, the ionosphere has different consequences for envelope timing and carrier phase, and documented operation near the target band shows that local interference can determine which receiver mode works.

## Couple atmospheric loss to receive noise

ITU-R P.618-14, Annex 1 §§2–3, supplies Earth–space methods for gases, precipitation, clouds and clear-air effects within the individual methods’ validity ranges. The full recommendation PDF was accessible. Section 3, equation 69, adds thermal emission from an attenuating atmosphere. Its received sky expression uses a 2.7 K background. For the lunar rays, use the same transfer form with the Moon’s brightness as the background and integrate over the receive pattern. Nearby blank sky should use its own background. A warm absorber reduces the signal and adds thermal emission. Scintillation enters the propagation budget separately, as equation 69 excludes it from the attenuation used for thermal emission. Apply the atmospheric loss to each leg using its own elevation and conditions. Model correlations from shared weather and geometry when justified, especially for a self-echo, where independently drawing two fades can distort the tails. P.618 supplies per-leg models, with no joint two-leg EME availability result. [ITU-R P.618-14, Annex 1](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.618-14-202308-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf)

For scale, a 0.2 dB increase of receive-path absorption with a 275 K mean radiating atmosphere adds about 12.4 K of emitted brightness before accounting for the corresponding attenuation of the background. The net added brightness on that ray is about 12.3 K against 2.7 K of sky and 2.7 K against an illustrative 215 K lunar patch. The aperture-weighted change depends on beam filling. These are calculations at assumed attenuation and temperatures. A small path-loss term therefore merits a receive-noise sensitivity check for a cold front end. For comparable weather, a measured elevation sweep can distinguish extra ground spillover or site multipath from the smooth atmospheric contribution. Obtain same-elevation on/off-Moon power and a calibrated reference such as a load or noise source at the same gain and reference plane. Köppen’s accessible engineering tool documents same-elevation references and consistency checks across Sun, Moon and ground measurements under an assumed beam and uniform disk. It gives a method to adapt, with the target system’s brightness, pattern and spillover still to be measured. [ITU-R P.618-14, equations 69–70](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.618-14-202308-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf) · [Köppen, measurement and interpretation notes](https://portia.astrophysik.uni-kiel.de/~koeppen/JS/SunMoonGround.html)

## Separate timing, phase and polarization

ITU-R P.531-16, Annex 1 §§4.3–4.6, gives Faraday rotation, plasma delay and phase, dispersion and TEC-rate terms. Relevant PDF text was accessible. At 5.8 GHz, equation 6 gives about **4.0 ns** envelope delay for an illustrative summed slant TEC of $10^{18}$ electrons per square metre over both legs. Near the carrier, the corresponding phase advances by about **23 cycles** over the vacuum result. Thus the dispersion can be negligible for a symbol timing budget while TEC changes still contribute to residual carrier phase. A vacuum fractional Doppler estimate transfers by multiplying by the DATA-to-Helper frequency ratio. The first-order ionospheric phase term scales with inverse frequency and requires separate treatment if its residual matters. At the example TEC and a 1 MHz separation, scaling an unwrapped Helper phase as if the entire plasma advance were geometric would leave about **2.9 degrees** of error in magnitude from that term. The example uses nearby frequencies, the same TEC on both carriers and a first-order approximation. Actual spacings, TEC variation and phase tolerance determine its relevance. Use receiver tolerances and actual separation to decide whether plasma can stay a stress parameter or needs an explicit phase process. [ITU-R P.531-16, equation 6 and surrounding text](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.531-16-202509-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf)

For linear feeds, calculate the two rotation operators in their respective propagation bases before applying the lunar polarization transfer. P.618-14 §4 explicitly warns that the up- and down-leg rotations viewed from the Earth station can have the same sense, undermining blanket cancellation in a self-echo. For bistatic EME, use the two station directions and local magnetic conditions. The current recommendation’s presence of scintillation, refraction and ionospheric Doppler as separate mechanisms gives reasons to test adverse geometry and disturbed conditions when supported by station data. Generic satellite scintillation rates do not establish their significance for this EME channel. [ITU-R P.618-14, §4](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.618-14-202308-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf) · [ITU-R P.531-16, scope and §§4.3–4.6](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.531-16-202509-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf)

## Add an interference and receiver-control discriminator

In the March 2026 *432 and Above* newsletter, the OK1KIR operators attribute unusable 6 cm CW operation during a contest to local Wi-Fi interference and report completing a Q65-60D contact in that session. OH3LWP independently reports completed 5760 MHz contacts and stations heard and called with no completed contact. These first-person records are accessible in the March 2026 article under the respective callsigns. They establish feasible near-band operation and one interference-limited scenario, with unknown calibration, interference duty cycle and control conditions. The receiver’s frame error curve still needs calibrated input conditions and outcomes for individual transmission attempts. The same OK1KIR entry flags reference-bandwidth ambiguity in a separate 3 cm contact. [March 2026 operator logs, OH3LWP and OK1KIR](https://eme.radio/432-and-above-newsletter/432-and-above-2026-03)

The WSJT-X 2.7.0 guide’s indexed EME text lists Q65-60D as a 5.7 GHz default and says that other submodes may fare better depending on spreading and SNR. The PDF lookup for “Moon Doppler Width” found no match, so this evidence is limited to the indexed EME excerpt. The software guidance identifies spread-dependent choices in a real receiver. Calibrate thresholds against the proposed DATA and Helper receiver. Compare an ephemeris-only tracking control with Helper tracking and oracle timing/frequency under the same fades and calibrated input conditions. Inject measured noise-only and interference recordings at the proposed receiver input when available. For each carrier vary the occupancy of local signals or blockers separately from the lunar channel. Distinguish per-carrier interference and common front-end compression or gain changes, which can make multiple carriers fail simultaneously. Replay with the echo absent to measure wrong-lock rates, and compare error bursts, outage durations and recovery with and without interference at matched thermal noise. Bench sensitivity, report reference bandwidth and any processing or normalization used in a field comparison. ## Remaining evidence and stopping point

The first appraisal still needs actual carrier spacings and occupied bandwidths, shared oscillator and receiver timing assumptions, two antenna patterns and polarization calibrations, and simultaneous complex lunar and noise-only traces. Receiver noise versus elevation and local interference can be measured independently of sounding fit. Site-specific environmental availability and conditional DATA/Helper error rates remain unresolved. The follow-up found a concrete near-band reason to include local interference and first-party models to bound atmospheric and plasma terms. The operation budget was exhausted. No simulator, receiver, hardware or field capture was tested.
