# Scientific appraisal of a passive 5.8 GHz Earth–Moon–Earth channel

## Model choice

Use a geometry-driven mean link budget and a **shared, time-varying complex delay channel** for all three carriers. Start with a small statistical model of the near-specular return and diffuse tail, with Doppler conditioned on the two station locations, epoch and beam footprints. Keep the single reflector as a deterministic control and independent per-carrier fades as a sensitivity case. Promote a weighted lunar-surface model if the simpler model misses the joint DATA/Helper behavior, delay–Doppler structure, or availability in a geometry that matters. The principal missing inputs are the three carrier spacings and occupied bandwidths, the receiver’s acquisition and tracking intervals, the stations and their beams, and the receiver’s use of Helper.

## Geometry, signal level and receiver noise

For each transmitting station, scattering point and receiving station, compute the two distances at their respective transmit, scatter and receive times. The path delay is the summed range divided by the speed of light, and to first order the received frequency shift at carrier frequency $f_i$ is $-f_i\,d\tau/dt$ for increasing delay $\tau$. This separates bulk offset and acceleration from differential delays and Dopplers across the illuminated disk. Rotation of the observing Earth, lunar orbital motion and apparent libration all enter even if the terrain in the lunar frame is fixed. Set link opportunity from simultaneous topocentric visibility, usable elevation and both antenna patterns. Track range, elevation, beam pointing and polarization at both sites. JPL tables supply a reference for topocentric range, range rate and light time. Check the two-leg calculation independently, including sign, time reference and site coordinates. [JPL Horizons manual, Observer Table](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/manual.html) · [Park et al., DE440/DE441, lunar orientation and ranging data](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/Park.2021.AJ.DE440.pdf)

At 5.8 GHz the wavelength is **5.17 cm**. At an illustrative range of 384,400 km per leg the bulk delay is **2.56 s**, and a spherical Moon of radius 1,737.4 km gives a **front-to-limb excess delay of about 11.59 ms** in the far-field monostatic limit. That excess bounds the geometric delay support. RMS spread and any guard interval depend on the measured power profile and receiver. Compute the power profile for the actual footprint. A few strong short-delay components can coexist with a weak extended tail, and near-zero libration Doppler spread still permits frequency selectivity. These scales are derived here and are consistent with the support discussed in the geometry and mapping sources.

The free-space bistatic radar equation gives an illustrative budget.

$$
P_{r,i}=\frac{P_{t,i}G_{t,i}G_{r,i}\lambda_i^2\sigma_{i,\mathrm{eff}}}{(4\pi)^3 R_t^2R_r^2}\,L_i,
$$

where $L_i\le 1$ represents remaining multiplicative transmission losses and $\sigma_{i,\mathrm{eff}}$ includes the weighted illuminated area and selected polarization. The antenna gains in this form set the reference and the effective cross section incorporates the normalized beam patterns. For a whole-disk illustrative albedo of 0.07 and otherwise ideal conditions, $\sigma=0.07\pi R_{\rm Moon}^2$ gives **283.9 dB loss** at the reference distance before antenna gains. This is a scale check using an assumed albedo. A narrow or off-centre beam needs the product of the two patterns integrated over the mutually visible scatterers. Apply projected polarization power and cross section once, and normalize the stochastic channel to the intended mean received power without normalizing away fluctuations. [Bistatic radar paper, equations 2–4 and §§2–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7180880/)

Compute $T_{\rm sys}$ at a stated receive reference plane with feed losses, receiver noise, ground spillover, atmosphere, sky and lunar thermal brightness. Convolve brightness with the receive pattern and let the Moon replace the background over its disk. The rise in antenna temperature depends on beam filling and the blocked patch of sky. Treat sunlight near the beam, changing elevation, local interference and pointing error as station or scenario inputs where relevant. Use $N_0=kT_{\rm sys}$, modem $C/N_0=P_r/N_0$, and $N=kT_{\rm sys}B_{\rm ENBW}$ for a receiver bandwidth with equivalent noise bandwidth $B_{\rm ENBW}$. In a power-normalized complex baseband with flat two-sided baseband density $N_0$, sampling the full Nyquist band at $F_s$ gives total I plus Q noise variance $N_0F_s$ before filtering. Thermal noise in disjoint ideal channels is independent under this model. Shared gain changes, interference and oscillator errors can couple receiver decisions. Use calibrated on/off-Moon and system temperature measurements to anchor the noise. Lindgren’s 2015 thesis, abstract and chapters 3–6 and 10–13, is a nearby-method example at 1296 MHz. Its abstract reports an estimated 5.4 dB SNR in 100 Hz with a reported two-standard-deviation interval of −1.6/+1.2 dB about the estimate and agreement with time-averaged measurements for that station. Publisher-indexed Chang’e microwave radiometry text reports adjacent 3 and 7.8 GHz brightness measurements and flags calibration issues. A disk-averaged 5.8 GHz antenna temperature for the intended dish still needs calibration. [Lindgren thesis PDF](https://publications.lib.chalmers.se/records/fulltext/217884/217884.pdf) · [Chang’e microwave radiometry, instrument and calibration sections](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2021JE007039)

Carry a fixed convention for each antenna polarization and its local sky basis. For a smooth reflection, circular echo power appears in the **opposite** sense under the radar transmit/receive convention. Rough terrain can also return substantial same-sense power, with a delay–Doppler distribution that can differ from the stronger sense. For linear feeds include deterministic pointing-dependent basis rotation and ionospheric Faraday rotation on both legs with the appropriate signs and $f^{-2}$ dependence. At 5.8 GHz prioritize geometry, antenna isolation and lunar depolarization, then bound Faraday effects for linear feeds or adverse TEC/elevation. A pair of complex polarization taps or a 2×2 scattering response lets the appraisal test selection or combining. A polarization mismatch scalar can serve a fixed-polarization budget when calibrated. [Bistatic measurements, §3 and figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7180880/) · [Polarimetric observations and modeling](https://doi.org/10.1029/2010JE003649)

## Why the carriers must share the channel

For each scatterer, all carriers share its excess delay $\delta\tau_p(t)$ and path-rate history, but acquire different phases $-2\pi f_i\delta\tau_p$. Apply a common geometry-derived bulk delay and the carrier-specific bulk phase from that delay. Within each carrier, apply the remaining paths as fractional envelope delays with complex weights. A carrier can be narrow enough for flat fading and still have substantially different response from a separated carrier. The relevant scales are each occupied bandwidth against the delay profile, carrier **separation** against the frequency correlation, and coherent integration time against the power-weighted Doppler spectrum. Reference phase to a common epoch and avoid counting Doppler again when the time-varying propagation phase already supplies it. At GHz carrier frequencies, evaluate range differences and reduce phase before conversion to the baseband sample precision.

For a locally stationary interval and uncorrelated diffuse scatterers, a common normalized diffuse delay–Doppler power density $S(\tau,\nu)$ gives the diffuse correlation defined by $E[H(t+\Delta t,f+\Delta f)H^*(t,f)]$.

$$
R(\Delta t,\Delta f)=\iint S(\tau,\nu)e^{j2\pi(\nu\Delta t-\tau\Delta f)}\,d\tau\,d\nu.
$$

This approximation assumes a modest fractional RF separation so that diffuse weights and differential Doppler are locally comparable. Use the actual $f_i$ and path rates in a shared-path realization. At zero time lag the normalized frequency correlation is the Fourier transform of the diffuse delay-power profile. For illustration only, an exponential tail with time constant 0.5 ms has correlation magnitude about 0.95 at 100 Hz separation and 0.30 at 1 kHz. A central component can sustain additional common fading or a common mean even where the diffuse part decorrelates. Wide carrier spacing can justify independence of the residual complex fading under a joint Gaussian model after validation. Bulk Doppler, geometry, system state and visibility remain shared. [Hagfors, primary theoretical abstract](https://doi.org/10.1029/JZ066i003p00777)

A simulator may fit both the power-delay profile and CW spectrum while assigning their power to the wrong delay–Doppler pairs. Early echoes grouped near a strong Doppler component and late echoes spread over a broader range can change cross-carrier phase aging relative to a model with the same separate marginals. Assess joint covariance, delay bias and conditional SNR if Helper is used for channel prediction, timing transfer or coherent integration. A Helper that tracks common Doppler or a shared oscillator may retain value even during DATA fades. Transfer fractional Doppler through the frequency ratio, then apply the oscillator model appropriate to the hardware. Transferring complex fade, phase or SNR requires evidence about the cross-carrier relationship.

## Alternatives and checks before relying on availability

1. **Point reflector.** Use the shared ephemeris, power budget and receiver noise to check acquisition windows, Doppler correction, sample-time alignment and the no-spread limit. Independent gains can stress-test assumed diversity and outage sensitivity. A physical single path produces one delay and fully related complex responses after the frequency-dependent phase and gain are accounted for. It can miss both a diffuse spectral tail and conditional deep fades when Helper is strong.
2. **Shared stochastic taps or delay–Doppler cells.** This is the first appraisal model. Calibrate a strong early component and diffuse delayed energy with geometry-dependent Doppler. Use persistent phases or appropriately correlated processes over a pass. Treat a stable coherent component and a strong but fluctuating early component as separate sensitivity choices until the data distinguish them. Project jointly onto all three carriers. Compare measured spectra, power-delay profiles and joint tone statistics against this model before adding parameters. A measured multicarrier complex replay is also a useful baseline when representative recordings exist. It loses coverage as geometries, carrier placements or station behavior depart from those recordings.
3. **Weighted sphere or terrain facets.** Generate joint delays and Dopplers from each patch’s two-leg ranges and derivatives and weight by beam overlap, area, incidence and polarized scattering law. Start with a sphere and a center-enhanced plus diffuse law, with complex scatterer weights fixed within the local geometry evolution. Add terrain or measured spatial reflectivity when narrow beams, pointing changes or repeatable regional effects warrant it. Changing random phases at every update destroys coherent integration. Check facet/cell convergence in delay and Doppler statistics and receiver metrics. Fine topography alone leaves centimetric roughness phases uncalibrated. A facet model can supply the statistical model’s joint density or input ensembles. Both representations can be equivalent for the receiver if they preserve the relevant joint statistics.

Use matched waveforms, station conditions, powers and noise realizations across models. First check one noiseless path, the static delay-spread case and the single-tone CW spread limit, then check time lag and carrier separation jointly. Expect frequency selectivity without temporal fading for static extended scatterers, and temporal fading while neighboring carriers remain coherent at sufficiently small separation. The decisive measurement is a calibrated near-band sounding with simultaneous complex tones across the actual spacings, including weak and strong libration, plus an occasional wideband sounding or coded pulse to resolve the delay tail. Record TX/RX polarization, beam pointing, frequency reference and noise-only intervals. Estimate $C/N_0$, power-delay quantiles, Doppler-power widths, complex covariance with the bulk phase removed, and conditional fade/run-length distributions. Account for noise bias in covariance estimates. Fit and validate on different passes or geometries when data permit. Near-band literature supplies plausibility bounds while acquiring that evidence.

Replay the same events through the actual receiver decisions when available. Report visibility duration, acquisition and reacquisition, false alarms with signal absent, residual timing and frequency, DATA frame success/error, and joint outcomes including **Helper valid / DATA fails** and **Helper lost / DATA would succeed**. Compare receiver behavior with Helper ignored, used for common tracking, and used for fade/phase transfer if that behavior is planned. Span weak and strong libration, range extremes, beam and polarization mismatch, and short and long coherent integration. Report both availability conditioned on mutual visibility and the fraction of scheduled time that delivers the requested receiver outcome. The immediate discriminator is whether matched stochastic taps and facets give the same decision and outage distributions within the uncertainty relevant to the proposed use. Promote the model when differences persist in a condition represented by field evidence. Unknown operating parameters support conditional curves and uncertainty bounds, without an absolute availability estimate.

## Coverage and limits

Search performed 14 November 2026 under the 24-operation ceiling. It covered passive terrestrial lunar echoes near 5.8 GHz, neighboring-band measurements, historical delay–Doppler and two-tone theory, recent broadband and polarimetric research, ephemerides and lunar thermal radiometry. Active spacecraft/relay channels, optical retroreflectors and far-band radiometry were screened for relevance to the RF passive path. The recent Li and Campbell methods could not be independently inspected in full. Within this search I found no calibrated 5.8 GHz joint dataset matching the waveform and station conditions, and no station parameters were supplied. The width, polarization and albedo examples are separate measurements or estimates as identified above. No simulator, receiver, field data analysis or product test was executed.
