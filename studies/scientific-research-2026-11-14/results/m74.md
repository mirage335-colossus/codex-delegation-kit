# HF channel appraisal for a three-carrier weak-signal modem

Use the illustrative two-ray simulator for reproducible engineering stresses. Add a stationary Gaussian-scatter reference, a mode-transition tier, and matched capture or channel-response replay. Build the reference around the full DATA–Helper frequency span and preserve a common realization across the composite waveform. The existing 7 ms/30 Hz setting is one severe case. It can leave acquisition, frequency selectivity, persistent fades, and recovery gaps at intermediate settings. This report concerns the supplied case description. No implementation inspection, simulator runs, or qualification was performed.

## Reference conditions and the meaning of “30 Hz”

ITU-R F.1487 Annex 1 represents propagation by fixed-delay taps with complex time-varying gains. Independent zero-mean, circular Gaussian components produce Rayleigh amplitude fading. Each component has its own mean frequency shift and Gaussian Doppler power spectrum with width convention 2σ. A mode can have two such components for unresolved magneto-ionic contributions with different spectra. Resolved differential delays can be represented by separate taps. A stable specular or ground-wave term is an option. The Recommendation validates the model for 3 kHz and gives qualified applicability up to about 12 kHz. Compare the complete occupied frequency span to that limit. [ITU-R F.1487, Annex 1 §§1–4, equations (1)–(2), and Figs. 2–3, public PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf)

Annex 3 gives the following comparative test settings. The first number is differential arrival time in ms and the second is frequency spread in Hz. These settings describe conditional reference channels. Annex 3 §5 supplies separate approximate marginal exceedances, e.g. 5 ms and 25 Hz at high latitudes, each exceeded about 5% of the time in the Recommendation's indication. Those marginals give no joint outage probability for this waveform or a specified link. [ITU-R F.1487, Annex 3 §§2–5, printed pp. 10–11](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf)

Document four distinct quantities at the generator and receiver interfaces.

- **Common shift and wander** rotate the composite by a shared phase whose derivative gives the instantaneous common frequency offset. Sweep signed static offset through the boundaries of acquisition frequency bins, ramps and bounded time variation. A single common correction can remove an ideal common rotation. The measured center of the received spectrum can also change when propagation modes exchange dominance, and oscillator error can contribute to the estimated center.
- **Differential Doppler centers** give separate phase rates by path. Two narrow lines 30 Hz apart give regular beating, with an envelope period of about 33 ms at a given frequency for equal-power stable rays. Their individual spectral widths can be zero. A common correction leaves their relative phase rate intact.
- **Doppler spread** gives an individual diffuse component energy over a frequency range. An F.1487 Gaussian test component with spread 30 Hz has σ = 15 Hz. Its normalized complex gain autocorrelation magnitude is $e^{-2\pi^2\sigma^2u^2}$ at lag $u$, so it falls to one half near 12.5 ms. This is a model calculation that defines the correlation threshold explicitly. A finite-window measured composite Doppler width includes any unresolved centroid differences, drift, and window effects.
- **Delay metrics** need separate labels for earliest-to-latest excess delay above a specified threshold, power-weighted RMS delay spread, and absolute flight time. For two taps with powers $P_1,P_2$ and separation $d$, RMS delay spread is $d\sqrt{P_1P_2}/(P_1+P_2)$. A weak delayed tap can produce long support and small RMS. The 7 ms/30 Hz labels coincide numerically with the high-latitude disturbed reference. Equivalence would require checking actual path delays, spectra, and powers. Keep the differential-center stress and the Gaussian spread case separately observable. [ITU-R F.1487, Annex 1 §§2–3 and Annex 3 §4](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf)

## Measurements and candidate model families

**Stationary tapped delay line.** Reuse the existing delay, gain, phase and summation primitives wherever they can realize the reference shapes. Start the comparison with two equal-average-power taps and one independent zero-centered Gaussian process per tap. Keep these parameters fixed while checking the tabulated spread and delay controls. Add a second centroid per mode or another tap when the measured structure or a diagnostic scenario calls for it. Independent taps are a useful reference. Nissen and Bello’s 2003 auroral wideband measurements support uncorrelated scattering for their resolved disturbed data after accounting for the composite equipment filter. Their April 1 example reports a 6.6 Hz mean shift, a 7.5 Hz Doppler width and about 0.10 ms delay width under the paper’s 2σ convention. This is direct evidence that appreciable spread and shift can occur with short delay width. The roughly 100-probe statistical subset excluded significant multimode observations. Delay and one-sided Doppler measurement windows were about 2 ms and at most 31.25 Hz. That subset cannot characterize joint tails, other links, or longer-term stationarity. Retain correlated taps as an explicit sensitivity test. Measure correlation with the sounding-filter response and resolution accounted for before selecting a default. [Nissen and Bello, *Radio Science* (2003), §§1–3, Fig. 1 and ¶¶8–11, publisher full text](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2002RS002746)

**Distributed delay–Doppler scattering.** A broadened delay cluster can contain many scatterers and show a tilted ridge, with Doppler center varying across excess delay. Vogler and Hoffmeyer’s ITS transfer-function model represents delay shape and this coupling and supports Gaussian or Lorentzian Doppler profiles. The authors associate exponential temporal autocorrelation and a Lorentzian shape with their auroral spread-F examples and allow that the profile can depend on the regime. A Lorentzian describes the within-mode Doppler power spectrum in that model. It shares a mathematical shape with a Cauchy density, which can describe a different observable such as a distribution of measured frequency residuals over time. Keep the PSD and residual distribution distinct. For an initial discriminator, discretize one sloped cluster into weighted shaped taps and compare to a Gaussian cluster at matched power, delay profile and a stated finite Doppler width convention. Use a half-power or specified in-band energy width for Lorentzian comparisons, since its ideal infinite-band second moment diverges. A general frequency-dependent ITS-like model is worth further work when the composite span or a measured within-mode structure challenges the tapped reference and changes a receiver decision. [Vogler and Hoffmeyer, NTIA TR-92-284 (1992), abstract, derivation on printed pp. 6–9 and Figs. 1–2, public OCR PDF](https://www.its.ntia.gov/publications/download/92-284.pdf)

**Locally stationary modes with nonstationary transitions.** Model a changing mixture of E/F, one-hop/multihop, and ordinary/extraordinary or off-great-circle modes where the scenario warrants them. Fade modes in and out, change relative power and Doppler centers, and enable finite deep absorption or unavailable modes with plausible ramps and dwell times. Preserve phase continuity when a mode persists. Zaalov et al. observed a near-zero-shift sporadic-E component around 5 ms absolute flight time and a delayed, split off-great-circle component around 7 ms at 6.95 MHz on a winter Uppsala–Leicester record. This is about 2 ms between groups. Their observations over two northerly paths and Stocker et al.'s polar-cap and trough measurements support dependence on geometry, time and ionospheric structure. Trough depletion can remove the direct great-circle mode while trough-wall refraction or auroral irregularities support other modes. Moving polar structures can add both delays and multiple Doppler centers. Use these as conditional mechanisms, with measured profiles or explicitly labeled engineering parameters. A single geomagnetic-index multiplier would miss the observed path dependence. [Zaalov et al., *Radio Science* (2007), §§2–4, Fig. 2 and ¶¶7–12, publisher full text](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2006RS003611) [Stocker et al., *Radio Science* (2013), §§1–2, publisher full text](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2013RS005264)

**Recent constraints from different observables.** Dear et al. report an 87 km low-latitude Java NVIS campaign with multiple resolvable components in quiet conditions and plausible E/F-layer contributions. The example on 21 December 2021 had excess peaks up to 1.882 ms, and the week’s RMS delay spread averaged about 0.463 ms. Table 2’s 25.5 ms delay and 19.6 Hz Doppler figures are sounding specifications, with the paper explicitly leaving its high-delay performance unconfirmed in the quiet field data. Quiet NVIS can therefore merit a multimode case even when its delay support is small. In a separate 10 MHz Fort Collins–Newark study, Fernandes et al. report diurnally different frequency-residual distributions, with daytime Cauchy fits and other fits more frequent before sunrise. They show a solar-flare power collapse and use PHaRLAP/IRI ray tracing to infer changing hop availability. Their frequency traces characterize link offsets and variability. They do not resolve a tap-level Doppler spectral shape, and the traced modes are modeled. Use this evidence for slow offset/wander, changed path mix and absorption scenarios. [Dear et al., *Radio Science* (2024), §§3.2–3.3, Table 2, Figs. 10–12 and associated results, publisher full text](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023RS007877) [Fernandes et al., *Frontiers in Astronomy and Space Sciences* (2026), §§3.2.1–3.2.3 and Figs. 6, 9–12, publisher-hosted PDF](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2026.1713968/pdf)

**Calibrated playback and link modeling.** Replay a calibrated complex channel impulse-response series on a known transmit waveform when available. Use received composite IQ recordings to compare receivers seeing the same realization. Preserve acquisition, fades and reacquisition in one run. Capture sounding or pilot references with known timing and frequency, monitor receiver gain and clipping, and keep interference and noise separate when their contributions are identifiable. Set the probe bandwidth and unambiguous delay window to resolve relevant multipath. Sample the channel fast enough to avoid aliasing the Doppler spectrum and use windows long enough to resolve spread yet short enough to track the changing mode mix. Record settings and data gaps. Simultaneous DATA and Helper measurements matter because sequential soundings cannot resolve fast cross-frequency fades. If the goal includes environmentally weighted channel selection, add route, season, UTC and local solar time, operating frequency relative to usable band, and activity metadata. Use ray tracing for geometry, group delay and mode availability. Stochastic residuals and calibrated measurements supply fine-scale fading, interference and receiver-relevant outage behavior. Replay is constrained by capture coverage, calibration, and the available assortment of waveforms. [ITU-R F.1487, Annex 1 §1 on simultaneous comparisons](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf) [Fernandes et al., §3.2.3](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2026.1713968/pdf)

## The Helper can share frequency error and see a different fade

In a local narrowband tapped model after factoring out common rotation, $H(t,f)=\sum_i g_i(t)e^{-j2\pi f\tau_i(t)}$, with $f$ measured relative to one baseband reference. Apply the propagation operator to the summed waveform before receiver separation, then add external noise or interferers and model the terminal response. When DATA and Helper share the propagation link, each carrier sees the same tap realization with its own delay-dependent phase factors. If the Helper has an independent transmit oscillator or a separate path, measure the shared offset and fading components rather than assuming that all its impairments are common. Under independent, zero-mean stationary taps with powers $P_i$, the normalized frequency correlation $E[H(t,f+\Delta f)H(t,f)^*]/E[|H(t,f)|^2]$ is $\sum_i P_i e^{-j2\pi\Delta f\tau_i}/\sum_i P_i$. This is a derivation for the reference model. Direct calibration is required for correlated taps, delay drift, a frequency-dependent response, or frequency separations outside that model. Keep the RF carrier phase convention consistent when delay motion is coupled to Doppler. A nondispersive delay change already implies phase evolution at the RF carrier, so a separately injected offset needs explicit bookkeeping.

For illustration, let two equal-amplitude rays add constructively at the Helper and let a DATA tone lie 142.86 Hz away. With 3 ms separation, the DATA magnitude is approximately $\lvert\cos(3\pi/7)\rvert=0.223$ of the Helper magnitude, about 13 dB down. With 7 ms the two contributions are again aligned at DATA, because the additional relative phase is a full cycle. This example fixes relative phase at the Helper in each case. The actual carrier spacing was unspecified. Repeat using the real spacing, delays, relative powers and phases to expose persistent nulls, Helper-only fades, DATA-only fades and common fades. Leave each carrier’s own occupied bandwidth in the calculation to see when a scalar per-carrier channel is insufficient.

Compare DATA-only, Helper-aided common frequency tracking, and a reliability-gated hybrid against an ideal common-frequency correction. Keep DATA complex channel estimates distinct from the Helper estimate unless measured covariance justifies transfer, including the predictable frequency-dependent phase. If ideal common correction helps but a real Helper loses track during its own fades, test weighting, holdover and reacquisition. If ideal correction leaves a large gap under delay-only cases, test per-carrier equalization, a guard where the waveform structure permits it, or frequency diversity. If diffuse Doppler at short delay causes the gap, compare shorter coherent windows, updated pilots and combining across windows at matched airtime and energy. Channel coherence, symbol time, spacing, latency and pilot overhead determine which change pays off. If outages dominate, compare interleaving across measured dwell times and explicit reacquisition behavior.

## A practical appraisal sequence

1. **Calibrate the simulator.** Use clean, AWGN, common-shift-only, delay-only and differential-center-only controls, followed by single-tap Gaussian spread. Check impulse delays and fractional-delay phase, center and PSD width, autocorrelation, complex-gain distribution, power, and between-tap and carrier covariance. Check that the low-rate gain generator and interpolation, if used, preserve the target spectrum and widths at waveform rate. Then check joint delay–Doppler profiles. Log effective SNR, reference bandwidth and gain settings. Retain existing tone/impulse and outage controls. Sweep burst duration, duty factor, tone location and blocker level separately from propagation, especially when AGC or clipping could couple a nominally local interferer into the whole front end. Hold average received power fixed for shape comparisons and vary attenuation explicitly for availability.
2. **Bracket the existing stress points.** Sample the Annex 3 settings over a useful SNR range with shared seeds across candidate receivers and multiple independent realizations. Cross delay with Doppler width as separate axes. Include long-delay/low-spread NVIS, short-delay/high-spread auroral-like scattering, widened or split Doppler spectra, unequal powers and specular/diffuse mixtures. Sweep phases and delays in the interval where an echo delay $\tau$ makes $B\tau$ across the occupied bandwidth, $\Delta f\tau$ across a carrier separation, or delay relative to symbol duration first affect reception. Slow fading can hold a codeword in a null, whereas some faster fading provides diversity after interleaving and also challenges phase tracking. Delay shifts nulls across carriers periodically. Frame synchronization and acquisition thresholds make intermediate points relevant even when the severe test already fails. F.1487 Annex 2 supplies the precedent for delay–spread performance surfaces, and Annex 3 §6 scales comparative test length with fading and the intended BER resolution. [ITU-R F.1487, Annex 2 and Annex 3 §6](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/f/R-REC-F.1487-0-200005-I%21%21PDF-E.pdf)
3. **Escalate fidelity where the receiver ranking changes.** Hold power, reference SNR, fade realization where comparable, waveform and receiver options fixed across controlled variants. Compare acquisition probability and time, missed detections and false locks per opportunity, residual frequency error, conditional packet error, successful payload delivery including outages, longest loss runs, and reacquisition after mode replacement. Report uncertainty with independent seeds or sufficiently separated blocks. Estimate measured per-band complex covariance and joint deep-fade rates, including conditioning on the Helper’s quality. Compare stationary passes to calibrated transition cases or replay with known mode changes. If model variants leave the ranking stable over the targeted conditions, a simpler simulator remains suitable for those comparisons.
