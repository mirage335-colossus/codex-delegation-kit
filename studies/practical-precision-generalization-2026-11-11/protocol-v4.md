## Three frozen observable decisions

1. **Sufficient arithmetic.** Examine executed arithmetic separately from storage.
   Use the lowest useful arithmetic; retain wider/exact portions for concrete
   output needs or supported end-to-end benefit. A plausible dominant narrower
   alternative receives a bounded investigation or concrete correctness argument.
   Extra reference digits alone do not justify it.
2. **Necessary work.** Inspect remaining dominant work and obvious reusable or
   invariant computations. Extra numerical machinery needs a concrete failure,
   range argument or bounded comparison against simpler sufficient computation.
   Product passing alone does not establish that such work is necessary.
3. **Qualified useful default.** Execute a demanding/default request with complete
   useful outputs and actual resource limits, preserve exactness and supported
   overrides, and use the result without corrective messages or per-stage tuning.
   Where performance drives the choice, use matching build/workload conditions and
   include preparation. Disclose noise and what was actually tested.

Independent source/history review applies these decisions along with frozen
public-derived product gates. A concrete correctness argument or bounded useful
alternative can justify a choice. Do not later require a particular algorithm,
minimum speed ratio, maximum conceivable optimization or every possible
micro-optimization. Repeated tiny local arithmetic after a dominant-work removal
is not automatically an unresolved throughput problem. Earlier counterexamples
and runtime diagnostics remain evidence, never credited as participant actions.

Fixture preflight must cover starter failure, feasible witness and relevant bad

and truthful supported overrides must be checked where supplied.
