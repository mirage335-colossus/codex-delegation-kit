## Method research and evidence limits

Three primary methodological sources were inspected under this review's separate allowance:

- [Cawley and Talbot, JMLR 2010](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume11/cawley10a/cawley10a.pdf) demonstrate that optimizing a noisy model-selection criterion can bias subsequent performance estimates, with effects comparable to model differences in their experiments. Their setting is primarily kernel methods/cross-validation, not these agents. The applicable methodological inference is to separate development tuning from frozen confirmation and retain the selection process; this paper does not prove present overfitting or dictate a sample size.
- [Blackwell, Barry and Cohn, 2024 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03492v1) examine repeated evaluation on two cardinal-direction benchmarks with six older models, including nondeterministic outputs under nominally fixed settings. They recommend documenting configuration and uncertainty. Their limited tasks and untested prompt variation prevent importing their suggested repeat counts or narrow intervals into long scientific-agent episodes. Here the single conditional replication is a bounded discriminator, not a calibrated reliability estimate.
- [Afli and Frank, Findings EMNLP 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.1992.pdf) distinguish performance under a fixed evaluation configuration from a broad construct claim and advocate matching distributional evidence to claim scope. It is a position/framework paper with limited QA/logic case studies; its abstract/introduction were inspected. It supports keeping component depth, task selection, reliability and deployment claims separate, not building its full framework or assuming its case studies validate this research workflow.
