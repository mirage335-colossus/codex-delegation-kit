## Distinct conditional alternatives

### A. An explicit scientific-authoring task mode within existing roles

For a research assignment, make scientific authorship the operative task mode
from the start: the actor owns a reusable account of the original scientific
problem, discovers and develops its questions, and returns that account plus
an evidence-bounded disposition. Engineering diagnosis, patch acceptance,
takeover and compact decision-advice defaults apply when that actor is assigned
those tasks, rather than defining completion of this one. Preserve all safety,
authorization, model, accounting and source-preservation instructions.
Controller no-network and supplied-context no-tools boundaries remain intact;
a task mode neither creates research tools nor bypasses an access hold.

This would require a **conditional replacement or routing of existing role
instructions**, not another research paragraph appended to the same defaults.
The generic policy must cause the ordinary owner to select the mode; a private
experimenter-written research dispatch would test delivery only. The existing
pair, independent checkpoint and protected review can remain. The sourcebook
is the primary scientific product; the short engineering handoff is derived
from it and cannot define its scope.

Prediction: if competing role framing matters, accounts will retain exploratory
questions and develop relevant findings beyond immediate plan support, with
scientific disposition tied to that account. If the mode is demonstrably
delivered and used but depth remains incomplete, role wording alone is
insufficient. Cost: instruction maintenance and potentially more research
attention, not necessarily more staff. No evidence currently requires a new
registered role. A separate role would need a deliberate reviewed inventory
change only if existing-role task routing cannot express the distinction;
renaming the actor is not the mechanism.

### B. Sustained participant-derived scientific branch authorship

Retain both independent full-problem initial accounts. After their preserved
checkpoint, the owner uses questions emerging from those accounts and literature
to assign complementary **investigation ownership**, while a lead retains the
whole problem and cross-branch dependencies. A branch author owns discovery,
explanation, opposing evidence, quantitative work where consequential, and the
scientific account through reconciliation. A narrow scout lookup remains
supporting work. The owner routes continuing work from those investigations,
not only amendments requested by a final critic.

This can reuse the two inquiry actors and existing permitted support rather
than requiring a larger team. It differs from V6 only if the delivered scope
and observed work actually sustain a branch investigation; “retain ownership”
alone already exists in V6. Avoid a fixed domain list, mandatory new questions,
fixed pass count or forced budget exhaustion. The finite envelope and real
evidence limits remain binding.

Prediction: if broad accounts plus episodic repairs are the bottleneck,
continuing authors will deepen underdeveloped explanations and follow relevant
literature beyond the first account without root scientific reminders. If they
merely produce smaller planning reviews or a longer grouped closure table, the
mechanism did not operate. Cost: integration and overlap; the matched diagnostic
does not establish complementary ownership's superiority. Do not stack this
with mode A and then claim to isolate either effect.
