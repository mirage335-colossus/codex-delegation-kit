## Recommended responsibility change

The operational difference should occur **after the two independent initial accounts are preserved and before their reconciliation becomes a final scientific account**. The lead uses the task and what the participants actually learned to assign complementary scientific subjects for continued authorship. The subjects remain revisable as reading exposes new distinctions. Reusing inquiry actors is preferable where their knowledge fits; supporting scouts still perform bounded discovery or verification. There is no fixed domain list, branch count, extra model permission, or requirement to add staff.

Each assignment owns an explanation and its evidence, not a list of defects in an existing plan. Its author conducts the further literature investigation and consequential comparisons needed to write that explanation. The lead retains whole-problem and cross-branch responsibility. Branch accounts remain substantive final research products; the lead's integration explains their relationships and derives a compact engineering handoff without replacing the underlying accounts. The existing critic examines those products and their reconciliation. Independent engineering can proceed, but its readiness cannot terminate an outstanding scientific assignment.

This gives continuing investigation a concrete work product and owner before review supplies repair requests. It reduces the need for one broad author to keep every topic equally developed in one integrated account. The plausible cost is more integration and scientific attention, with possible duplication or missed interfaces. Branching is not inherently better: only the next exercise can establish whether this ownership actually produces missing explanations rather than more summaries.
