## Proposed participant guidance

> When a development task warrants sustained scientific grounding, derive its research scope before settling the design. Start from the original outcome and actual operating conditions. Work forward through the system and backward from the decisions and observable outcomes. Identify where a change of conditions, competing mechanism, measurement limit or failed assumption would materially change a recommendation, including consequential combinations and absence cases. Treat named examples and the first branch assignments as starting points. Retain decision-relevant questions that cross branches or that the proposed design leaves out.
>
> For each material question, carry the evidence through to the decision. Inspect the original observations relevant to empirical claims, compare contrasting conditions or methods, and reconcile their definitions and limits. Where a quantity controls a choice, follow its inputs, conventions and uncertainty all the way to the engineering observable, then execute a feasible comparison that could change the choice. Record the actual result and its applicability. If the preferred source or method is unavailable, pursue an authorized, decision-equivalent comparison when feasible. Otherwise identify the specific missing input, what was tried, and which judgment remains open. Use the existing research notes and keep the handoff concise.
>
> A design can be ready for a bounded next step while its scientific grounding still has material gaps. Review those judgments separately within the existing work. For broad research, ask an independent reviewer to derive consequential cases from the original outcome and operating conditions before comparing their scope with the draft. Have the reviewer check the weakest primary evidence, decisive quantitative comparisons and adverse or coupled cases, and describe the smallest feasible missing work and what it could change. The owner or lead schedules and follows through on useful corrections with existing assignments and scope. Use readied engineering work in parallel. Close comprehensive grounding when material questions have decision-grade support or specific evidenced boundaries. If a real constraint leaves material work undone, report the science as partial alongside the design disposition and preserve its exact next action. Routine settled work still uses proportionate verification.

Deliver the main change through the existing science policy and the existing owner and independent-review cues. Keep the role cue short, for example: “For sustained scientific work, assess plan readiness and full-envelope research readiness separately. Derive coverage independently of the draft and branch assignments. Identify missing material cases or feasible evidence, then carry owner-initiated corrections through the existing review.” The same independent reviewer can give both judgments. Retain the existing research lead, review capacity, permission boundaries and model settings. Adjust goals and follow-ups within the normal evidence and time envelope. The change calls for no fixed topic count, bibliography size, extra formal review gate or new reporting system.

## Why this refinement, and what would disconfirm it

More requests or a larger cap would leave the decision rule that closed D1 untouched. Increasing headroom makes sense only when the owner finds material work and the existing ceiling demonstrably binds. A longer initial investigator prompt could improve execution inside existing scopes while missing questions outside those scopes. A coverage pass derived from the original problem can expose those questions without prescribing a domain list. Teaching the reported scientific omissions or supplying the evaluator matrix would measure recovery on a known answer set. The proposal instead changes what the owner and reviewer independently derive and what happens when the two readiness judgments differ.

Observe whether the first internal review derives independently missing cases or weak evidence, whether the owner commissions and finishes appropriate discriminators in response, and whether the locked research then clears the unchanged 89 criteria and 48 depth judgments. If the separate review identifies material gaps and repairs still remain infeasible, distinguish resource ceiling, source availability, role delivery and scientific execution before revising text again. If it declares scientific readiness and blind post-lock evaluation finds similar gaps, review scope alone was insufficient. Examine how questions were generated and what internal reviewers accepted as evidence. If no independent assessment reaches the owner, the run tests delivery rather than the proposed scientific decision rule. Positive outcomes can support feasibility of the combined delivered workflow; D1 and a successor do not isolate the causal effect of the wording.
